Loue is a diſcord and a ſtrange diuorce,
Betwixt our ſence and reſt, by whoſe power,
As mad with reaſon, we admit that force,
Which wit or labour neuer may diuorce.
It is a will that brooketh no conſent,
It would refuſe, yet neuer may repent.
— Loue's a deſire, which for to waight a time,
Doth looſe an age of yeares, and ſo doth paſſe,
As doth the ſhadow ſeuerd from his prime,
Seeming as though it were, yet neuer was.
Leauing behind, nought but repentant thoughts,
Of dayes ill ſpent, of that which profits noughts.
It's now a peace, and then a ſudden warre,
A hope conſumde before it is conceiu'd,
At hand it feares, and menaceth a farre,
And he that gaines, is moſt of all deceiu'd.
Loue whets the dulleſt wits his plagues be ſuch,
But makes the wiſe by pleaſing, dote as much.
