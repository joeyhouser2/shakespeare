A Pleasant wether Coridon, and lytte to keep the field,
This moon hath brought▪ hearst thou the birds what joyful tunes they yield,
Lo: how the lusty lambs do course, whom spring time heat doth prick
Behold again, the aged Yews, with bouncing leaps do kick,
Amonst then all, what ails thy ram, to halt so much behind,
Some sore mischance, hath him be •aln or else some grief of mind,
For wont he was, of stomach stout and courage hye to be,
And looked proud, amongst the flock, and none so stout as he.
A great mishap, and grief of mind, is him be fallen of late,
Which causeth him, against his will, to lose his old estate.
A lusty flock hath Titirus, that him Dametas gave,
Dametas he, that Martir died, whose soul the heaves have,
And in this flock, full many Yews of pleasant form do go,
with then a mighty Ram doth ronne, that works all Woers woe.
My Ram, when he the pleasant dames had viewed round about,
Chose ground of battle, with his foe and thought to fight it out.
But all to weak, (alas) he was, although his harte was good,
For when his enemy him espied, he ran with cruel mood.
And with his crooked weapon smote, him sore upon the side,
A blow of force, that stayed not there but to the legs did glide.
And almost laamd the woer quite. (such haps in love there be:)
This is the cause, of all his grief and wailing that you se.
Well Coridon let him go halt, and let us both go lie,
In yonder bush of Juniper, the Beasts shall fede hereby.
A pleasant place here is to talk: good Coridon begin,
And let us know the Towns estate, that thou remainest in.
The Towns estate? Menalcas oh thou makste my harte to grone,
For Vice hath every place possess, and Vertue thence is flown.
Pride bears her self, as Goddess chief and boasts above the Sky,
And Lowliness an abject lies, with Gentleness her bye,
Wit is not joynde with Simpleness, as she was wont to be,
But sekes the aid of Arrogance, and crafty Policy.
Nobylitie begins to fade, and Carters up do spring,
Then which, no greater plague can hap, nor more pernicious thing.
Menalcas I have known my self, within this thyrtye year,
Of Lords and Ancient Gentlemen a hundreth dwelling there,
Of whom we Shepherds had relief such Gentleness of mind,
Was placed in their noble Hearts, as none is now to find.
But Hawtynes and proud Disdain hath now the chief Estate,
For sir John Straw, and sir John Cur, will not degenerate.
And yet, they dare account then selves to be of Noble blood.
But Fish bred up, in dirty Pools, will ever stink of mud.
I promise the Menalcas here, I wolde not them envy.
If any spot of Gentleness, in them I myght espy.
For if their Natures gentle be, though birth be never so base,
Of Gentlemen (for meet it is) they ought have name and place:
But when by birth, they base are bred, and churlish harte retain,
Though place of gentlem•n they have yet churls they do remain.
A proverb old, hath ofte been hard• and now full true is tried:
An Ape, will ever be an Ape, though purple garments hide.
For seldom, will the masty course, the Hare or else the Dear:
But still, according to his kind. will hold, the hog by there.
Unfit are dunghill knights to serve the town, with Spear in field:
Nor strange it seems, (a sudain Chop) to leap from whip, to shield.
The chiefest man, in all our town, that bears the greatest sway,
Is Coridon no kin to me, a Neteherd thother day
This Coridon come from the Cart, In honour chief doth sytte,
And governs us: because he hath, a Crabbed, Clownish wit.
Now se the Churlish Cruelty, that in his harte remains.
The selye Sheape the Shepherds good, have fosterd up with Pains,
And brought away, from Stinking dales on pleasant Hills to feade:
O Cruel Clownish Coridon, O cursed Carlish Seade:
The simple Sheep, constrained he, their Pasture sweet to leave,
And to their old corrupted Grass. enforceth them to cleave.
Such Sheep, as wold not them obey but in their Pasture bide.
with (cruel flames,) they did consume and vex on every side.
And with the sheep, the Shepherds good, (O hateful Hounds of Hell,)
They did torment. and drive then out, in Places far to dwell.
There died Daphnes for his Sheep, the chiefest of them all.
And fair Alexis flamed in Fire, who never perish shall.
O Shepherds wail, for Daphnes deth, Alexis hap lament,
And curs the force of cruel hearts, that them to death have sent.
I, since I saw such sinful sights, did never like the Town,
But thought it best to take my sheep, and dwell upon the down.
Whereas I live, a pleasant life, and free from cruel hands,
I wolde not leave, the pleasant field for all the Townish Lands.
For sith that Pride, is placed thus, and Vice set up so hye:
And Cruelty doth rage so sore, and men live all awry:
Thynkste thou? the God, will long forbear, his scourge, and plague to send?
To such as him do still despise and never seek to mend?
Let them be sure he will revenge. when they think leaste upon.
But look a stormy shower doth rise, which will fall hear anon.
Menalcas best we now depart, my Cottage us shall keep,
For there is rowme for the, and me, and eke for all our sheep:
Some Chestnuts have I there in store with Cheese and pleasant whaye,
God sends me Vittayles for my need, and I sing Care away.
