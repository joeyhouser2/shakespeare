Fie Fie, I lothe to speak wylt thou my lust,
Compel me now, to do so foul an act.
Nay rather God, with Flame consume to dust.
My carry on vile, then I perform this fact
Let rather thoughts, that long, have wearied me:
Or sickness such as Fancy fond hath brought,
O gaping Hell, dryne me now down to the,
Let boiling sighs, consume me all to nought.
Ons musing as I sat, and Candle burning bye,
When all were husht I myght discern a simple selye Fly.
¶That flew before mine eyes, with free rejoicing Hart,
And here and there, with wings did play as void of pain and smart,
¶Sometime by me she sat, when she had played her fill,
And ever when she rested had about she flyttered still.
¶When I perceived her well, rejoicing in her place,
O happy Fly quoth I, and eake, O worm in happy case.
¶Which two of us is best? I that have reason? no:
But thou that reason art without and therewith void of woe.
¶ I live and so doste thou, but I live all in pain,
And Subject am to her alas, that makes my Grief her gain.
When I do hear thy name, alas my hart doth rise:
And seeks forthwith to se the salve that most contents mine eyes.
But when I se thy Face, that hath procured my pain,
Then boils my blood in every part, and beats in every vain?
Thy voice when I do hear, then colour comes and goes,
Some time as pale as Earth I look, some time as red as Rose.
If thy sweet Face do smile, then who so well as I?
If thou but cast a scornful look, then out alas I die.
But still I live in pain, my fortune willeth so,
That I should burn and thou yet know, no whytt of all my woe.
Unhappy tonge, why dydste thou not consent
When fyrst mine eyes did vewe that Princely face,
To show good will, that hart opprest than ment.
And whylst time was, to sew for present grace.
O fainting Hart, why dydst thou then conceal?
Thine inward Fyers, that flamed in every vain,
When pity and gentleness, were bent to heal.
Why dydst thou not, declare thy raging pain?
When well thou mightest have moved her gentle mind,
Why dydste thou than, keep back thy woeful plain?
Thou knews full well, redress is hard to find,
When in thy own affairs, thy corage faints.
But since she is gone, bewail thy grief no more
Since thou thy self, wart Causer of the Sore.
