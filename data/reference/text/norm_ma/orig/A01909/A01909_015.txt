The Muses joy, and well they may to se,
So well their labour come to good success,
That they sustained long ago in the,
Minerua smiles, Phebus can do no less,
But over all, they chiefly do rejoice,
That leaving things, which are but fond and vain,
Thou dyddest chuse, (O good and happy choice)
In sacred Scoles, thy lucky years to train,
By which thou hast obtaynde (O happy thing)
To learn to live, whyle other wander wide,
And by thy life, to please the immortal king,
Then which so good, nothing can be applied,
Law gyves the gain, and Physycke fyls the Purse,
Promotions hye, gyves Arts to many one,
But this is it, by which we scape the Curse,
And have the bliss of God, when we be gone.
Is this but only Scriptures for to read?
No, no. Not talk, but life gyves this in dead.
