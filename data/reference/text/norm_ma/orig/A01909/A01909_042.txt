The little Bird, the tender Marlyon,
That useth ofte upon the Lark to pray,
With great reproach, doth stain the mind of man
If all be true, that Writers of her say.
For she a Creature, maymde of Reasons part,
And framed to live according to her kind,
Doth seem to foster Reason in her Hart
And to aspire unto Devyner mind.
When Hungers rage she hath exiled quite,
And supped well as falleth for her state.
The selye Lark, doth take by force of flight,
And hies to tree, where as she lodged late,
And on the trembling Bird all night she stondes,
To keep her feet, from force of nipping cold,
The amazed Wretch, within her enemies hands,
And closed fast, within the clasping hold.
Awaiteth Death, with drowsy drooping Hart,
And all the night with fear draws on her life,
The gentle Bird, when darkness doth depart
Doth not deprive, the selye soul of life,
Nor fills with her her hungred egre brest
But wayeng well, the service she hath done.
To spill the Blood, her Nature doth detest,
And from so great a Crime, her self doth shun.
She lets her go and more with stedfast eyes.
Beholds which way she takes with mazed flight,
And in those parts that Day she never flies
Leaste on that Bird again she chance to light.
Loe, Blundston hear how kindness doth habounde,
In selye Souls where Reason is exylde,
This Bird alone sufficeth to confound,
The Brutish minds of men that are defiled,
With that great Vice, that vile and haynous Crime
Ingratitude (which some unkindness call.)
That Poison strong that springeth still with time,
Till at the length, it hath infected all.
