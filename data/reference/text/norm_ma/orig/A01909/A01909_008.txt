O Faustus, whom above the rest, of Shepherds here that keep,
Upon these holts, the number great of weighty flees sheep:
I ever have estemde: and counted eke, the chiefest Friend of all,
What great mishap, what scourge of mind or grief hath the befall?
That hath the brought in such a plight far from thy wonted guise?
What means this countenance all besprent with teres? these wretched eyes
This mourning look, this Vesture sad this wrethe of Wyllow tree,
(Unhappy man) why doste thou weep what chance hath altered the?
Tell tell, me soon, I am thy friend, Disclose to me thy grief,
Be not afraid, for friends do serve, to gyve their Friends relyefe.
The woeful cause of all my hurt, good Felix long ago,
Thou knewest full well: I need not now by words to double woe,
Since that (alas) all hope is past since grief, and I am one,
And since the Lady of my life, (my fault) I have forgone,
What woldst thou have me do (oh frend?) to Joye? in such distress?
Nay pleasures quite I banish here, and yield to Heaviness,
Let gryefes torment me evermore, let never Cares away.
Let never Fortune turn her wheal to gyve me blysfull day.
Love hath me scourged: I am content lament not thou my state.
Let spite on me take vengeance now let me be torn with hate.
Let her enjoy, her happy life, a Flower of golden hew,
That closeth when the Son doth set, and spreads with Phebus new.
Sith fron my Garland now is fallen, this famous Flower sweet:
Let Wyllows wind about my hed, (a Wrethe for Wretches meet)
Fie Faustus, let not Fancy fond, in the bear such a sway,
Expel Affections from thy mind, and drive them quyght away.
Embrace thine Ancient Liberty, let Bondage vile be fled:
Let Reason rule, thy crazed Brain, place Wit, in Follies stead.
Since she is gone, what remedy? why shuldest thou so lament?
Wilt thou destroy thy self with tears and she to pleasures bent?
Gyve ear to me, and I will show the remedies for Love
That I have learned long ago: and in my youth did prove.
Such remedies as soon shall quench the flames of Cupids Fire,
Such remedies as shall delay, the Rage of fond Desire.
For Faustus if thou follow still, the blinded God to please,
And wylt not seek, by Reasons Rule, to purchase thine own ease,
Long canst thou not thy friends enjoy but bid them all farewell.
And leave thy life, and give thy soul to deepest floods of Hell.
Leave of therefore, betimes and let Affection bear no sway,
And now at fyrst the Fire quench before it further stray,
Each thing is easily made to obey, whyle it is yong and green,
The tender twyg, that now doth bend at length refuseth clean.
The fervent Fire, that flaming fyrst, may little water drench,
When as it hath obtained time, whole Rivers can not quench:
Forsake the Town, (my Faustus deare) and dwell, upon this plain,
And time shall heal, thy festering wound and Absence banish Pain.
Above all things fly Idleness, For this doth double strength,
To Lovers flams, and makes then rage, till all be lost at length,
Here in these fields, are pleasant things to occupy thy brain,
Be hold: how spring revyves again, that winter late had slain,
Behold: the pleasant Hills adournd, with dyvers colours fair,
Geve ear to Scillas lusty songs, rejoicing in the air,
What pleasure canst thou more desire, then here is for to se:
Thy lusty yews, with many a lam, Lo: where they wait on the,
Think not upon that cursed face, that makes the thus her slave
But well regard the pleasant life, that here thou seest me have,
When I long time a go, did feale, the flames of Cupids fire,
These means Lo than I practised, to cure my fond desire.
I fyrst wayed with my self, How fond a thing it seamed,
To let my heart lie there in chains, where I was nought esteemed.
And how with flames I burnt for her, that passed nought for me,
And how, these eyes encreast my harms that fyrst her face did se,
With pensive heart full fraight with thoughts, I fled fron thence away,
And though that Love bad tourne my steps, yet wold I never stay,
But from that foul infectyve ayer, where first I took my sore,
I hied in hast, and shund the place, to se for ever more.
Each letter that I had received from her, I cast away,
And tokens all, I threw them down, to my no small dysmay.
Then busied I my self in things that myght me most delight,
And sought the chiefst means I could, to help my wearied spryght.
Sometime I wold behold the fields, and Hills that thou doste se,
Sometime I wold betray the Birds, that light on lime tree,
Especially in Shepstare time, when thick in flocks they fly,
One wold I take, and to her Leg, a lime Line wold tie,
And where the slock flew thickest, there I wold her cast away,
She straight unto the rest wold hye, amongst her Mates to play.
And preasyng in the mydste of them, with Line and Lyme, and all,
With clevyng wings, entangled fast they down together fall.
Sometime I wold the little Fish: with baited Hook beguile:
Sometime the crafty Fore I wold, deceive for all his wile:
Sometime the Wolf, I wold pursue, sometime the foaming Boor:
And when with labour all the day, my wearied Lyms were sore.
Than rest and sleep I straightway sought no Dreams did me affray:
Tormented nought with care, I past the lyngryng night away.
And thus I clean forgot: in time, the doting Days I saw,
And freed my self, to my great Joy, from Yoke of Lovers Law.
More of this same, I will the tell, the next time here we meet,
And stronger Medicines will I gyve, to purge that Venym sweet.
Behold the Day is slypt away, and Stars do fast appear,
Lo where Calicto Virgin ones, doth shine in Skies so clear.
Loe where old Ceph•us walks about, with twining Serpent bye,
We will no lenger hear abide, But hence will homwarde hye.
