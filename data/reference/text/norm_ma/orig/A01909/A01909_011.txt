When Brutish broil, and rage of war in Clownish hearts began
When Tigers stout, in Tanners bond unmusled all they ran,
The Noble Sheffeyld Lord by birth and of a courage good,
By clubbish hands, of crabbed Clowns there spent his Noble blood.
His noble birth availed not, his honor all was vain,
Amid the prease, of Mast ye Curs, the valyant Lord was slayve.
And after such a sort (O ruth,) that who can tears suppress.
To think the Dunghyll Dogs should dawnt the Flower of worthiness.
Whyle as the ravening Wolves he prayed his gylteles life to save.
A bloody Butcher big and blunt, a vile unweldy knave
With beastly blow of boisterous bill at him (O Lord) let drive,
And cleste his head, and said therewith shalt thou be left alive?
O Lord that I had present been, and Hectors force withal,
Before that from his Carlish hands, the cruel Bill did fall.
Then should that peasant vile have felt the clap upon his Crown,
That should have dazed his dogged hart from dryvyng Lords adown.
Then should my hands have saved thy life good Lord whon deare I loved
Then should my hart in doubtful case, full well to the ben proved,
But all in vain thy death I wail, thy Corpse in earth doth lie.
Thy king and Country for to serve thou dydste not fear to die.
Farewell good Lord, thy deth bewail all such as well the knew,
And every man laments thy case: and Googe thy death doth rew.
