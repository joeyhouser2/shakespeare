Receive you worthy Dame this rude and ragged verse,
Lend willing ear unto the tale, which I shall now rehearse.
and thogh my witless words, might move you for to smile
Yet trust to that which I shall tell, and never mark my style.
Amongst five hundreth Dames, presented to my view,
I find most cause by due desert, to like the best of you.
I see your beauty such, as seemeth to suffise,
To bind my hart in links of love, by judgment of mine eyes.
And but your bounty quench, the coals of quick desire,
I fear the face of yours will set, ten thousand hearts on fire.
But bounty so abounds, above all my desert,
As that I quake and shrink for fear, to shew you of my smart.
Yet since mine eye made choice, my hart shall not repent,
But yield it self unto your will, and therewith stand content.
God knoweth I am not great, my power it is not much,
The greater glory shall you gain, to shew your favor such.
And what I am or have, all that I yield to you,
My hand and sword shall serve always, to prove my toung is true.
Then take me for your own, and so I wilbe still,
Beleve me now, I make this vow, in hope of your good will.
Which if I may obtain, God leave me when I change,
This is the tale I ment to tell, good Lady be not strange.
