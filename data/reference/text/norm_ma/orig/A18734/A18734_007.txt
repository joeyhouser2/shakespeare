I yeild to death, and upward lift the mind
Where lothsome life, shall present comfort find.

Sens hope can have, no honey from the Hive
And pains can pluck, no pleasure for his toil
It is but vain, for weary life to strive
And stretch out time, with torment and tormoile
Get what we can, death triumphs oar the spoil
Than note this well, though we win neer so mitch
When death tacks all, we leave a mizer ritch.

To live and lack, is doble death in deed
A present death, exceeds a lingering woe
Sens no good hap, in youth did help my need
In age why should, I strive for Fortune so
Old years are come, and haests me hens to go
The t•me draws on, I hate the life I have
When hart shall break, my grief shall end in grave.

Should I seek life, that finds no place of rest
Ne sotle nor seat, to shroud me from the air
When cramping cold, be clips my careful brest
And dollor drives, my hart in deep dispayre
For such foul days, dark death is wondrous fair
As good to make, the skrawlling worms a feast
As pleasant the world, where mischief makes her neast.

Hie time it is, to haest my carcase hens
Youth stool away, and felt no kind of joy
And age he laft, in travel ever sens
The wanton days, that made me nice and coy
Wear but a dream, a shadoe and a toy
Sith slaurye heer, I find and nothing else
My hoem is thear, where soul in freedom dwells.

In wars and woe, my years aer waested clean
What should I see, if lordly lief I led
I loek in glass, and find my cheeks so lean
That every owre, I do but wish me ded
Now back bends down, and forwards faulls the hed
And hollow eyes, in wrinkled brow doth shrowd
As though two stars, wear creeping under cloud.

The lips wax cold, and loeks both pael and thin
The teeth fawlls out, as nuts forsoek the shaell
The baer bald head, but shoes where hear hath bin
The lively joints, wax weary stiff and staell
The reddy tongue, now folters in his taell
The wearish face, and tawny colour shoes
The corraeg quaills, as strength decays and goes.

The sweet delights, are drowned in dulled mind
The gladsome sports, to groaning sighs are bent
The frisking limbs, so far from frame I find
That I▪ forthincke, the time that youth hath spent
But when I way, that all these things wear lent
And I must pay, the earth her duty throw
I shrink no whit, to yield these pleasures now.

Had I possest, the gifts of Fortune heer
A house a wife, and children therewithal
And had in store (to make my friends good cheer)
Such common things, as neighbours have at call
In such dispayre, perchance I would not fall
But want of this, and other lacks a score.
Bids me seek death, and wish to live no more.

Yet for to bear, a peece of all my woes
(And to impart, the privy pangs I felt)
From country soil, a sober wife I choes
In mine own house, with whom I seldom dwelt
When thousandes slept, I waekt I swet I swelt
To compass that, I never could attain
And still from hoem, abroed I brack my brain.

The thatcher hath, a cottage poor you see
The shepherd knoes, where he shall sleep at night
The daily drudge, from cares can quiet be
Th•s Fortune sends, some rest to every wight
So borne I was, to house and land by right
But in a bag, to Court I brought the same
From Shrews brye town, a seat of ancient fame.

What thinks my friends, that thear behind I laft
What fault finds she, that gave me lief and suck
O courting fien, thou art to cold a craft
The Carter haeth, at hoem much better luck
Well, well I say, a due all worldly muck
Ne house nor land, we bear away I knoe
I naked cam, and naked hence must go.

The greatest king, must pass the self saem way
Our day of birth, and burial are alike
Their joy, their pomp, their wealth and rich array
Shall soen consuem, like snow that lies in dieck
No bucklar serves, when sudden death doth striek
As soen may coem, a poer mans soul to bliss
As may the rich, or greatest Lord that is.

Well ear my breath, my body do forsaek
My spreet I do, bequeathe to God above
My books, my skrowls, and songs that I did maek
I leave with friends, that freely did me love
To flyring foes, whoes malice did me move
I wish in haest, amendment of their ways
And to the Court, and courtiars happy days.

My fortuen strange, to strangers do I leave
That strangely can, retain such strange mishap
To such as still, in world did me disseave
I wish they may, bewaer of such like trap
To sclaundrous tongues, that killed me with their clap
I wish moer rest, than they have given me
And bles thoes shreaws, that corst and crabbed be.

To such as yet, did never pleasure man
I give those ryems, that nyps the gawlled back
To such as would, do good and if they can
I wish good luck, long lief, and void of lack
To currish karls, a whip and collyars sack
And to the proud, that stands upon their braus
A waynskot face, and twenty crabtree staves.

To surly syers, that scorns the meaner sort
A nightcap ford, with Foins I them bequeathe
To such as skowll, at others good report
(And sets much stoer, by their own painted sheath)
In sien of luck, I give a willow wreathe
To such as aer, unnaemd and merits mutch
The stoen I leave, that tries the gold by tutch.

To gentill race, with good conditions joined
I wish moer joy, than man imagine may
And sens for pooer, I have no money coined
God graunt them all, a merry marriage day
To such as doth, delyte in honest play
I wish the gold, that I have lost thearby
And all the wealth, I want befoer I die.

Now friends shack hands, I must be gone my boys
Our mirth taeks end, our triumph all is dONE

Another coms, when I my race have ron
Shall pass the time, with you in better plyt
