Upon my corpse pour forth thy wine, O frend that comes this way,
And on my tomb with pleasant hand, thy precious spices laye.
No gulf of grief my grave shall, but spring of lasting bliss,
I am not dead but changed my life, lo such my fortune is,
My former joys are not decayed, but as they were before,
If ought or nought I bear in mind, yet blest for evermore.
Now wealth doth wield the world, and wealth doth worship gain
Yea wealth doth win the friends at will, the poor each where complain
The grassy flag, the silk more soft
doth yield the sleeps with great delight:
But stately beds in towers aloft,
the richmans rest with fears, affright.
Some hourding up great heaps of gold, not knowing how to use then,
Like sacred stuff doth store up wealth: so folly doth abuse them,
But would to God he rather had, his time consumed in toys.
Then cast such care on cruel dedes, or sought such wicked joys,
When noble states he plucked a down, and men of worthy fame,
His subjects love forthwith he lost, and gained eternal blame.
But when in blood of simple souls, he bathed his bloody hand,
Without renenge he felt the smart, and could no longer stand.
Such guerdon God doth geve, to men of cruel mind
that seek the lives of harmless folk.
Happy is he whom God hath provided sufficiently wherewith to live.
Exceeding care of coin, doth mortal men begile,
and love of wordly pelf, all other joys exile.
O safe estate of life,
The pleasant days which poor men pass, a bliss above the rest,
to Gods almost unknown.
Amazed at this mischief new, now rich and yet in woe,
His wished wealth lo now he loathes, that erst he loved so,
No store can honger stanch, dry thurst his throte torments,
Thus worthily amids his gold, his former wish repentes.
You mourning Muses reach, whereof I should endight,
And bathe▪ my face in bitter tears, wherewith my woes I wright.
