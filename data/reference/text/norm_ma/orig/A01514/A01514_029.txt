Now have I found the way, to weep and wail my fill,
Now can I end my doleful days, and so content my will.
The way to weep inough, for such as list to wail,
Is this: to go abord the ship, where pleasure beareth sail.
And there to mark the jests, of every joyful wight,
And with what wind and wave they fleet, to nourish their delight.
For as the stricken Dear, that seeth his fellows feed,
Amid the lusty heard (unhurt,) and feels himself to bleed
Or as the seely bird, that with the Bolte is bruised,
And lieth aloof among the leaves, of all her pheares refused,
And hears them sing full shrill, yet cannot she rejoice,
Nor frame one warbling note to pass, out of her mournful voice.
Even so I find by proof, that pleasure doubleth pain,
Unto a wretched wounded hart, which doth in woe, remain.
I pass where pleasure is, I hear some sing for joy,
I see some laugh, some other dance, in spight of dark annoy.
But out alas my mind, amends not by their mirth,
I deem all pleasures to be pain, that dwell above the earth.
Such heavy humours feed, the blood that lendes me breath,
As merry medcins cannot serve, to keep my corpse from death.
