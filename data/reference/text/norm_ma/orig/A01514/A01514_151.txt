That self same day, and of that day that hower,
When she doth reign, that mocked Vulcan the smith,
And thought it meet to harbor in her bower,
Some gallant gest for her to dally with,
That blessed hour, that blist and happy day,
I thought it meet, with hasty steps to go:
Unto the lodge, wherein my Lady laye,
To laugh for joy, or else to weep for woe.
And lo, my Lady of her wonted grace,
First lent her lips to me (as for a kiss)
And after that her body to imbrace,
Wherein dame nature wrought nothing amiss.
What followed next, gesse you that know the trade,
For in this sort, myr Fydaies feast I made.
Beauty shut up thy shop, and truss up all thy trash,
My Nell hath stolen thy finest stuff, and left thee in the lash got
Thy market now is marred, thy gains are gone god wot,
Thou hast no ware, that may compare, with this that J have
As for thy painted pale, and wrinkles surfted up:
Are deare ynough, for such as lust to drink of every cup:
Thy bodies bolstered out, with bumbact and with bags,
Thy rolls, thy ruffs, thy, caules, thy coifes, thy Jerkins and thy Jagges.
Thy curling, and thy cost, thy friesling and thy fare,
To court to court with all those tois, and there set forth such ware
Before their hungry eyes, that gaze on every gest,
And choose the cheapest chaffaire still, to please their fancy best.
But I whose stedfast eyes, could never cast a glaunce,
With wandering look, amid the prese, to take my choise by chance
Have won by due desert, a peece that hath no peer,
And left the rest as refuse all, to serve the market there:
There let him chuse that list, there catch the best who can:
A painted blazing bait may serve, to choke a gazing man.
But I have slipped thy flower, that freshest is of hew:
I have thy corn, go sell thy chaff, I list to seek no new,
The windows of mine eyes, are glazed with such delight,
As each new face seems full of faults, that blaseth in my sight:
And not without just cause, I can compare her so,
Lo here my glove I challenge him, that can, or dare say no.
Let Theseus come with club, or Paris brag with brand,
To prove how fair their Hellen was, that scourged the Grecian land:
Let mighty Mars himself, come armed to the field:
And vaunt dame Venus to defend, with helmet, spear, and shield.
This hand that had good hap, my Hellen to embrace,
Shall have like luck to still her foes, and daunt them with disgrace.
And cause them to confess by verdict and by othe,
How far her lovely looks do stein, the beauties of them both.
And that my Hellen is more fair then Paris wife,
And doth deserve more famous praise, then Venus for her life.
Which if I not perform, my life then let me leese,
Or else be bound in chains of change, to beg for beauties feese.
THE stately Dames of Rome, their Pearls did wear.
About their necks to beautify their name:
But she (whom I do serve) her pearls doth bear,
Close in her mouth, and smiling shewe, the same.
No wonder then, though every word she speaks,
A Jewel seem in judgement of the wise,
Since that her sugared tongue the passage breaks,
Betweene two rocks, bedecked with pearls of price.
Her hair of gold, her front of Juory,
(A bloody heart within so white a breast)
Her teeth of Pearl lips Ruby, christall eye,
Needs must J honour her above the rest:
Since she is form of none other mould,
But Ruby, Christall, Ivory, Pearl, and Gold.
