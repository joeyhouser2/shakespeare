Reader reward nought else, but only good report,
For all these pleasant Posies here, bound up in sundry sort.
The flowers fair and fresh, were set with painful toil,
Of late in Gascoignes Garden plot, a passing pleasant soil.
Now weeds of little worth, are culde from out the rest,
Which he with double pain, did work, to glean the bad fron best,
The state is very strange, and fortune rare in use,
Whose heavy happen he neither helps, nor blazeth their abuse.
In thundering verse he wrays, where highest minds be thrall,
Where mischief seeks to raise it self, by force of others fall
He plucks the visour of, from masks of peevish pride,
And wrays what sowre (in sweet pretence) the coustly corts can hide.
In every gallant flower, he setteth forth to show,
Of Venus thralls, the hap, the harm, the want, the weal, the woe.
He finely finds their faults, whose wealth doth foster wrong,
Who toucheth sin (without offence) must plainly sing his song.
His lofty vain in verse, his stately style in prose,
Foretells that Pallas ment by him, for to defend her foes.
Wherewith to Mars his might, his lusty limbs are knit,
(A sight most rare) that Hectors mind, should match with Pallas wit.
By proof of late appeared (how so reports here ran)
That he in field was foremost still, in spoil the hynmost man.
No backward blasts could bruse the valour of his thought,
Although sly hap, forestoode his hope, in that he credit sought.
In fortunes spight he strave, by virtues to aspire,
Resolved when due deserts might mount, then he should have his hire
Thus late with Mars in field, a lusty Soldier showed,
And now with peace in Pallas school, he friendly hath bestowed,
On thee this heap of flowers, the fruits of all his toil,
Whereof if some but simple seem, consider well the soil.
They grew not all at home, some came from foreign fields,
The which (percase) set here again, no pleasant savour yields.
Yet who misliketh most, the worst will hardly mend,
And he were best not write at all, which no man will offend.
