IN youthful years when fyrst my young desires began,
To prick me forth to serve in Court a sclender tall young man.
My Fathers blessing then I asked upon my knee,
Who blessing me with trembling hand, these words gan say to me.
My son, God guide thy way, and shield thee from mischance,
And make thy just deserts in Court, thy poor estate to advance.
Yet when thou art become one of the Courtly train,
Think on this proverb old (quoth he) that fair words make fools faine.

This counsel gravely given, most strange appears to me.
Till tract of time, with open eyes, had made me plainly see.
What subtle sleights are wrought, by painted tales devise,
When hollow hearts with friendly shoes the simple do entice.
To think all gold that shines to feed their fond desire,
Whose shivering cold is warmed with smoke, in stead of flaming fire.
Sith talk of tickle trust, doth breed a hope most vain,
This proverb true by proof I find, that fair words make fools faine.

Fair speech alway doeth well, where deeds insue fair words,
Fair speech again, alway doth evil, that bushes gives for birds.
Who hopes to have fair words, to try his lucky lot,
If I may counsel let him strike it, whyle the iron is hot.
But them that feed on clods, in stead of pleasant grapes,
And after warning often given, for better luck still gapes.
Full loth I am, yet must I tell them in words plain,
This proverb old proves true in them, that fair words makes fools faine.

Woe worth the time that words, so slowly turn to deeds,
Woe worth the time, that fair sweet flowers, are growen to rotten weeds.
But thrice woe worth the time, that trueth, away is fled,
Wherein I see how simple hearts, with words are vainly fed.
Trust no fair words therefore, where no deeds do ensue,
Trust words, as skilful Falkeners do trust Hawks that never flew.
Trust deeds, let wodrdes be words, which never wrough me gain,
Let my experience make you wise, and let words make fools faine.
