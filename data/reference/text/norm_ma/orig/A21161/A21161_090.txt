TO counsel my estate, abandonde to the spoil,
Of forged friends whose grossest fraud, it set with finest foil.
To verify true dealing wights, whose trust no treason dreads,
And all to deare thacquaintance be, of such most harmful heads.
I am advised thus, who so doeth friend, friend so,
As though to morrow next he feared, for to become a foe.

To have a feigned friend, no peril like I find,
Oft fleryng face may mantel best, a mischief in the mind:
A pair of angels ears, oft times doeth hide a serpents harte,
Under whose gripes who so doeth come, to late complains the smart.
Wherefore I do advise, who so doeth friend friend so,
As though to morrow next, he should become a mortal foe.

Refuse respecting friends, that courtly know to faine,
For gold that wins for gold, shall lose, the self same frend again:
The Quail needs never fear, in fowlers nets to fall,
If he would never bend his ear, to listen to his call.
Therefore trust not to soon, but when you friend friend so,
As though to morrow next, ye feard for to become a foe.
