What death may be, compared to love?
What grief therein, now doest thou prove?
My pains alas who can express,
I see no cause of heaviness.
My Ladies looks, my woe hath wrought:
Then blame thine eyes that first have sought,
I burn alas, and blow the fire,
A fool consumes by his desire,
What shall I do than?
come out and thou can.
Alas I die,
what remedy?
My sugared sweet, is mixed with gall,
Thy Lady can not do with all:
The more I seek, the less I find,
Then strive not with the stream and wind.
Her must I love, although I smart,
With thy own sword, thou slaiest thy harte:
Such pleasant baits, who can refrain,
Such beats will sure breed the great pain.
What shall I do than?
Come out and thou can.
Alas I die,
what remedy.
Her golden beams, mine eyes do daze,
Upon the Son thou mayest not gaze:
She might reward my cruel smart,
She thinks thou bearest a feigned harte.
She laughs to hear my woeful cries,
Forsake her then, in time be wise:
No no alas, that may not be,
No wise man then will pity thee,
What shall I do than?
come out and thou can.
Alas I die,
what remedy.
A livyng death, lo thus I prove,
Such are the fruts of froward love:
O that I might her love once again,
Thy gain would not, half quite the pain.
Her will I love though she be coy,
A fool him self will still annoy:
Who will not die for such a one,
Be wise at length, let her alone:
I can not do so,
then be thy own foe,
Alas I die,
what remedy.
