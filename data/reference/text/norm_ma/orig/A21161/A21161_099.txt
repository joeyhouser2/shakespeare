I Rage in restless ruth, and ruins rule my days,
I rue (to late) my rechlesse youth, by rules of reasons ways:
I ran so long a race, in search of surest way,
That leisure learned me tread the trace, that led to leud decay.
I gave so large a rain, to unrestrained bit,
That now with proof of after pain, I wail my want of wit:
I trifeled forth the time, with trust to self conceits,
Whilst plenties use pricked forth my prime, to search for sugared baits.
Wherein once learned to find, I found so sweet a taste,
That dew foresight of after speed, self will esteemed waste.
Which will, through wilfulness, hath wrought my witless fall,
And heedless youths unskilfulness, hath leapt my life in thrall.
Whereby by proof I know, that pleasure breedeth pain,
And he that evil seed doeth sow evil frute must reap again.
Let such therefore, whose youth, and purses are in Prime,
Foresee and shun, the helpless ruth, which fews misspence of time.
For want is nexte to waste, and shame doeth sin ensue,
Evil speeding proof hath hedeles hast, my self hath proved it true.
When neighbours next house burns, tis time thereof take heed,
For fortunes wheel hath choise of turns, which change of chanses breds.
My sail hath been aloft, though now I bear but low,
Who clims to high selde falleth soft, dedst ebb hath highest flow.
