The higher that the Ceder tree, under the heavens do grow,
The more in danger is the top, when sturdy winds gan blow,
Who judges then in princely throne, to be devoid of hate,
Doeth not yet know, what heaps of ill, lies hid in such estate.
Such dangers great, such gripes of mind, such toil do they sustain,
That oftentimes, of God they wish, to be unkyngde again.

For as the huge and mighty rocks, withstand the raging seas,
So kingdoms in subjection be, whereas dame Fortune please:
Of brittle joy, of smiling cheare, of honey mixt with gall,
Allotted is to every Prince, in fredome to be thrall.
What watches long, what steps unsure, what griefs and cares of mind:
What bitter broils, what endless toils, to kingdoms be assingde.

The subject then may well compare with prince for pleasant days,
Whose silent might brings quiet rest, whose might no storm bewrays:
How much be we, then bound to God, who such provision makes
To laye our cares upon the Prince thus doeth he for our saks.
To him therefore, let us lift up our hearts, and pray a main:
That every Prince that he hath placed, may long in quiet reign.
