Behold the blast which blows, the blossoms from the tree,
The end whereof consumes and comes, to nought we see.
Ere thou therefore be blowen, from life that may not last,
Begin for grace, to call for time mispent and past.

Have mind on brittle life, whose pleasures are but vain,
On death likewise bethink, how thou maist not remain.
And fear thy Lord to green, which sought thy soul to save,
To sin no more be bent, but mercy ask and have.

For death who doth not spare, the kings on earth to kill,
Shall reap also from thee, thy pleasure, life, and will.
That life which yet remains, and in thy brest appears,
Hath sown in thee such seeds, you ought to weed with tears.

And life that shall succeed, when death is worn and past,
Shall spring for ever then, in joy or pain to last.
Where death on life hath power ye see, that life also,
Hath mowen the fruits of death, which never more shall grow.
