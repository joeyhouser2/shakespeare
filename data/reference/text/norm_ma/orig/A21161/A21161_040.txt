Although the purple morning, brags in brightness of the sun,
As though he had of chased night, a glorious conquest won:
The Time by day, gives place again to forse of drowsy night,
And every creature is constrained, to change his lusty plight.
Of pleasures all, that here we taste:
We feel the contrary at laste.
In spring, though pleasant Zephirus, hath fruitful earth inspired,
And nature hath each bush, each branch, with blossoms brave attired:
Yet fruits and flowers, as buds and blomes, full quickly withered be,
When stormy Winter comes to kill, the Somers jollity.
By Time are got, by Time are lost
All things, wherein we pleasure most.
Although the Seas so calmly glide, as dangers none appear,
And dout of storms, in sky is none, king Phebus shines so clear:
Yet when the boisterous windes break out, and raging waves do swell,
The seely bark now heaves to heaven, now sinks again to hell.
Thus change in every thing we see,
And nothing constant, seems to be.
Who floweth most in worldly wealth, of wealth is most unsure,
And he that chiefly tastes of joy, doth sometime woe indure:
Who vaunteth most of numbered friends, forgo them all he must,
The fairest flesh and livelest bioud, is turned at length to dust.
Experience gives a certain ground,
That certain here, is nothing found.
Then trust to that which aye remains, the bliss of heavens above,
Which Time, nor Fate, nor Wind, nor Storm, is able to remove:
Trust to that sure celestial rock, that rests in glorious throne,
That hath bene, is, and must be still, our anker hold alone.
The world is but a vanity,
In heaven seek we our surety.
