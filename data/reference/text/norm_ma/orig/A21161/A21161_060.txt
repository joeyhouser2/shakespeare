IN hope the Shipman hoiseth sail, in hope of passage good,
In hope of health the sickly man, doeth suffer loss of blood.
In hope the prisoner linked in chains, hopes liberty to find,
Thus hope breds health and health breds ease, to every troubled mind.

In hope desire gets victory, in hope great comfort springs,
In hope the lover lives in joys, he fears no dreadful flings:
In hope we •ive and may abide, such storms as are assigned,
Thus hope breds health, and health breds ease, to every troubled mind.

In hope we easily suffer harm, in hope of future time,
In hope of fruit, the pain seems sweet, that to the tree doeth clime
In hope of love such glory grows, as now by proof I find:
That hope breds health, and health breds ease to every troubled mind.
