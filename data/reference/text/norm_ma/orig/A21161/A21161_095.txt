The bitter sweat that strains my yielded harte,
The careless count, that doeth the same embrace:
The doubtful hope, to reap my due desert,
The pensive path, that guides my restless race.
Are at such war within my wounded brest,
As doeth bereave my joy and eke my rest,

My greedy will, that seks the golden gain,
My luckless lot, doeth alway take in worth:
My mated mind, that dreads my suits in vain,
My piteous plaint, doeth help for to set forth.
So that betwene twoo waves of raging seas,
I drive my days in troubles and disease.

My woeful eyes do take their chief delight,
To feed their fill upon the pleasant maze:
My hidden harms that grow in me by sight,
With pining pains do drive me from the gaze.
And to my hope I reap no other hire,
But burn my self, and I to blow the fire.
