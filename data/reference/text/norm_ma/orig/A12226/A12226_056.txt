Sweet swelling lip well maist thou swell in pride,
Since best wits think it best thee to admire,
Natures praise, virtues stall, Cupids cold fire,
Whence words, not words but heavenly graces slide,
The new Pernassus where the Graces bide:
Sweetness of Musique, Wisdoms beautifier,
Breather of life, and fastness of desire,
Where Beauties blush in Honours grain is dyde.
Thus much my heart my mouth compelled to say:
But now, spite of my heart my tongue will stay,
Loathing all lies, doubting this flattery is,
And no spur can this resty race refrain;
Wherefore to try if that I said be true,
How can I better prove then with a kiss?

O Kiss which doth those ruddy gems impart,
Or joys or fruits of new found Paradise,
Breathing all bliss and sweetness to the hart,
Teaching dumb lips a nobler exercise.
O kiss which souls even souls together ties
By links of love, and only natures Art,
How faine would I paint thee to all mens eyes,
Or of thy gifts at least set out some part?
But she forbids, with blushing words she says,
She builds her fame on higher seated praise:
But my heart burns, I cannot silent be,
Then since deare kiss you faine would have me peace,
And I (mad with delight) want wit to cease,
Stop you my mouth with still still kissing me.
