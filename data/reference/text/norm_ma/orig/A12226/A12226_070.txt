Dian that faine would cheare her friend the Night,
Doth shewe her oft at full her fairest face,
Bringing with her those starry Nymphs, whose chace
From heavenly standing hurts each mortal wight.
But ah poor Night in love with Phoebus light,
And endlessly despairing of his grace,
Her self to shewe no other joy hath place,
Sylent and sad in morning weeds doth dight:
Even so (alas) a Lady Dians peer,
With choise delight and rarest company,
Would faine drive clouds from out my heavy cheer:
But woe is me, though joy her self were she,
She could not shewe my blind brain ways of joy
While I despair my Suns light to enjoy.

AH bed the feeld where joys peace some do see:
The feeld where all my thoughts to war be trained,
How is thy grace by my strange fortune stained?
How thy low shrowds by my sighs stormed be?
With sweet soft shades thou oft invitest me
To steal some rest, but wretch I am constrained,
Spurred with Loves spurr, this held and shortly rained
With Cares hard hand, to turn and toss in thee,
While the black horrors of the silent night,
Paint Woes black face so lively in my sight,
That tedious leisure marks each wrinkled line:
But when Aurora leads out Phoebus dance,
Mine eyes then only wink for spite perchance,
That worms should have their Sun and I want mine.
