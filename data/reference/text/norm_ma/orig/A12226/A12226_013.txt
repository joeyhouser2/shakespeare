Alas, have I not pain enough my friend,
Upon whose breast, a fiercer gripe doth tyre,
Than did on him, who first stole down the fire;
While Love on me, doth all his quiver spend,
But with your rubarbe words you must contend,
To greeve me worse in saying, that desire
Doth plunge my well formed soul, even in the mier
Of sinful thoughts, which do in ruin end.
If that be sin which doth the manners frame,
Well stayed with trueth, in word and faith of deed,
Ready of wit, and fearing nought but shame;
If it be sin which in sixt hart doth breed,
A loathing of all lost true chastity;
Then love is sin, and let me sinful be.
