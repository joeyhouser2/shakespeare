Fair eyes, sweet lips, deer hart, that foolish I
Could hope by Cupids help, on you to pray:
Since to himself he doth your gifts apply,
As his main force, chief sport, and easeful stay.
For when he will see who dare him gainsay,
Then with those eyes he looks, lo by and by,
Each soul doth at Loves feet his weapons lay,
Glad if for her he give them leave to die.
When he will play, then in her lips his eye,
Where blushing red, that Loves self them do love,
With either lip he doth the other kiss;
But when he will for quiets sake remove
From all the world, her hart is then his room:
Where well he knows, no man to him can come.

MY words I know do well set forth my mind,
My mind, bemones his sence of inward smart:
Such smart may pity claim of any hart;
Her hart, sweet hart, is of no Tigers kind,
And yet she hears, and I no pity find,
But more I cry, less grace she doth impart;
Alas, what cause is there so overthwart,
That Nobleness it self makes thus unkind?
I much do gesse, yet find no truth but this,
That when the breath of my complaints do touch
Those dainty doors unto the Court of Bliss,
That once come there, the sobs of my annoys,
Are metamorphosed straight to tunes of joys.
