Sir painter, are thy colours ready set,
My Mistress can not be with thee to day,
She's gone into the field to gather May,
The timely Prymrose, and the Violet:
Yet that thou maist, not disappointed be,
Come draw her picture by my fantasee.

And well for thee, to paint her by thine ear,
For should thine eye, unto that office serve,
Thine Eye, and Hand, thy Art, and Hart, would swerve,
Such majesty her countenance doth bear,
And where thou wert Apelles thought before,
For failing so, thou shouldst be praised no more.

Draw first her Front, a perfect Ivory white,
Hie, spacious, round, and smooth on either side,
Her temples branched with vains, blew, opening wide.
As in the Map, Danubius runs in sight:
Colour her semicircled browes with jet,
The throne where Love triumphantly doth set.

Regard her Eye, her eye, a wondrous part,
It woundeth deep, and cureth by and by,
It drives away, and draweth courteously,
It breeds and calms, the tempest of the hart,
And what to lightning Jove, belongeth too,
The same her looks, with more effect can do.

Her Cheek, resembleth every kind of way,
The Lily stained, with sweet Adonis blood,
As wounded he strayed up and down the wood,
For whom fair Venus languished many a day,
Or plainly more to answer your demaune,
Her cheeks are Roses, overcast with lawn.

Her lovely Lip, doth others all excel,
On whom it please (ay me) a kiss bestoe,
He never tasteth afterward of woe,
Such special vertue in the toutch doth dwell:
The colour tempered of the morning red,
Where with Aurora doth adorn her head.

Her ample Chest, an heavenly plot of ground,
The space betweene, a Paradise at least,
Parnassus like, her twofold mounting breast,
Her heavenly graces, heapingly abound,
Love spreads his conquering colours in this feeld,
Whereto the race of Gods and men do yield.

The other parts, which custom doth conceal,
Within a sarcenet vaile thou must convey,
So due proportion well discern I may,
What though the garment do not all reveal,
The shadow of a naked thigh may fraight,
His head brim full, hath any fine conceit.

Before her Feet, upon a Marble stone,
Inflamed with the Sunbeams of her eye,
Depaint my hart that burneth passionately,
And if thy pencil can set down such mone,
Thy picture self, will teeling semblance make,
Of ruth and pity for my torments sake.

How now Apelles, are thy senses tane?
Hast drawn a picture, or drawn out thy hart?
Wilt thou be held a Master of thine art,
And temper colours tending to thy bane?
Happy my hart, that in her Sunshine fries,
Above thy hap that in her shadow dies.

I Pray thee Love, say, whither is this posting,
Since with thy deity first I was acquainted,
I never saw thee thus distracted coasting,
With countenance tainted.

Thy conquering arrows broken in thy quiver,
Thy brands that wont the inward marrow sunder,
Fireles and forceless, all a pieces shiver,
With mickle wonder.

That maketh next my staylesse thoughts to hover,
I cannot sound this uncouth cause of being,
The vaile is torn that did thy visage cover,
And thou art seeing.

A stranger, one (quoth Love) of good demerit,
Did sute and service to his Sovereign proffer,
In any case she would not seem to hear it,
But scorned the offer.

And very now upon this Maying morrow,
By break of day, he found me at my harbour,
I went with him, to understand his sorrow,
Unto her Arbour

Where he Love torments dolefully unfolded,
With words, that might a Tigers hart have charmed,
His sighs and tears, the mountain ye had moulted,
And she not warmed.

Her great disdain against her Lover proved,
Kindled my brand, that to her brest I seated,
The flame betweene her paps, them often moved.
Nor burnt, nor heated.

My arrows keen I afterward assayed,
Which from her brest without effect rebounded.
And as a ball, on Marble floor the played,
With force confounded.

The brand that burnt, old Pryams Town to ashes,
Now first his operation, wants it than,
The darts that Emerald skies in pieces dashes,
Skornd by a woman.

Thus while I said, she toward me arrived,
And with a tutch of triumph, never doubted,
To tear the vaile, that use of sight bereaved,
So Love was louted.

The vaile of erroe, from mine eyes bereaved,
I saw heavens hope, and earth her treasury,
Well maist thou err said I, I am deceived,
Bent to pleasure thee.

Cease hapless man, my succors to importune,
She only she, my stratagems repelleth,
Vainly endeour I, to tempt her Fortune,
That so excelleth.

Content thee man, that thou didst see and suffer,
And be content, to suffer, see, and die,
And die content, bicause thou once didst move her,
She displeased thereby.

And herewithal I left the man a dying,
For by his passions I perceived none other,
I hie me thus ashamed with speedy flying,
To tell my Mother.
