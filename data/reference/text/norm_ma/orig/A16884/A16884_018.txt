O Chastity the chief of heavenly lights,
Which makest us most immortal shape to wear•,
—Chastities attire,
The unstained vaile which innocents adorn,
Th'ungathered rose defended with the thorn.
O Charity, the gift of blessed souls,
Comfort in death, a crown unto the life:
Which all the passions of the mind controls,
Adorns the maid, and bewtifies the wife,
That grace, the which nor death, nor time attaints.
Of earthly creatures making heavenly Saints.
— A Woman cannot take upon her,
With beauty, riches, nor with hie nobility,
To claim the true deserved praise of honour,
If Chastity do fail by her fragility,
This is the vertue that defends her honour.
Who doth desire that chaste his wife should be,
First be he true, for truth doth truth deserve,
Then he be such as he his words may see,
And always one credit, which her preserve
Not toying kind, nor causelessly unkind.
Not stirring thoughts, nor yet denying right,
Not spying faults, nor in plain errors blind,
Never •ard hand, nor ever raines to light,
As far from want, as far from vain exspence:
Th'one doth enforce, th'other doth entice,
All owe good company, but drive from thence
All filthy mouths that glory in their vice.
This done, thou hast no more, but leave the rest
To nature, fortune, time, and womans brest.
Penelope in spending chaste her days,
As worthy as Ulisses was of praise.
