First we do taste the fruit, then see our sin.
Shame follows sin, disgrace is daily given,
Impiety will out, never so closely done,
No walls can hide us from the eye of heaven,
For shame must end what wickedness begun,
Forth breaks reproach when we least think thereon.
Like as diseases common cause of death,
Bring danger most when least they prick and smart:
Which is a sign they have expulst the breath
Of lively heat which doth defend the hart,
Even so such sinnes as felt are on no part,
Have conquered grace, and by their wicked ure,
So killed the soul that it can have no cure.
Sinnes harvest never fails, but grace hath death.
Cover thou fier never so close within,
Yet out it will, and so will secret sin.
It doubles sin if finely sin we practise to prevent.
Man may securely sin, but safely never.
What wight on earth can void of fault be found?
What Saint is that who doth not sin sometime?
Tween good and bad this difference sole is found,
That good men sin but seld, and mend betime.
The bad man (making scruple none nor question)
Yields willingly to every lewd suggestion.
Sinnes oft assayed, ere thought to be no sin,
So soileth sin, the soul it sinketh in.
Shame leaves us by degrees, not at first coming,
For nature checks a new offence with loathing.
But use of sin doth make it seem as nothing.
What though our sinnes go brave and better clad?
They are as those in rags, as base, as bad.
The spot is foul, though by a Monarch made,
Kings cannot privilege a sin forbade.
—Sin ever must
Be tortured with the rack of his own frame,
For he that holds no faith, shall find no trust,
But sowing wrong, is sure to reap the same.
— Cunning sin being clad in virtues shape,
Flies much reproof, and many storms doth scape.
—Place for people, people place, and all for sin decay.
To punish sin is good, it is no nay,
They wreck not sin, but merit wreck for sin
The fathers fault that wreak upon the kin.
The sin to which a man by love is driven,
So much rhe rather ought to be forgiven.
