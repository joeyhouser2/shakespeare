Riches of children pass a princes throne,
Which touch the fathers heart with secret joy.
When without shame he saith, these be mine own.
This pattern good or ill our children get,
For what they see, their parents love or hate,
Their first caught sence prefers to teachers blows,
The cockerings cockerd, we bewail too late,
When that we see our offspring gaily bent,
Women man-wood, and men effeminate.
— What children apprehend,
The same they like, they follow and amend.
There is no love may be compaerd to that,
The tender mother bears unto the child,
For even so much the root it doth encrease,
As their grief grows, our contentation cease.
