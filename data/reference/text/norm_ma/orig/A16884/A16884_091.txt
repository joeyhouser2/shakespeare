All like as sicker as the end of woe is Joye,
And glorious light to obscure night doth tend,
So extreme Joy in extreme woe doth end.
For why extremes are haps rackt out of course,
By violent might far swinged forth perforce:
Which as they are piercingest they violentest move:
For that they are nere to cause that doth them shove.
So soonest fall from that their highest extreme,
To th'other contrary that doth want of mean,
So laughed he erst that laughed out his breath.
The pleasing means bode not the luckiest ends,
Nor aye, found treasure to like pleasure tends.
Mirth means not mirth always thrice happy line
Of wit to shun th'excesse that all desire.
Joy lighteneth woe, woe Joy doth moderate.
Joy is forgetful, weal thinks not of woe.
—Joy ascends, but sorrow sinks below.
Fruits follow flowers, and sorrow greatest Joys.
As sudden grief, so sudden Joy doth kill.
The Roman widow died when she beheld
Her son who erst she counted slain in field.
—Excessive Joy
Leapeth and likes finding the Appian way
Too strait for her: whose senses all possess
All wished pleasure, in all plenteousness.
