—Next within the entry of the gate,
Sat fell revenge, gnashing her teeth with ire,
Devising means how she may vengeance take,
Never in rest till she have her desire.
But frets within so far forth with the fier
Of wreaking flames, that now determines she,
To die by death, or vengd by death to be.
O fearful frowning Nemesis,
Daughter of justice most severe,
That art the worlds great arbitress,
And Queen of causes reigning here.
Fierce Nemesis mother of fate and change,
Sword bearer of th'eternall providence.
— Nemesis whose hasty revenging
Hands are ever at hand: whose mind is mutable always,
At miseries laughing, at mens felicity grudging.
—Nemesis hie mistress of revenge,
That with the scourge keeps all the world in awe.
The mind by wrong is made a malcontent,
And clouds her shine in pleaslesse melancholy,
Her holy humours are in passion spent,
Till by revenge she's set at liberty.
For tis revenge that satisfaction brings
To injured minds, and to oppressed things.
The soul is like a boisterous working sea,
Swelling in billows for disdain of wrongs,
And tumbling up and down from bay to bay,
Proves great with child of indignations.
Yet with revenge is brought to calm allay,
Disburdend of the pain thereto belongs.
Her bowers are turned to bright-faced sunshine braves,
And fair content plays gently on her waves.
Revenge dies not, rigour begets new wrath,
And blood hath never glory, mercy hath.
Revenge is mine, saith he that sits on hie.
O dire revenge when thou in time art rakte,
From out the ashes that preserve thee long,
And lightly from thy cinders art awaked,
Fuel to freedom, and revived with wrong:
How soon from sparks the greatest flames art sprung?
Which doth by nature to his top aspire,
Whose massy greatness once kept down his fier.
Revenge in tears doth ever wash his hands,
Who so doth threat means of revenge doth lose.
Had I revenged bene of every harm,
My coat had never kept me half so warm.
Though vengeance come behind, and her foot sore,
She overtakes th'offender going before.
