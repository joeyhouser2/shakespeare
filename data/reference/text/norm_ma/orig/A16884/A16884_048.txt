— His gliste•ing armour made
A little glooming light, much like a shade:
By which she saw the ugly monster plain,
Half like a serpent, horribly displayed,
But th'other half did womans shape retain,
Most loathsome, filthy, foul, and full of vile disdain.
And as she lay upon the dyrtie ground,
Her huge long tail, her den all overspread,
Yet was in knots, and many bouts upwound:
Pointed with mortal sting: of her there bred
A thousand yoong ones, which she daily fed,
Sucking upon her poisoned dugs, each one
Of sundry shapes, yet all ill savoured.
Soon as the uncouth light upon them shone,
Into her mouth they crept, and suddenly were gone.
To err is proper unto men, and but brutish to persist▪
— Errors are no errors, but by fate,
For oft the event makes foul faults fortunate.
— Errors left unpunished, are profest,
And being not defended, are opprest.
To hear good counsel, error never loves.
— Errors are hardly moved,
That love doth breed in an unadvised brest.
A stony coldness hath benumbde the sence,
And lively spirits of each living wight,
And dimmed with darkness their intelligence,
Darkness more then Cymerians day by night,
And monsters Error flying in the air,
Hath marred the face of all that seemeth fair.
