Now gan the golden Phoebus for to steep,
His fiery face in billows of the west,
And his faint Steeds watered in Ocean deep,
Whilst from their journal labours they do rest.
—Lo the great Automedon of day,
In Isis stream his golden locks doth steep,
Sad even her dusky mantle doth display,
Light flying fouls the posts of night do sport them,
And cheerful looking Phoebe doth comfort them.
By this the welked Phoebus gan avail,
His weary wain and now the frosty night,
Her mantle black through heaven gan overhaile,
Such love as Phoebus from the coloured sky,
Did headlong drive his horses toward the west,
To suffer horned Luna for ro pry,
Amidst the dusky dark.

And the tree shadows three times greater made.
And now the Sun was past his middleway,
Leaning more lovely to his lemons bed,
And the Moons third hour had attached the day.
By this the sun had spred his golden locks
Upon the pale green carpet of the sea,
And opened wide the scarlet door which locks,
The easeful evening from the labouring day,
Now night began to leap from yron rocks.
And whips her rusty wagon through the way.
The blushing sun plucks in his smiling beams,
•aking his steeds to mend their wonted pace,
Till plunging down into the ocean streams,
There in the frothy waves he hides his face,
Then raines them in more then his usual space,
And leaves foul darkness to possess the sky,
A time most fit for foulest tragedy.
Now the sun is mounted up on hie,
And pawseth in the midst of all the sky,
His fiery face upon the earth doth beat,
And bakes it with intolerable heat.
