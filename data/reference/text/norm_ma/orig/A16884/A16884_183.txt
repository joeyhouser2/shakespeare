— Words
Windy attorneys of our client woes,
Ayery succeeders of intestate joys,
Poor breathing Orators of miseries,
Let them have scope, though what it doth impart
Help not at all, yet doth it ease the heart.
Words are the tenants of an itching toy.
Allusion of words is no sure ground,
For one thereon a steddie work to found.
One word of woe another after traineth.
—Few words shall fit the trespass best,
Where no excuse can give the fault amending.
Deep sounds make better noise then shallow fords,
And sorrow ebbs being blown with wind of words.
Words are but wind, why cost they then so much,
The guilty kick when they too smartly to•ch.
Forth irreturnable flies the spoken word,
Be it in scoff, in earnest, or in bourd,
Without return and unreceiued it hangs,
And at the takers mercy or rigor stands:
Which if he sourly wrest, with wrathful cheare,
The shivering word turns to the hearers fear:
If friendly courtesy do the word expound,
To th'speakers comfort quickly it doth redound.
Smooth words dissolve hard stones, fair words inforce
Pity in flinty hearts.
Through the world if it were sought,
Fair words enow a man should find,
They be good cheap, they cost right nought,
Their substance is but only wind:
But well to say, and so to mean,
That sweet accord is seldom seen.
— Words well placed move things were never thought.
Even as the vapour which the fire repels,
Turns not to earth, but in mid-ayre dwells,
Where while it hangs, if Boreas frosty flaws,
With rigor rattle it: not to rain it thaws,
But thunder, lightning, rattling, hail, or snow,
Sends down to earth, whence first it rose below.
But if fair Phebus with his countenance sweet
Resolve it, down the dew or Manna sleete:
The Manna dew that in the Esterne lands,
Excelles the labour of the Bees small hands,
Else for her Memnon, gray Auroraes tears,
On the earth it stilleth the partner of her fears,
Or sendeth sweet showers to glad their mother earth
Whence first they took their first in constant birth.
To those great griefs ill taken words do grow,
Of words well taken such delights do flow.
— Men do foulest when they finest speak.
They wash a More, they strive to dry the seas,
And plain proud Atlas, that intend to please
By filthy words, by railing, and detraction,
Proper to Momus, and his hateful faction:
For when they think they have deserved most,
Alas say wisdom, all the toil is lost.
Few words well couched, do most content the wise.
Rash words flow from an unadvised mind.
Who once hath past the bounds of honesty
In earnest deeds, may pass it well in words.
Have care to whom, of whom, and what to speak, though speech be true
That miss made Phoebus contrary his ravens swan-like hue.
If so the crow would feast him without prate,
More meat he should receive, less brawl and hate
A fool he is that comes to preach and prate,
When men with swords their right and wrong debate.
— Words well disposed,
Have secret power t'appease inflamed rage.
