This false painted deity called Laude,
Which makes us thirst for vain eternity:
Twixt our desires and hope, a cunning baud
Ushers the soul unto extremity:
And helped by sly insinuating fraud,
Covers her deeds in scrolls of piety.
The hope of praise makes men no travel shun,
To say an other day this have we done.
Who rightly climes the top of endless praise,
Regards not what the wise discourser says.
—From praise takes envy cause.
The chiefest praise is to imbrace the man
In wealth and woe, with whom our love began.
The greatest praise, in greatest perils won.
The looser wantons sild are praised of many,
Vice oft finds friends but vertue seldom any.
In Athence where Themistocles remained,
Though much he conquered by his regiments,
Yet Solon was more praised for his intents.
Praise not the beauty of thy wife, though she of fame be spred,
For Gyges moved so, did graft on Caudales his hed.
