When Phoebus rose he left his golden weed,
And dond attire in deepest pulple died,
His sanguine beams about his forhead spred,
A sad presage of ill that should betide,
•ith vermile drops at even his tresses bleed
•or shows of future heat from th'Ocean wide.
•hilst thus he bent gainst earth his scorching rays,
He burnt the flowers, and burnt his Clitia deare,
The leaves grew wan upon the withered sprays,
The grass and growing herbs all parched were.
Earth cleft in rifts, in floods their streams decays,
The barren clouds with lightning bright appear,
And mankind feard least Clymens child again
Had driven away his Syers ill-guided wain.
As from a fornace flew the smoke to skies,
Such smoke as that when damned Sodome brent:
Within his Cave sweet Zephyre silent lies,
Still was the air, the rack nor came nor went,
But ore the lands with lukewarm breathing flies
The Southern wind, from sun-bright Affrique sent,
with thick and warm, his interrupted blasts,
Upon their bosoms, throats, and faces casts.
Nor yet more comfort brought the gloomy night,
In her thick shade was burning heat uprold,
Her sable mantle was imbrodered bright
with blazing stars and gliding fires of gold.
Nor to refresh sad earth thy thirsty spirit,
The niggard Moon let fall her May-dewes cold,
And dried up the vital moisture was
In trees, in plants, in herbs, in flowers, in grass.
