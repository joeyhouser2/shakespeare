—Mother of the living, second nature
Of th'elements, fire, water, earth and air:
The grace whereby men clime the heavenly chair,
Whence void, this world harbors no happy creature.
Piller of laws, religions pedestal,
Hope of the glory, glory of the immortal.
Honor of cities, pearl of kingdoms all,
The nurse of virtues, Muses chief supportall.
Patron of arts, of good the special spring.
Heavens sacred nymph, fair goddess that renuest
The golden age, and brightly now revewest,
Our cloudy sky, making our fields to smile,
Hope of the vertuous, horror of the vile.
Virgin unseen, in France this many a year,
O blessed peace, we bid thee welcome here.
O holy peace by thee are only found,
The passing joys that every where abound.
—Most sacred peace
Doth nourish vertue, and fast friendship breeds,
Weak she makes strong, and strong things does increase:
Till it the pitch of highest praise exceeds.
Brave be her wars, and honorable deeds,
By which she triumphs over ire and pride,
And wins an Olive garland for their meeds.
Peace doth depend on reason, war on force,
The one is humane, honest and upright:
The other brutish, fostered by despight.
The one extreme, concluded with remorse,
The other all injustice doth divorce.
Peace brings in pleasure, pleasure breeds excess,
Excess procureth want, want worse distress.
Distress contempt, contempt is not repaired,
Till liveless death determine hope despaired.
Wars greatest woes, and miseries increase,
Flows fron the surfeits which we take in peace.
