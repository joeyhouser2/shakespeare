From idle wit, there springs a brainsick will,
With wise men lust, which foolish make a God,
This in the shape of vertue reigneth still.
Will puts in practise what the wit deviseth.
Will ever acts, and wit contemplates still,
And as from wit the power of wisdom riseth,
All other virtues daughters are of will.
Will is the Prince, and wit the counsellor,
Which doth for common good in council sit,
And when wit is resolved, will lends her power,
To execute what is devised by wit.
Will is as free as any Emperor,
Nought can restrain her gentle liberty,
No tyrant nor no torrent hath the power
To make us will when we unwilling be.
Even as the will should goodness truely know,
We have a will which that true good should choose
Although will oft, when wit false forms doth show,
Take ill for good, and good for ill refuse.
It lives not in our power to love or hate,
For will in us is overruled by fate.
A stronger hand restrains our wilful powers,
A will must rule above this will of ours,
Not following what our vain desires do woe,
For virtues sake, but what we only do.
—Headless will true judgement doth ensnare.
Self-will doth frown, when honest zeal reproves,
Whereas our actions measure no regard,
Our lawless will is made his own reward.
—With a world of mischiefs and offence,
Unbridled will rebels against the sence.
He least should list that may do what he will.
