His face was lean and some deal pinde away,
And eke his hands consumed to the bone:
But what his body was I cannot say,
For on his carcase, raiment had he none.
Save clouts and patches pieced one by one,
With staff in hand, and scrip on shoulder cast,
His chief defence against the winters blast.
His food for most, was wild fruits of the tree,
Unless sometimes, some crumbs fell to his share,
Which in his wallet long, God-wot kept he,
As one the which full daintily would fare:
His drink the running stream his cup, the bare
Of his palm clozd, his bed the hard cold ground,
To this poor life, was Misery ybound.
— This Iron world
Brings down the stoutest hearts to lowest state,
For Misery doth bravest minds abate,
And makes them seek for that they wont to scorn,
Of fortune and of hope, at once forlorn.
—He hath a foolish fantasy,
That thinks to find a friend in misery.
O Misery, where once thou art possest,
How soon thy faint infection alters kind,
And like a Circe, turnest man to beast,
And with the body dost transform the mind,
That can in fetters our affection bind.
— Misery is trodden on by many,
But being low, never relieved by any.
— The mightiest that have lived,
Have fallen and headlong too, in Misery,
It is some comfort to have company.
Men fly from foes, but not from Misery.
Let him that sees his private Misery
Avoid the prospect of prosperity:
It breeds pale envy, and sad discontent
Procures offence before a proffered wrong.
Torments it self till all conceits are spent,
And thoughts delivered by malicious tongue,
Then rapt with violent fury goes so strong,
That it enuenomes all our human parts,
Blind judging in eyes, and sence confounding hearts.
