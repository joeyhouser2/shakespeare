The Mind hath in it self a deity,
And in the stretching circle of her eye,
All things are compast, all things present still
Will framed to power, doth make us what we will.
It is the mind that maketh good or ill,
That makes a wretch, or happy, rich or poor,
For some that have a boundance at their will,
Have not inough, but want in greatest store.
An other that hath little, asks no more,
But in that little is both rich and wise.
The Mind is free what ere afflict the man,
A King's a King, do fortune what she can.
— The Mind times enemy, oblivions foe,
Disposer true of each note worthy thing.
Our minds discern where eyes could never see.
— That Mind most is beautiful and hie,
And nearest comes to a divinity,
That farthest is from spots of earthes delight,
Pleasures that lose their substance with their sight,
Such one Saturnius ravisheth to love,
And fills the cup of all content to Jove.
The settled mind is free from fortunes power,
They need not fear who look not up aloft:
But they that are too careful every hower,
For when they fall they light not very soft.
What plague is greater then the grief of mind?
The grief of mind that eats in every vain:
In every vain that leaves such clods behind,
Such clods behind as breed such bitter pain.
So bitter pain that none shall ever find
What plague is greater then the grief of mind.
Ill mind, to mind so much of others ill,
As to become unmindful of his own.
Into our minds let us a little fall,
And we shall find more spots then leopards have.
O vanity of mans unstable mind,
Puft up with every blast of friendly wind.
In base minds no friendship dwells, nor emnity.
Oft times we see that sorrows of the mind
Find remedy unsought, which seeking cannot find.
Weak body well is changed, for minds redoubled force.
So move our minds, as motions move the air.
Nor is it his our minds that make our native homes our grave,
As we to ours, others to theirs, like partial fancy have.
Transmut we but our minds, and then all one an alien is,
As if a native once resolved, makes every country his.
