Physche in stedfast love and happy state
With Cupid lives, and hath him borne a child,
Pleasure that doth both Gods and men aggrate.
Most easy is the way and passage plain,
To Pleasures palace; it may soon be spied,
And day and night her doors to all stand open wide.
Her face was wan, a lean and withered skin,
Her stature scant three horsloaves did exceed:
Her hair was gray of hue, and very thin,
Her teeth were gone, her gums served in their steed.
No space there was betweene her nose and chin.
Her noisome breath contagion would breed.
In fine, of her it might have well bene said,
In Nestors youth she was a pretty maid.
O poisoned hook that lurks in sugared bait
O Pleasures vain, that in this world are found:
Which like a subtle thief do lie in wait
To swallow man in sink of sin profound.
—Revels, dances, masks and merry hours,
Forerun fair love, strowing her way with flowers.
O Pleasure thou the very lure of sin,
The root of woe, our youths deceitful guide:
A shop where all infected persons bin,
The bait of lust, the instrument of pride.
Inchaunting Circes smoothing covert guile,
Alluring Syren, flattering Crocodile.
Pleasures be poor, and our delights be dead,
When as a man doth not enjoy the head.
Never have unjust pleasures bene compleat
In joys intire; but still fear kept the door:
And held back something from that hell of sweet,
To inter sowre unsure delights the more.
For never did all circumstances meet
With those desires which were conceived before.
Something must still be left to cheare our sin,
And give a touch of what should not have bin.
Pleasure is felt, opinion but conceived.
In fear her arts are learned now a days,
To counterfait their hair and paint their skin:
But reasons ring their craft and guile bewrays,
No wise men of their paintings pass a pin.
Too much desire to please, pleasure divorces,
Attempts, and not intreat, get Ladies largess.
Our fond preferments are but childrens toys,
And as a shadow all our pleasures pass:
As years increase, so waining are our joys,
And beauty crazed like a broken glass,
A pretty tale of that which never was.
—Pleasures never dine but on excess,
Whose diet made to draw on all delight:
And overcome in that sweet drunkenness,
His appetite maintained by his sight,
Strengtheneth desire, but ever weakeneth might.
Until this ulcer ripening to an head,
Vomits the poison which it nourished.
Short hours work long effects minutes have change,
While pleasure joyeth, pain more ripe doth grow.
The secret sweet is sweetest, sweet to fall.
—To them that know not pleasures price,
Alls one, a prison, or a paradise.
