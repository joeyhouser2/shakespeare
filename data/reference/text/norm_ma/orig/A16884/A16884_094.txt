Kings are the Gods vicegerents on the earth,
The Gods have power, Kings from that power have might:
Kings should excel in vertue and in birth:
Gods punish wrongs, and kings should maintain right,
They be the suns from which we borrow light,
And they as Kings, should still in justice strive
With Gods, from whom their beings they derive.
The baser is he coming from a King,
To shame his hopes with deeds degenerate:
The mightier man, the mightier is the thing,
That makes him honoured, or begets him hate:
For greater scandal waits on greater state.
The Moon being clouded, presently is mist,
But little stars may hide them where they list.
The Crow may bathe his coalblack wing in mire,
And unperceived, fly with the filth away,
But if she like the snow white swan desire,
The stain upon his silver down will stay,
Poor grooms are sightless nights, kings glorious day.
Gnats are unnoted wheresoever they fly,
But Eagles are gazed upon with every eye.
— Since the heavens strong arms teach Kings, to stand,
Angels are placed about the glorious throne,
To gard it from the strokes of traitrous hand.
When thou becomest an earthly God, mens faults to oversee,
Forget not that eternal God, that overlooketh thee.
The least part of a King is allowing him, and none
Less private then a Prince, the weal or woe of every one.
He and his people make but one, a body, weak or strong,
As doth the head, the limbs, or limbs the head assist, or wrong.
Kings, Lords of times and of occasions,
May take th'aduantage when and how they list.
Kings will be alone, Competitors must down,
Near death he stands, that stands to wear a crown.
— It is a dangerous thing
In rule of love, but once to cross a King.
Endless cares concur with crowns, a bitter sweeting is raign.
Not all the water in the rough rude sea
Can wash the balm from an anointed King:
The breath of worldly men cannot depose
The deputy elected by the Lord.
He knows not what it is to be a King,
That thinks a Scepter is a pleasant thing.
A glittering Crown doth make the hair soon gray,
Within whose circle, a king is but arrested,
In all his feasts he's but with sorrow feasted,
And when his feet disdain to touch the mold,
His head's a prisoner in a Jail of Gold.
Unhappy Kings that never can be taught,
To know themselves, or to discern their fault.
— No outrageous thing
From vassal actors can be wipte away,
The Kings misdeeds can not be hid in clay.
No Scepter serves dishonour to excuse,
Nor kingly vaile can cover villainy.
Fame is not subject to authority.
—Think not but Kings are men, and as the rest miscarry,
Save that their fame and infamy continually doth tarry.
Kings want no means to accomplish what they will,
Mislikes are silly lets where Kings resolve them,
Where counsel chasing will hath empery,
Deeds are too prest for reason to dissolve them,
In mighty minds a grounded vanity.
Like springs that ceassesse never stoppeth,
Until her neighbour Oak she overtoppeth.
—Great men too well graced, much rigor use,
Presuming favorites mischief ever bring:
So that concluding, I may boldly speak,
Minions too great, argue a king too weak.
New kings do fear when old kings farther strain,
Established state to all things will consent.
—Good from kings must not be drawn by force.
A Scepter like a pillar of great height,
Whereon a mighty building doth depend:
Which when the same is over-prest with waight,
And past his compass forced thereby to bend.
His massy roof down to the ground doth send.
Crushing the lesser part, and murdering all
Which stand within the compass of his fall.
Too true that tyrant Dyonisyus
Did picture out the image of a king:
When Damocles was placed in his throne,
And ore his head a threatening sword did hang,
Fastened up only by a horses hair.
