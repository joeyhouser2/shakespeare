The sailing Pine, the Cedar proud and tall,
The Vine-prop Elm, the Poplar never dry,
The builder Oake, sole king of Forests all,
The Aspine good for staves, the Cypress funeral.
The Laurel meed of mighty conquerors,
And Poets sage, the Firrhe that weepeth still,
The Willow worn of forlorn paramours.
The Eughe obedient to the benders will,
The Birch for shafts, the Sallow for the mil.
The Myrrh sweet bleeding in the bitter wound,
The warlike Beech, the Ash for nothing ill.
The fruitful Olive, and the Platane round,
The carved Holm, the Maple seldom inward sound.
Downe came the sacred Palms, the Ashes wild,
The funeral Cypress, Holly ever green:
The weeping Fir, thick Beech, and sailing Pine,
The married Elm fell with his fruitful Vine.
The shooter Eughe, the broad leaved Sycamore,
The barren Plataine, and the Walnut found,
The Myrrh that her fowl sin doth still deplore:
The Alder owner of all waterish ground,
Sweet Juniper whose shadow hurteth sore,
Proud Cedar, Oak, the king of Forests crowned.
Behold fond Boy this Rozen weeping Pine,
This mournful Larix, dropping Turpentine.
This mounting Teda, thus with tempests torn,
With Inky tears continually to mourn.
Alcides speckled Poplar tree,
The Palms that Monarchs do obtain,
With love-ivice stained the Mulberry,
The fruit that dews the Poets brain.
And Phillis Philbert there away,
Compared with Myrtle and the Bay.
The tree that Coffins doth adorn,
With stately height threatening the sky,
And for the bed of love forlorn.
The black and doleful Ebony.
All in a circle compact are,
Like to an Amphitheater.
The Spartan Myrtle whence sweet gums do flow,
The purple Hyacinth and fresh Costmary,
And Saffron sought, for in Cicilian soil,
Laurel, the ornament of Phaebus toil.
Fresh Rododaphne and the Sabine flower,
Matching the wealth of the ancient Frankincense:
And pallid Ivy building his own bower,
And Box yet mindful of his old offence:
Red Amaranthus luckless paramour:
Oxeye still green and bitter patience.
Ne wants there pale Narcisse, that in a well
Seeing his beauty, in love with it fell.
Myrtle's due to Venus, green Laurel due to Apollo,
Corn to the lady Ceres, ripe grapes to the yong merry Bacchus.
Poplar to Alcides, and Olives unto Minerua▪
Gentle Amaranthus thou fairest flower of a thousand,
Shalt be loves flower henceforth, thogh thou camest fron a bleeding,
Yet blood shalt thou stanch, this gift will I give thee for ever.
Dead-sleeping Poppy and black Hellebore,
Cold Coloquintida, and Tetra mad,
Mortal Samnites and Cicuta bade,
With which th'uniust Athenians made to die,
Wise Socrates who thereof quaffing glad,
Poured out his life and last Philosophy.
To the fair Critias his dearest Belamye.
The wholesome Sage, and Lavender still gray,
Ranke-smelling Rue, and Comin good for eyes:
The Roses reigning in the pride of May,
Sharp Isope good for green wounds remedies.
Fair Marygolds and Bees alluring Thime,
Sweet Marioram and Daisies decking prime.
Cool Violets and Orpin growing still,
Embathed Balm, and cheerful Galingale,
Fresh Costmary, and breathful Camomile,
Dull Poppey, and drinke-quickning Setnale,
Veine-healing Vervin, and head-purging Dill,
Sound Savory, and Bazill harry hale.
Fat Coleworts, and comforting Perseline,
Cold Lettuce, and refreshing Rosmarine.
A soft enflowred bank imbrac'd the fount
Of Chloris ensigns, an abstracted field:
Where grew Melanthy, great in Bees account,
Amareus that precious balm doth yield.
Enameled Pansies, used at nuptials still,
Dianaes arrow, Cupids crimson shield:
Ope-morne, Night-shade, and Venus Navel.
Sollem Violets hanging heads as shamed,
And Verdant Calaminth for Odour famed.
Sacred Nepenthe purgative of care,
And sovereign Ruberb that doth rancor kill.
Sia and Hyacinth that Furies wear,
White and red Jessamines, merry Melliphill,
Fair crown, imperial emperor of flowers,
Immortal Amaranth, white Aphrodil,
And cuplike twill pants strewed in Bacchus bowers.
The Marigold Phaebus beloved friend,
The Moly which from sorcery doth defend.
