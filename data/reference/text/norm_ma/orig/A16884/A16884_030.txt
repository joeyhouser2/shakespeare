—To Courage great
It is no less beseeming well to bear,
The storm of fortunes frown, or heavens threat,
Then in the sunshine of her countenance clear,
Timely to joy and carry comely cheare.
High Courage with true wisdom ever backed,
Wins perfect fame.
Nere was there ever noble courage seen,
That in advantage would his puissance boast,
Honor is least where ods appeareth most.
Where is no courage, there is no ruth nor mone.
Good hart in ill, doth th' evil much amend.
Courage imboldneth wit, wit courage arms.
They make their fortune who are stout and wise,
Wit rules the heavens, discretion guides the skies.
Action is fiery valou•s soverainge good.
No foot to foe Repining courage yields.
Then are the valiant who more vain, then cowards who more wise,
Not men that travel Pegasus, but fortunes fools do rise?
Be valiant, not too venturous, but fight to fight again,
Even Hercules did hold it ods, for one to strive with twaine.
Might wanting measure, moveth surquedry.
—More is he that ventureth for more,
Then who fights but for what he had before.
—Valour mixt with fear, boldeneth dread,
May march more circumspect with better heed.
Valour in greatest dangers shows most bright.
As full-faced Phoebe in the darkest night.
The Spartanes once exiled Archilochus,
The Author of Lycambes Tragedy,
Because he said it was commodious,
Rather to cast away his shield and fly,
Then boldly to resist, and bravely die.
