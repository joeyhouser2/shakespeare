Ambition is a Vulture vile,
That feedeth on the heart of pride,
And finds no rest when all is tride.
For worlds cannot confine the one,
Th'other lists and bounds hath none.
And both subvert the mind, the state
Procure destruction, envy, hate.
Ambition, fie upon thy painted cheek,
(Woe worth the beauty sleeps not with the face)
For thou art hateful, foul, unfair, unmeet:
A poison-painted pleasure▪ mads men chase.
Thou reasonless desire that makes men seek
To kiss the same, whilest fire doth thee imbrace.
Thou only strong disordered, rulest passion,
Thou marst mens minds, and pu•st them out of fashion.
The golden chain of Homers hie devise
Ambition is, or cursed avarice:
Which all gods haling being tied to Jove
Him from his settled height could never move.
Intending this, that though that powerful chain
Of most Herculean vigor to constrain
Men from true vertue, or their present states,
Attempt a man, that manless changes hates:
And is ennobled with a deathless love,
Of things eternal dignified above:
Nothing shall stir him from adorning still,
This shape with vertue and his power with will.
—The greedy thirst of royal crown
That knows no kindred, nor regards no right,
Stirred Porrex up, to pluck his brother down:
Who unto him assembling forraine might,
Made war on him, and fell himself in fight:
Whose death to avenge his mother merciless
Most merciless of women, Eden hight:
Her other son fast sleeping did oppress,
And with most cruel hand him murdered pitiless.
A diadem once dazzling the eye,
The day too dark, to see affinity:
And where the arm is stretched to reach a crown,
Friendship is broke, the dearest things thrown down.
—Realme-rape, spareth neither kin nor friend.
Who fight for crowns, set life, set all to light,
Who aim so hie, will die, or hit the white.
One riseth by an others fall, and some do clime so fast,
That in the clouds they do forget what climates they have past
The Eagle minded minds that nestle in the sun,
Their lofty heads have leaden heals, and end where they begun.
O, fatal is the ascent unto a crown,
From whence men come not down, but tumble down
Like as the heaven two Suns cannot contain,
So in the earth two Kings cannot remain
Of equal state: so doth Ambition crave,
One King will not another equal have.
Whom so the minds unquiet state upheaves,
Be it for love or fear; when fancy reaves
Reason her right, by mocking of the wit,
If once the cause of this affection flit,
Reason prevailing on the unbridled thought,
Down falls he, who by fancy climbed aloft.
Desire of rule within a climbing brest
To break a vow, may bear the buckler best.
In some courts shall you see Ambition
Sit peecing Dedalus old waxen wings:
But being clapt on and they about to fly,
Even when their hopes are busied in the clouds
They melt against the sun of majesty,
And down they tumble to destruction.
Better sit still men say then rise and fall.
High state the bed wherein misfortune lies.
Mars most unfriendly, when most kind he seems:
Who climeth hie on earth, he hardest lights,
And lowest falls attends the highest flights.
As highest hills with tempest be most touched,
And tops of trees most subject unto wind,
And as great Towers with stone strongly couched,
Have many falls when they be undermined,
Even so by proof in worldly things we find,
That such as climb the top of hie degree,
From fear of falling never can be free.
Ambition with the Eagle loves to build,
Nor on the mountain dreads the winter blast:
But with self soothing doth the humour guild
With arguments, correcting what is past.
Forecasting kingdoms, dangers unforecast:
Leaving this poor word of content to such,
Whose earthly spirits have not fiery tuch.
—The ambitious once inured to reign,
Can never brook a private state again.
—Warlike Caesar tempted with the name
Of this sweet Island never conquered,
And envying the Britons blazed fame,
(O hideous hunger of dominion) hither came.
—Ambition
In princely palace and in stately towns,
Doth often creep, and close within conveys
(To leave behind it) damage and decays:
By it be love and amity destroyed.
It breaks the laws, and common concord beats.
Kingdoms and realms it topsy turuy turns.
Be not ambitiously a king, nor grudgingly decline,
One God did root out Cis his stock, and raise up Jesses line.
The aspirer once attained unto the top,
Cuts off those means by which himself got up.
Haughty Ambition makes a breach in hills,
Runs dry by sea amongst the raging scills.
