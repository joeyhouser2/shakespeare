—Sacred Beauty is the fruit of sight,
That courtesy that speaks before the tongue:
The feast of souls, the glory of the light,
Envy of age, and everlasting yoong:
Pities commander, Cupids richest throne,
Music entranced, never duly sung:
The sum and court of all proportion.
And that I may dull speeches least afford,
All Rhethorickes Flowers, in less then in a word.
— Beauty borne of heavenly race.
Beauty (daughter of marvel) ô see how
Thou canst disgracing sorrows sweetly grace,
What power thou shewest in a distressed brow,
That makest affliction fair givest tears their grace.
What? can untressed locks, can torn rent hair?
A weeping eye, a wailing face be fair?
I see then artless feature can content,
And that true Beauty needs no ornament.
— Beauty is the bait which with delight
Doth man allure for to enlarge his kind,
Beauty the burning lamp of heavens light,
Darting her beams into each feeble mind,
Against whose power, nor God nor man can find
Defence, reward, the danger of the wound:
•ut being hurt, seek to be medicinde,
Of her that first did stir that mortal wound.
— Beauty is womans golden crown,
Mans conqueress and feminine renown:
•ot joined with love, who deare yet ever sold it?
•or beauties cheap, except loves eye behold it.
— Beauty is an adamant to all.
•ewtie, natures Ivy-bush each passenger doth call.
•eldome wants guests where Beauty bids the feast,
•ens eyes with wonders nere are satisfied,
At fairest signs best welcome is surmised,
The shrine of love doth seldom offering want,
Nor with such counsel, clients never scant.
All Orators are dumb where Beauty pleadeth.
Beauty it self doth of it self persuade
The eyes of men, without an Orator?
What needeth then Apologies be made,
To set forth that which is so singular?
Nought under heaven so strongly doth allure
The sense of man, and all his mind possess,
As Beauties lovely bate that doth procure
Great warriors oft their rigor to repress,
And mighty hands forget their manliness.
Driven with the power of an heart-robbing eye,
And wrapt in flowers of a golden tress.
That can with melting pleasance mollify,
Their hardened hearts enured to blood and cruelty.
O how can beauty maister the most strong,
And simple truth subdue avenging wrong?
No armour can be found that can defend,
Transpercing rays of christall pointed eyes.
Hard is that heart which Beauty makes not soft.
— Who so young that loves not?
Or who so old that womens Beauty moves not?
A spark of Beauty burns a world of men.
O what is Beauty if it be not seen?
Or what is't to be seen and not admired,
And though admired, unless in love desired.
Never were cheeks of Roses locks of Amber,
Ordained to live imprisoned in a Chamber.
Nature created Beauty for the view,
(Like as the fire for heat, the Sun for light)
The fair do ever hold this pledge as due,
By ancient charter to live most in sight,
As she that is debarred it, hath not right:
In vain our friends from this doe us dehort,
For Beauty will be where is most resort.
All excellence of shape is made for sight,
To be a beetle else were no defame:
Hid Beauties lose their ends, and wrong their right.
Heaven made beauty like her self to view,
Not to be leapt up in a smoky mewe:
A rosy tainted feature is heavens gold,
Whil'st all men joy to touch, all to behold.
The ripest corn dies if it be not reaped,
Beauty alone is lost too early kept.
It hath bene through all ages ever seen,
That with the praise of arms and chivalry,
The praise of Beauty still hath joined been,
And that for reasons special privity,
For either doth on other much rely,
For he me seems most fittest is to serve,
That can her best defend from villainy,
And she most fit his service doth deserve,
That fairest is, and from her faith doth never swerve.
— Beauty is more bright and clear.
The more it is admired of many a wight,
And noblest she that served is of noble Knight.
Rich Beauty, that each Lover labours for,
Tempting as heaps of new coined glowing Gold,
(Rackt of some miserable treasurer,)
Draws his desires, and them in chains enfold,
Urging him still to tell it and conceal it:
But Beauties treasure never can be told,
None can peculiar joy, yet all must steal it.
O Beauty, this same bloody siege of thine,
Starves me that yield, and feeds me till I pine.
O Beauty, still thy Empire swims in blood,
And in thy peace, war stores himself with food.
O Beauty Syrene fair enchanting good,
Sweet silent Rhethoricke of persuading eyes:
Dumb eloquence, whose power doth move the blood
More then the works, or wisdom of the wise.
Still harmony whose Diapazon lies
Within a brow, the key which passions move
To ravish the sence and play a world in love.
Beauty enchasing love, love gaining Beauty,
To such as conflict Sympathies enfold:
To perfect riches doth a sounder duty
Then all endeavours, for by all consent
All wealth and wisdom rests in time content.
More force and art is beauty joined with love,
Then thrones with wisdom, joys of them composed,
Are arms more proof gainst any grief we prove:
Then all their vertue scorning misery,
Or judgements grave in stoic gravity.
Beauty a beggar, fieit is too bad
When in it self sufficiency is had:
It was not made to please the wandering eye,
But an attire to adorn sweet modesty.
If modesty and women once do sever,
Farewell our fame, farewell our name for ever.
O Beauty that betrays thy self to every amorous eye,
To trap thy proud professors, what is it but wantons try?
V Uhere through it sildom haps the fair from mean deceits to fly.
This Beauty fair, is an inchauntment made
By natures witchcraft, tempting men to buy
With endless shows, what endlessly will fade,
Yet promise chapmen all eternity.
But like to goods ill got a fault it hath,
Brings men inricht therewith to beggary,
Unless the enricher be as rich in faith
Enamoured, (like god selflove) with her own
Seen in an other then tis heaven alone.
—Beauty is a baine
To such as feed their fancy with fond love,
That when sweet youth with lust is overthrown,
It rues in age.
Where Venus strikes with Beauty to the quick,
It little vails safe reason to apply:
Few are the cares for such as are love sick,
But love.
Truce, war, and woe, do wait at Beauties gate,
Time lost, laments, reports and privy grudge:
And last, fierce love is but a partial judge,
Who yields for service shame, for friendship hate.
The bees of Hybla have besides sweet honey smarting stings,
And beauty doth not want a bait that to repentance brings.
—Fair colours soonest soil,
Things of best price are subject most to spoil.
The fairer cheek hath oftentimes a soul
Leprous as sin it self, then hell more foul.
All men do err, because that men they be,
And men with Beauty blinded, cannot see.
Beauty, heaven and earth this grace doth win,
It supples rigor, and it lessons sin.
Nought is under heavens wide hollowness,
That moves more deare compassion of mind:
Then Beauty to unworthy wretchedness
Through envies snares, or fortunes freaks unkind.
—Nothing ill becomes the fair,
But cruelty which yields unto no prayer.
Like as the Sun in a Diameter
Fires and inflames objects removed far,
And heateth kindly, shining laterally,
So Beauty sweetly quickens when tis nie:
But being separated and removed,
Burns where it's cherished, murders where it loved.
Simples fit Beauty, fie on drugs and art.
—Fair words and powre-attractive beauty,
Bring men to want on in subjective duty.
—Wayward Beauty doth not fancy move.
A frown forbids, a smile ingendreth love.
—What else is form, but fading air?
Yea oft, because assaulted of, it hurteth to be fair.
Full soon the fairest face would cease from being such,
If not preserved curiously from tendering more then much
That wondrous pattern where soever it be
Whether in earth laid up in secret store,
Or else in heaven that no man may it see
With sinful eyes, for fear it to deflore:
Is perfect Beauty which all men adore.
Whose face and feature doth so far excel
All mortal sence, that none the same may tell.
O Beauty, how attractive is thy power?
For as the lives heat clings about the hart,
So all mens hungry eyes do haunt thy bower:
Reigning in Greece, Troy swum to thee in art.
Removed to Troy, Greece followed thee in fears,
Thou drewest each syrelesse sword, each childless dart
And puldst the Towers of Troy about thine ears.
