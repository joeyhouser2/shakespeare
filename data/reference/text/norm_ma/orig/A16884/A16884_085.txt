At last with creeping crooked pace forth came
An old old man, with beard as white as snow:
That on a staff his feeble limbs did frame,
And guide his weary gate both too and fro.
For his eye sight him failed long ago,
And on his arm a bunch of keys he bore,
The which unvsed, rust did overgrow.
But very uncouth sight was to behold
How he did fashion his untoward pace:
For a• he forward moved his footing old,
So backward still was turned his wrinkled face•
Unlike to men who ever as they trace
Both feet and face one way are wont to lead,
His name Ignaro, did his nature right aread.
Image of hellish horror, Ignorance,
Borne in the bosom of the black abyss,
And fed with furies milk for sustenance,
Of his weak infancy begot amiss:
By gnawing sloth, upon his mother night,
So he his sons, both Sire and brother hight.
—All is turned into wilderness,
Whilest Ignorance the Muses doth oppress.
—Hell and darkness and the grisly grave,
Is Ignorance, the enemy of grace:
That minds of men borne heavenly, doth deface.
Tis nought but shows that Ignorance esteems
The thing possest, is not the thing it seems.
—Great ill upon desert doth chance,
When it doth pass by beastly Ignorance.
