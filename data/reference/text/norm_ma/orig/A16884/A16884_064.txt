The gentle mind by gentle deeds is known,
For a man by nothing is so well bewrayed,
As by his manners, in which plain is shown,
Of what degree, and what race he is grown.
Sweet gentleness is Beauties waiting maid.
— In gentle thoughts,
Relenting thoughts, remorse, and pity rests.
All like as Phoebus, with his cheerful beams,
Doth freshly force the fragrant flowers to flourish,
So gentle rulers subjects love do nourish.
Like as the gentle heart it self bewrays,
In doing gentle deeds with frank delight:
Even so the baser mind it self displays,
In cankered malice, and revenge for spight.
O what an easy thing is't to descry
The gentle blood, how ever it be wrapt,
In sad misfortunes foul deformity
And wretched sorrows which have often hapt.
For howsoever it may grow mishapped,
That to all vertue it may seem unapt,
Yet will it shew some sparks of gentle mind,
And at the last, break forth in his own proper kind.
