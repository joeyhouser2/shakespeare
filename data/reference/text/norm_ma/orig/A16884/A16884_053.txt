What God hath said, that cannot but ensue,
Though all the world would have it overthrown:
When men suppose by fetches of their own
To fly their Fate, they further on the same,
Like blasts of wind, which oft revive the flame.
The heavens do rule in their continual course,
That yields to Fate, that doth not yield to force.
Chance is uncertain, fortune double faced.
—Demogorgon ruler of the Fates.
—The Fates can make
Way for themselves, their purpose to pertake.
—What the Fates do once decree,
Not all the gods can change, nor Jove himself can free.
—The laws of Fate
Being graven in steel, must stand inviolate.
Who can escape what his own Fate hath wrought,
The work of heavens will, surpass all humane thought.
—Who can deceive his destiny?
Or ween by warning to avoid his Fate?
That when he sleeps in more security
And safest seems, him soonest doth amate,
And findeth due effect, or soon or late
So feeble is the power of fleshly arm.
—Indeed the Fates are firm,
And may not shrink though all the word do shake:
Yet ought mens good endeavours them confirm,
And guide the heavenly causes to their constant term.
Each man they say his Fate hath in his hands,
And what he makes or marres to leese or save,
Of good or evil, is even self do, self have.
The Fates far off, foreseen come gently near.
Our Fate is not prevented though foreknown,
For that must hap decreed by heavenly powers,
Who work our fall, yet make the fault still ours.
—Fate
Keeps in eternal dark our fortunes hidden,
And ere they come to know them, tis forbidden.
All men are men in ignorance of Fate,
To alter chance, exceedeth humane state.
The heavens do rule in their continual course,
That yields to Fate, that doth not yield to force.
