—Thou sweet Music, dancings only life,
The ears sole happiness, the airs best speech:
Loadstone of fellowship, charming rod of strife,
The soft minds paradise, the sick mans leech.
With their own tongue that trees and stones canst teach.
That when the air doth dance her finest measure,
Then art thou borne, the gods and mens sweet pleasure.
As without breath no pipe doth move,
No Music kindly without love.
Esclepiad did cure with Trumpets sound,
Such men as first had lost their hearing quite:
And many such as in their drink lay drownd,
Damon revived with tunes of grave delight.
And Theophrast when ought his mind opprest,
Used Music sound to bring himself to rest.
With sound of Harp Thales did make recure
Of such as lay with pestilence forlorn:
With Organ pipes Xenocrates made pure
Their wits, whose minds long lunacy had worn.
Some that report great Alexanders life,
They say that harmony so moved his mind:
That oft he rose from meat to warlike strife,
At sound of Trompe, or noise of battle kind.
And then that Musics force of softer vain,
Caused him return from strokes to meat again.
