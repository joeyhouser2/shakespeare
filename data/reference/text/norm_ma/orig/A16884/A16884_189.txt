What time the native Belman of the night,
The bird that warned Peter of his fall,
First rings his silver bel to each sleeping wight,
That should their minds up to devotion call.
The cheerful cock, the sad nights trumpeter,
Waiting upon the rising of the sun,
Doth sing to see how Cynthia shrinks her horn,
Where Clitia takes her progress to the East,
Where wringing west with drops of silver dew,
Her wonted tears of love she doth renew,
The wandering swallow with her broken song,
The country wench unto her work awakes,
Whilst Cytherea sighing, walks to seek,
Her murdered love transformed to a rose,
Whom though she see, to crop she kindly fears
But kissing sighs, and dewes him with her tears.
Now ere the purple dawning yet did spring,
The joyful Lark began to stretch her wing,
And now the cock the mornings trumpeter,
