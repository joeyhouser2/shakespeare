—In Athence
The custom was, that every maid did wear
During her maidenhead, a silken sphere:
About her waste above her inmost weed
Knit with Mineruaes knot, and that was freed
By the fair bridegroom on the marriage night,
With many ceremonies of delight.
Shouldst thou but dream what marriage is, thou wouldst not live a maid,
One hart of two, two souls of one, by wedlock is conveyed.
Beleeve me man, there is no greater bliss,
Then is the quiet joy of loving wife:
Which who so wants, half of himself doth miss.
Friend without change, playfellow without strife.
Food without fullness, counsel without pride,
Is this sweet doubling of our single life.
In choice of wife, prefer the modest chaste,
Lilies are fair in shew, but foul in smell:
The sweetest looks by age are soon defaste,
Then choose thy wife by wit and loving well.
Who brings thee wealth, and many faults withal,
Presents thee honey mixt with bitter gall.
Wild savages that drinks of running springs,
Thinks water fair, exceeds all other things.

Virginity, all be some highly prise it,

One is no number, maids are nothing then

—Marriage will soon destroy
Those passions which to youthful head do clime,
Mothers and nurses of all vain annoy.
—Wretched wedlock breeds but hated heat,
Where no love seems so sweet, as stolen and secret.
Offer no love rights, but let wives still seek them,
For when they come unsought, they sildom like them.
—Even as Adam wrote his overthrow
By tasting fruit that God did him forbid,
So he that curiously will search to know
All that his wife hath said, or what she did,
May fortune at the last himself beshrew.
Let him that his wife to his bent will draw,
Match with a virgin and keep her in awe.
To love, and wed for love, is perfit bliss.
His be the hurt that looks not ere he wed.
The husband may the woman make or mar.
We are not male nor female borne, that we should fruitless die.
—Experience bidding us, doth bid us lay to thrive.
The first degree to which say some, is warily to wine:
But wife if shrow or saint become (as not unlike) a shrow,
Then is that first degree to thrift, the third degree in woe.
Let nothing severe those whom God doth link.
—The chance that once befell
To wandering Dina, may be witness well
That secret marriage that to few is kend,
Doth never lead the lovers to good end.
For of our bodies we no power may claim,
Except our parents do confirm the same.
We worldly folks account him very wise,
That hath the wit most worthily to wed,
By all means therefore, always we devise
To see our issue rich in spousal fed,
We buy and sell rich Orphans; babes scant bred
Must match, ere they do know what marriage means:
Boys marry old trots, old fools wed yoong queans.
We call this wedding, which in any wise
Can be no marriage, but pollution plain:
A new found trade of human merchandise,
The devils net, a filthy fleshly gain,
Of kind and nature, an unnatural stain:
A foul abuse of Gods most holy order,
And yet allowed almost in every border.
A filthy trull, is irksome to the eye,
A gallant girl allures the lookers mind:
A wanton wench will have the head to die,
An aged trot to like, is hard to find.
A bearing wife with brats, will cloy the store,
A greater care then childrens care is none:
A barren beast will grieve thee ten times more,
No joy remains when sap of fruit is gone.
Wherefore let wiving go live single aye,
A shrew we see is wedded on a day,
But ere a man can shift his hands tis long.
