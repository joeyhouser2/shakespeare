Like as two mastiff dogs with hungry jaws,
Moved first to hate, from hate to raging ire:
Approach with grinning teeth and grisly jaws,
With staring eyes as red as flaming fire.
At last they bite and scratch with teeth and claws,
Tearing themselves, and trembling in the mire.
So after biting and reproachful words,
Sarcapant and Rinaldo drew their swords.
Like as an exhalation hot and dry,
Amongst the air bred moisty vapours thrown
Spilleth his lightening forth courageously,
Renting the thick clouds with a thunder-stone,
As though the huge all covering heaven did grone.
Such is the garboil of this conflict then,
Brave English men encountering English men.
Like as ye see the wallowing sea to strive
Flood after flood, and wave with wave to drive:
Then waves with waves, then floods with floods to chace,
And eft returns unto their former place.
Or like the crops of corn in midst of May,
(Blown up with western wind) aside do sway,
Both too and fro as force doth them constrain,
And yet their tops redresseth up again.
So whiles the Sirians are by Medes displaced,
And whiles the Medes by Syrians are rechaced.
Like as a puttock having spied in sight
A gentle faulchon sitting on a hill,
Whose other wing now made unmeet for flight,
Was lately broken by some fortune ill.
The foolish kite led with licentious will,
Doth beat upon the gentle bird in vain,
With many idle stoops her troubling still.
Even so did Radagond with bootless pain,
Annoy this noble knight, and sorely him constrain.
Even as an Eagle that espies from hie,
Among the herbs a party coloured snake:
Or on a bank sunning her self to lie,
Casting the elder skin, anew to make
Lies hovering warily till she may spy
Advantage sure the venomed worm to take:
Then takes him by the back and beats her wings,
Maugre the poison of his forked stings,
So doth Rogero both with sword and spear,
The cruel monster warily assail.
Like as a mountain or a cape of land,
Assailed with storms, and sails on every side
Doth unremoved stedfast still withstand,
Storm, thunder, lightning, tempest, wind and tide.
The Souldan so withstood Latinus band.
So thick flew floods and darts that no man sees
The azure heavens, the sun his brightness lost:
The clouds of weapons like to swarms of bees
Met in the air, and there each other crost.
And look how falling leaves drop down from trees
When the moist sap is mixt with lively frost,
Or apples in strong windes from branches fall,
The Sarazens so tumbled from the wall.
As when a windy tempest bloweth hie,
That nothing may withstand his stormy stowre,
The clouds as things afraid before him fly,
But all so soon as his outrageous power
Is laid, they fairly then begin to shower,
And as in storm of his spent stormy spight,
Now all at once their malice forth do pour.
So did Sir Guion bear himself in fight,
And suffered rash Pirrocles want his idle might.
As Eagle fresh out of the Ocean wave,
Where he hath left his plumes all hoary gray,
And decked himself with feathers youthly gay,
Like Eyas hauke upmounting to the skies,
His newly budded pinions to assay,
And marvels at himself still as he flies,
So new, this newborn knight to battle did arise.
As gentle shepherd in sweet eventide,
When ruddy Phoebus gins to walk in west,
He on an hill his flock to viewen wide,
Marks which do bite his hearty supper best.
A cloud of combrous gnats do him molest,
All striving to infix their feeble stings,
That from their noyance, he no where can rest,
But with his clownish hands their tender wings
He brusheth off, and oft doth mar their murmurings.
—They him espying, both with greedy force
At once upon him ran, and him beset
With strokes of mortal steel, without remorse,
And on his shield like Iron sledges bet,
As when a Bear and Tigre being met,
In cruel fight on Libicke Ocean wide,
Espied a traveller with feet surbet,
Whom they in equal pray hope to devide,
They stint their strife, and him assail on every side.
