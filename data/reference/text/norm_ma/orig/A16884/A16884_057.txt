Folly in youth is sin, in age is madness.
A greater sign of Folly is not shown,
Then trusting others force, distrust our own:
— Wicked men repine their sinnes to hear,
And Folly flings, if counsel touch him near.
Fair fools delight to be accounted wise.
Fools will find fault without the cause discerning.
And argue most of that they have no learning.
— There is a method, time, and place,
Which fools observing do commence, ere wise men have their grace.
Tis better be a fool then be a fox,
For Folly is rewarded and respected,
Where subtlety is hated and rejected.
— The foolish commons use
Obey them most, who doth them most abuse.
A witless fool may every man him gesse,
That leaves the more, and takes him to the less.
