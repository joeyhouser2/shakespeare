As one that saw in Aprill or in May
A pleasant garden full of fragrant flowers,
Then when the earth new clad in garments gay
Decks every wood and grove with pleasant bowers,
Coming again on some Decembers day,
And sees it mard with winters storms and showers,
So did the Court to Bradamant appear,
When as she saw Rogero was not there.
As gorgious Phaebus in his first uprise,
Discovering now his scarlet-coloured head,
By troublous motions of the lowering skies,
His glorious beams with fogs are overspread
So are his cheerful browes eclipsed with sorrow,
which cloud the shine of his youths smiling morrow
Like as when Phaebus darting forth his rayes,
Glydeth along the swelling Ocean streams,
And whilst one billow with another plays
Reflecteth back his bright translucent beams:
Such was the conflict then betwixt our eyes▪
Sending forth looks as tears do fall and rise.
Like to a vessel with a narrow vent,
Which is fild up with licour to the top,
Although the mouth be after downward bent,
Yet is it seen not to distil a drop;
Even thus our breast brimful with pensive care,
Stopping our tongues, with grief we silent are.
As the high Elm (when his deare Vine hath twind
Fast in her hundred arms and holds imbrast)
Bears down to earth his spouse and darling kind
If storm or cruel steel the tree down cast,
And her full grapes to nought doth bruise and grind,
Spoils his own leaves, faints, withers, dies at last,
And seems to mourn and die, not for his own,
But for the death of her that lies orethrowne:
So fell he mourning▪ mourning for the dame
Whom life and death had made for ever his.
As when a foggy mist hath overcast
The face of heaven, and the clear air ingrost,
The world in darkness dwells, till that at last
The watery South-wind from the Seaboard coast
Up blowing doth disperse the vapours lost,
And powers it self forth in a stormy shower:
So the fair Britomart having disclost
Her cloudy care into a wrathful stowre,
The midst of grief dissolved into vengeance pour.
— As a stroke given on the righter eye
Offends the left, even so by sympathy
Her husbands dolours made her hart unglad,
And Judiths sorrows made her husband sad.
