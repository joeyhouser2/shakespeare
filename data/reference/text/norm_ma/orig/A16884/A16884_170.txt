—Next to Tyranny
Comes wars, dissension, civil mutiny.
In greatest wants t'inflict the greatest woe,
This is the worst that tyranny can show.
Hell haleth tyrants down to death amain,
Was never yet, nor shall be cruel deed
Unquited left, but had as cruel meed.
— No tyrant commonly
Living ill can kindly die,
But either traitorously surprised
Doth coward poison quail their breath,
Or their people have devizde,
Or their Guard to seek their death.
It is an hell in hateful vassalage,
Under a tyrant to consume ones age,
A selfe-shaven Dennis, or an Nero fell,
Whose cursed Courts with blood and incest swell:
An Owl that flies the light of Parliaments
And state assemblies, jealous of th'intents
Of Private tongues, who for a pastime sets
His Peers at odds, and on their fury whets,
Who neither faith, honour, nor right respects.
