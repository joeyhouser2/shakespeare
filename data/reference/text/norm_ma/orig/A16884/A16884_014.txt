These days example hath deep written here
Deep written in my heart with yron pen,
That Bliss may not abide in state of mortal men.
Doth sorrow fret thy soul? ô direful spirit,
Doth pleasure feed thy heart? ô blessed man.
Hast thou bene happy once? ô heavy plight.
Are thy mishaps forepast? ô happy than:
Or hast thou bliss in eld? ô bliss too late:
But hast thou bliss in youth? ô sweet estate.
—Hard it is
To imitate a false and forged bliss,
Ill may a sad mind forge a merry face,
Nor hath constrained laughter any grace.
—Bliss not in height doth dwell.
—Quiet Bliss in no state lasteth long.
Assailed still by mischief many ways,
Whose spoiling battery glowing hote and strong,
No flowing wealth, no force nor wisdom stays.
Her smoakelesse powder, beaten soldiers slays.
By open force, foul mischief oft prevails:
By secret sleight, she sild her purpose fails.
Blessed the man that well can use his bliss.
We think no greater bliss, then such to be, as be we would,
When blessed none, but such as be, the same, as be they should▪
Our bliss consists not in possessions,
But in commanding our affections.
In virtues choice, and vices needful chace,
Far from our hearts for staining of our face.
