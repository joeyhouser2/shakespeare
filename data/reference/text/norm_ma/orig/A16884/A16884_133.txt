O policy scarce known in times that's past,
Or being known, yet least of most esteemed,
Thy providence most worthily shall last,
And in these latter days be better deemed.
— War, honour doth deserve,
Yet counsel in all Kingdoms policied,
Is far more worthy and more dignified:
For arms but in extremes do never serve,
To reconcile and punish such as swerve.
He that will gain what policy doth heed,
By Mercurie must deal, or never speed.
Grounded advice in danger seldom trips

Tis better far thy enemy to aband
Quite from thy bowers to a stranger soil,
Then he at home thee and thy country foil.
The head that deems to overtop the sky,
Shall perish in his humane policy.
How oft have watching policy devizde
A cunning clause which hath himself surprised?
How often hath lewd fraud bene set a flore
Of purpose that his goods might cut his throte?
Who builds on strength by policy is stript,
Who hurts his wit by wit, is soonest tript.
Endeavours politic take small effect,
That wants assistance from the heavenly word:
Beside fome help must wealth and state afford.
For judgement uttered by the mouth of want,
Is either partial or admired scant.
A Clergy man his calling much impairs,
To meddle with the politic affairs.
Though Marius could begin and make the fray,
Yet Scaurus policy deserves the bay.
Let Catulus with Pompey be compared,
Or witty Cicero with Cateline:
And to prevent with policy divine,
That which the other over-rashly darde,
Deserves such fame as may not be imparde.
Say military virtues do require
A valiant heart, great strength and constancy:
The self like gifts in civil policy,
Are requisite for such as do aspire
To gain renown by counsel for their hire.
A little harm done to a great good end,
For lawful policy remains inacted,
The poisonous simple sometime is compacted
In a pure compound; being so applied
His venom in effect is purified.
