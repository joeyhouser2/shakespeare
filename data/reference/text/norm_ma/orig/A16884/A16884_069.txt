I am that one, is, was, and aye shall be,
Who create all of nought, as pleaseth me:
I can destroy, I am the great and just,
The fair, the good, the holy one to trust:
Whose strong right hand this world hath set in frame.
I plague my foe, and graunt my servants grace,
All those that knowledge me, and all their race.
—How soever things in likelihood discent
In birth, life, death, our God is first, the middle and event.
And not what he can do he will, but what he will he can,
And that he do or do it not, behoves us not to scan.
God first made Angels bodiless pure minds,
Then other things, which mindless bodies be:
Last he made man the Horizon twixt both kinds,
In whom we do the worlds abtidgement see.
How fond is that man in his fantasy
Who thinks that Jove the maker of us all,
The Sun, the Moon the Stars celestial,
So that no leaf without his leave can fall.
Hath not in him omnipotence also,
To guide and govern all things here below.
— Heaven is his seat,
Th'earth his footstool, and the prison great.
Of Plutoes reign, where damned souls are shut,
Is of his anger evermore the but.
—Full hard it is to read aright
The secret meaning of the eternal might:
That rules mans ways, and rules the thoughts of living wight.
The man of earth sounds not the seas profound
Of Gods deep judgements, where there is no ground
Let soberr•esse be still thy wisdoms end,
Admiring that thou canst not comprehend.
Under his feet (subjected to his grace,
Sit nature, fortune, motion, time, and place.
—Is there care in heaven? is there love
In the heavenly spirits to these creatures base,
That may compassion of their evils move?
There is: else much more wretched were the case
Of men, then beasts; but ô the exceeding grace
Of highest God, that loves his creature so:
And all his works with mercy doth imbrace.
That blessed Angels he sends too and fro,
To serve to wicked man, to serve his wicked foe.
Our gracious God makes scant waight of displeasure,
And spreads his mercy without waight or measure,
The eternal power that guides the earthly frame,
And serves him with the instrument of heaven:
To call the earth, and summon up our shame:
By an edict from everlasting given,
Forbids mortality to search the same.
Where sence is blind, and wit of wit bereaven:
Terror must be our knowledge, fear our skill,
To admire his work, and tremble at his will.
—Howsoever things in likely hood discent,
In birth life, death, our god is first, the middle and event
And not what he can do he will, but what he will he can,
And that he do or do it not, behoves us not to scan.
God may all that he wills, his will is just,
God wills all good to them that in him trust.
Where the Almighties lightening brand doth light,
It dims the daz'led eyes, and daunts the senses quite
—The Gods are ever just,
Our faults excuse their rigour must.
The Lord law-maker just and righteous,
Doth frame his laws not for himself but us:
He frees himself; and flies with his powers wing,
No where but where his holy will doth bring.
All that he doth is good, because it doth proceed
From him: that is the root of good indeed
From him; that is the spring of righteousness:
From him, whose goodness nothing can express.
—Indeed the evil done
Dies not when breath the body first doth leave,
But from the grandsire to the nephews son,
And all his seed the curse doth often cleave,
Till vengeance utterly the guilt bereave:
So straightly God doth judge.
There is no strength in armour, man or horse,
Can vaile, If Jove on wronged take remorse:
For he on whom the deadly dart doth light,
Can never scape by ransom, friend nor flight.
Eternal providence exceeding thought,
Where none appears, can make her self away.
If Gods can their own excellence excel,
It's in pardoning mortals that rebel.
God most doth punish, whom he most regardeth.
—Where Gods do vengeance crave,
It is not strong deensive walls that any thing can save.
—God hath made a salve for every sore,
If men would learn the same for to apply.
Man purposeth, but all things are disposed
By that great God that sits and rules above.
What man is he that boasts of fleshly might,
And vain assurance of mortality?
Which all so soon as it doth come to fight,
Against spiritual foes, yields by and by,
Or from the field most cowardly doth fly?
Ne let the man ascribe it to his skill,
That though grace hath gained victory.
If any sleight we have it is to ill,
But all the good is Gods, both power and eke the will.
God never seeks by trial of temptation,
To sound mans heart and secret cogitation.
For well he knows man, and his eye doth see
All thoughts of men, ere they conceived be.
—God
Conjoins no less our willes then bolds our hearts,
A sure presage that he is on our parts.
Our God is just, whose stroke delayed long,
Doth light at last with pain more sharp and strong.
The misty clouds that fall sometime
And overcast the skies:
Are like to troubles of our time,
Which do but dim our eyes.
But as such dews are dried up quite
When Phebus shows his face:
So are sad fancies put to flight,
When God doth guide by grace.
Gods mercy gently weighs his justice down.
So blinds the sharpest counsel of the wise,
This overshadowing providence on hie:
And dazzleth the clearest sighted eyes,
That they see not how nakedly they lie.
There where they little think the storm doth rise,
And overcast their clear security.
When man hath stopped all ways save only that,
That (least suspected) ruin enters at.
When Sathan tempts he leads us unto hell,
But God doth guide whereas no death doth dwell.
When Sathan tempts he seeks our faith to foil,
But God doth seal it never to recoil.
Sathan suggesteth ill, good moves to grace,
The divel seeks our baptism to deface.
But God doth make our burning zeal to shine,
Amongst the candles of his Church divine.
—Gods word
(Which made the world, sustains and guides it still)
To divers ends conducts both good and ill.
He that prefers not God fore all his race,
Amongst the sons of God deserves no place.
And he that plows the furrows of Gods feeld,
May not turn back his fainting face nor yield.
God with eternal bread in time of need,
His loved Jacob fortie years did feed.
And gave them water from the solid stone,
Which of it self had never moisture none.
Their caps, their coats, and shoes that they did wear,
God kept all fresh and new full fortie year.
The most just God when once mans sinnes do grow
Beyond the bounds of pardon and of grace:
Because that men his judgements best may know:
Like to his love, to rule on earth doth place
Monsters most vile to tyrannize us so,
With wrong the right, with lust laws to deface.
For this said cause were Scylla sent and Marius,
The Nerons both, and filthy minded Varius:
For this Domitian held in Rome the reign,
And Antoninus of that name the last:
And Messinine a base unworthy swain.
To place mankind in princely throne was plaste:
For this in Thebes did cruel Creon reign,
With other tyrants more in ages past.
For this of late hath Italy bene won,
By men of Lombardie, of Goth and Hunne.
