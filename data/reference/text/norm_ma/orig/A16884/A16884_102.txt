— All mans life me seems a Tragedy,
Full of sad sighs and sore Catastrophes,
First coming to the world with weeping eye,
Where all his days like dolorous Trophies,
Are heaped with spoils of fortune and of fear.
And he at last laid forth on baleful bear.
Our life is but a step in dusty way.
This mortal life as death is tride,
And death gives life.
What in this life we have or can desire,
Hath time of growth and moment of retire.
Our bodies, every footstep that they make,
March toward death, until at last they die:
Whether we work or play, or sleep or wake,
Our life doth pass, and with times wings doth fly.
The life of man a warfare right, in body and in soul,
Resignes his robbed carcase to be rolled in the mould.
—The term of life is limited.
Ne may a man prolong or shorten it,
The soldier may not move from watchful stid,
Nor leave his stand until his captain bid.
The longer life I wot the greater sin,
The greater sin, the greater punishment.
Thus passeth with the overplus of life,
The pleasant spring and flower of mortal life:
The Aprils pomp once subject to decay,
Returns not in the bud that earst was rife.
Whilest mornings weep, the lively flower doth bost,
Then pluck the stalk, and let not it be lost.
The sun doth set and brings again the day,
But when our life is gone, we sleep for aye.
Sun sets and riseth, goes down and quickly reviveth,
But mans light once out, eternal darkness abideth.
All mortal men must from this life be gone,
Of life and death, there are more souls then one.
The greatest and most glorious thing on ground,
May often need the help of weakest hand,
So feeble is mans state, and life unsound,
That in assurance it may never stand,
Till it disordered be from earthly band.
— The restless life which men here lead,
May be resembled to the tender plant:
It springs, it sprouts, as babes in cradle breed,
Flourish in May, like youths that wisdom want,
In Autumn ripe, and rots least store wax scant.
In winter shrinks and shrowds from every blast,
Like crooked age, when lusty youth is past.
The wicked livers oftentimes have wicked ends.
Life is not lost said she, for which is bought
Endless renown, that more then death is to be sought.
Better it is for one to live obscure,
Then in a public state to live unsure.
No life is blest that is not graced with love.
They double life that dead things grief sustain,
They kill, that feel not their friends living pain.
That life's ill spared that's spared to cast more blood.
