—These two parts belong
Unto true knowledge, words and tears have force,
To move compassion in the savage minds
Of brutish people reason wanting kinds.
Tears, vows, and prayers gain the hardest hearts.
Tears work no truce, but where the heart is tender.
Tears harden lust, though marble wear with rain.
Seld speaketh love, but sighs his secret pains,
Tears are his truch-men, words do make him tremble.
Tears cannot soften flint, nor vows convert.
A doleful case desires a doleful song,
Without vain art, or curious complement,
And squalid fortune into baseness flung,
Doth scorn the pride of wonted ornament.
