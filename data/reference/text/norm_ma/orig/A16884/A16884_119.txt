Nature in which divinity doth shine,
Lively presenting unborn deity:
Is that same spirit of reason most divine,
Which causeth every natural work to be.
All things she doth preserve, and can refine
Muddy pollutions from impiety.
Philosophy can teach no art nor ground,
Which Nature (elder borne) had not first found.
—Nature in mans heart her laws doth pen,
Prescribing truth to wit, and good to will,
Which do accuse, or else excuse all men,
For every thought or practise good or ill.
Nature above all things requireth this,
That we our kind do labour to maintain.
Nature which headlong into life doth throng us
With our feet forward to our grave doth bring us:
What is less ours, then this our borrowed breath▪
We stumble into life, we go to death.
Inexplicable nature by the God of nature wroght,
Makes things seem miracles to some, to some not wonders thoght.
And every climates people both as they are men and live,
Do differ: if observed, she not admired doth give
The workman rather then the work extol we, though in her
Not curiously, and all things to his providence refer.
Nature hath poured inough in each mans lap,
Could each man learn to use his private happen.
— Marks descried in mens nativity,
Are natures faults, not their own infamy.
Nature is Learnings eyes, she natures thought,
Use wanting either, is imperfect made,
They without use, no better then a shade.
— Nature seemeth only fair in change.
—Where nature fails in strength she adds in wit.
Nature gives beauty, fortune wealth in vain.
—The desire of nature is not vain,
She covets not impossibilities,
Fond thoughts may fall into some Idle brain,
But one assent of all is ever wise.
Nature doth hate and shun her contrary.
—Nature teacheth ever
Who loves preferment, needs must love the giver.
