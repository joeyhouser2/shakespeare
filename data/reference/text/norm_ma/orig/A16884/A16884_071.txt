Great things still orewhelme themselves by waight.
Greatness like to the suns reflecting powers,
The fier bred vapours naturally exhailes,
And is the cause that oft the evening lowers,
When foggy mists enlarge their dusky sails.
That his own beams he in the clouds impailes,
And either must extinguish his own light,
Or by his vertue cause his proper right.
To be huge is to be deadly sick.
O blinded Greatness, thou with thy turmoil,
Still selling happy life, makest life a toil.
— He that strives to manage mighty things,
Amidst his triumphs, bears a troubled mind:
The greatest hope the greatest harvest brings,
And poor men in content there glory find.
The man that furthereth other men to thrive,
Of private greatness doth himself deprive.
