His head grows giddy, and his foot indents,
A mighty fume his troub•ed brain torments,
His idle prattle from their purpose quite,
Is abrupt, fluttering, all confused, and light,
His wine stuffed stomach wrung with wind he feels,
His trembling tent all topsyturvy wheels,
At last not able on his legs to stand,
More like a foul swine then a sober man,
Opprest with sleep he wallows on the ground,
His shameless snorting trounke so deeply drownd,
In selfe-oblivion, that he did not hide,
Those parts that Caesar covered when he died.
