She seemed of womans shape, but in her head
A thousand eyes she had that watch did keep:
As many ears with which she hearkened,
Her eyes want lids, and therefore never sleep.
In stead of hair, her crown snakes overspread.
Thus marched she forth of the darkness deep,
Her tail one serpent bigger then the rest,
Which she with knots fastened about her brest.
A monster, others harm, self misery
Beauties plague, virtues scourge, succour of lies.
Who since he hath by natures special grace,
So piercing pawes as spoil, when they embrace,
So nimble feet, as stir though still on thorns.
So many men seeking their own woe.
So ample ears that never good news kowes
Is it not ill that such a beast want hotness?
O hateful hellish snake what fury first
Broughr thee from baleful house of Proserpine?
Where in her bosom she the long had nursed,
And fostered up with bitter milk of time,
Foul jealousy that turnest love divine.
To day less dread, and makest the living hart
With hateful thoughts to languish and to pine,
And feed it self with self consuming smart,
Of all the passions in the mind, thou viler art.
O Jealousy, daughter of Envy and love,
Most wayward issue of a gentle sire
Fostered with fears thy fathers joys to prove,
Mirth marring monster, borne in subtlety,
Hateful unto thy self, flying thy own desire,
Feeding upon suspect that doth renew thee,
Happy were Lovers, if they never knew thee.
Thou hast a thousand gates thou enterest by,
Condemning trembling passions to our hart.
Hundred eyed Argus, ever making spy,
Pale hag, infernal fury, pleasures smart:
Envious observer, prying in every part,
Suspicions fearful, gazing still about the hart.
O would to God that love could be without thee.
A new disease? I know not, new, or old;
But it may well be termed, poor mortal plain.
For like the pestilence, it doth infect
The houses of the brain: first it begins
Solely to work upon the phantasie,
Filling her seat with such pestiferous air,
As soon corrupts the judgement, and from thence
Sends like contagion to the memory,
Still each of other taking like infection,
Which as a searching vapour spreads it self,
Confusedly through every sensive part,
Till not a thought or motion in the mind,
Be far from the black poison of suspect.
Where love doth reign, disturbing jealousy,
Doth call himself affections Centinel,
And in a peaceful hour, doth cry kill, kill,
Distempering gentle love with his desire,
As air and water doth abate the fire:
This sound informer, this bare-breeding spy,
This cancker that eats up this tender spring,
This carry-tale, discentio's jealousy.
Foul weatherd jealousy to a forward spring,
Makes weeds grow rank, but spoils a better thing.
Sows tares gainst harvest in the fields of love,
And dogged humor dogdayes like doth prove,
Scorching loves glorious world with glowing tong,
A serpent by which love to death is stonge
A foe to waste his pleasant summer bowers,
Ruin his mansions, and deface his bowers.
Pale Jealousy child of insatiate love,
Of heartsick thoughts, which melancholy bred,
A hell tormenting fear, no faith can move:
By discontent with deadly poison fed,
With heedless youth and error vainly led.
A mortal plague, a vertue drowing flood,
A hellish fier, not quenched but with blood.
What state of life more pleasant can we find,
Then these that true and hearty love do bear?
Whom that sweet yoke doth fast together bind,
That man in Paradise first learned to wear.
Were not some so tormented in their mind
With that same vile suspect that filthy fear,
That torture great, that foolish phrenezie,
That raging madness, called Jealousy,
For every other sower that gets a place
To seat it self amidst this pleasant sweet,
Helps in the end to give a greater grace,
And make loves joys more gracious then they were,
He that abstains from sustenance a space,
Shall find both bread and water relish sweet.
— Jealousy is Cupids food,
For the swift steed runs not so fast alone,
As when some other strive him to out go.
Love wakes the jealous eye, least then it moves
The jealous eye, the more it looks it loves.
—No jealousy can that prevent,
Whereas two parties once be full content.
Impatience changeth smoke to flame, but jealousy to hell.
On love, says some, waits jealouzie, but jealousy wants love
When curiously the overplus doth idle quarrels move.
—Where jealousy is bred,
Horns in the mind, are worse then horns in the hed.
That cankerworm, that monster jealousy,
Which eats the heart, and feeds upon the gall,
Turning all loves delight to misery,
Through fear of losing his felicity.
Shun jealousy that hart-break love, if cat will go to kind,
Be sure that Jo hath a means, that Argus shall be blind.
True love doth look with pale suspicious eye,
Take away love, if you take jealousy.
No beast is fierer then a jealous woman.
