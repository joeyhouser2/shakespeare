Vertue, in price, whom ancient sages had:
Why poorly clad? for fading goods past care:
Why double faced? I mark each fortunes rare:
This bridle what? minds rages to restrain:
Why bear you tools? I love to take great pain:
Why wings? I teach above the stars to fly:
Why tread you death? I only cannot die.
The path that leads to Virtues Court is narrow,
Thorny, and up a hill, a bitter journey:
But being gone through, you find all heavenly sweets,
Th'entrance is all flinty, but at th'end
Two Towers of pearls and crystal you ascend.
Vertue is fairest in a poor art aye.
Vertue abhors too wear a borrowed face.

When vertue riseth, base affections fall.
Like as the horse well mand abides the bit,
And learns his stop by rain in riders hand,
Where mountain colt that is not saddled yet,
Runs headlong on amidst the fallowed land,
Whose fierce resist scarce bends with any band.
So men reclaimed by vertue tread aright,
Where led by follies, mischiefs on them light.
Vertue doth •urb affection, and for conscience flieth sin,
To leave for imperfection fear or shame no praise doth win.
Vertue it self turns vice, being misapplied,
And vice sometime by action dignified.
Vertue in greatest danger is most shown,
And though opprest, yet nere is overthrown.
In vertue it is said, that men themselves suruie.
Honour indeed, and all things yield to death,
(Vertue excepted) which alone survives,
And living toileth in an earthly gail,
At last to be extolled in heavens high joys.
All things decay, yet vertue shall not die,
This only gives us immortality.
Whence is it that the flower of the field doth fade,
And lyeth buried long in winters bale,
Yet soon as spring his mantle doth display,
It flowereth fresh, as it should never fail,
But thing on earth that is of most avail.
As virtues and beauties bud,

All that we had, or mortal men can have,
Seems only hut a shadow from the grave,
Vertue alone lives still.
Vertue is more amiable and more sweet,
When vertue and true majesty do meet.
—All the sorrow in the world is less
Then virtues might and valures confidence,

What vertue gets, once got doth never waste,
And having this, this thou for ever haste.
Joy graven in sence, like snow in water wasts,
Without preserve of vertue nothings lasts.
Vertue obscured yields small and happy gains,
But actively imployed, she worth retains.
What vertue breeds, iniquity devours,
We have no good at all that we can say is ours▪
But ill annexed opportunity,
