From hence with grace and goodness compast round
God ruleth, blesseth, keepeth, all he wrought:
Above the air, the fire, the sea, and ground,
Our sense, our wit, our reason, and our thought:
Where persons three, with power and glory crowned,
Are all one God, who made all things of nought.
Under whose feet subjected to his grace,
Sit nature, fortune, motion, time, and place.
This is the place from whence like smoke and dust
Of this frail world, the wealth, the pomp, the power
He tosseth, humbleth, turneth as he lust,
And guides our life, our end, our death and hower:
No eye (how ever vertuous, pure and just)
Can view the brightness, of that glorious bower,
On every side the blessed spirirs be
Equal in joys, though differing in degree.
In this great temple, richly beautified,
Paved all with stars, dispersed on Saphire flower,
The Clarke is a pure Angel sanctified,
The Judge our hie Messias full of power,
The Apostles, his assistance, every hower
The Jury Saints, the verdit Innocent,
The Sentence, Come ye blessed to my tent.
The spear that pierced his side, the writing Pen,
Christes blood the Ink, red Ink for Princes name,
The vails great breach, the miracles for men,
The sight is shew of them that long dead came
From their old graves, restored to living fame.
And that last signet passing all the rest,
Our souls discharged by Consumatum est.
Here endless joy is, there perpetual cheare,
Their exercise, sweet songs of many parts,
Angels the quier, whose symphony to hear,
Is able to provoke conceiving hearts,
To misconceive of all inticing arts.
The dirty praise, the subject is the Lord,
That tunes their gladsome spirit to this accord.
What so the Heavens in their secret doombe,
Ordained have, how can frail fleshly wight
Forecast, but it must needs to issue come.
What in the heavenly parliament above,
Is written by the finger of the first,
Mortals may feel, but never can remove,
For they are subject to the heavens worst.
By mortal law the bond may be divorced,
The heavens decrees by no means can be forced.
In vain doth man contend against the Stars,
For what he seeks to make, his wisdom marres.
— Humane wishes never have the power
To hurt or hast the course of heaven one hower.
Experience proves, and daily it is seen,
In vain (too vain) man strives against the heavens.
It is most true, that eyes are bound to serve
The inward part, and that th'heavenly part
Ought to be King, from whose rules who doth swerve,
Rebels to nature, strive for their own smart.
True that true beauty, vertue is indeed,
Whereof this beauty can be but a shade:
Which elements, with mortal mixture breed,
True that on earth we are but pilgrimss made,
And should in soul up to our country move.
Heaven is our home, we are but strangers here.
The heavens, earth, and air, and seas and all,
Taught men to see, but not to shun their fall.
Things which presage both good and ill there be,
Which heaven foreshows, yet will not let us see.
From them comes good, from them comes also ill,
That which they made, who can them warn to spill.
In vain be arms, when heaven becomes thy foe.
Look when the heavens are to justice bent,
All things be turned to our just punishment.
All powers are subject to the power of heaven,
Nor wrongs pass unrevenged, although excused.
Would heaven her beauty should be hid from sight,
Nere would she thus adorn her self with light,
With sparkling Lamps; nor would she paint her throne
But she delighted to be gazed upon.
And when the glorious sun goes down,
Would she put on her stary bestudded crown,
And in her masking sure, the spangled sky
Come forth to bride it with her revellry,
Heavens gave this gift to all things in creation,
That they in this should imitate their fashion.
Heavens influence was never constant yet,
In good or bad, as to continue it.
If thou be wise hold this as ominous,
The heavens not like disposed every hour,
The stars be still predominant in us:
Fortune not alway forth her bag doth pour,
Nor every cloud doth rain a golden shower.
