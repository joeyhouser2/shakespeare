— Desire, whom not the firmament,
Nor air, nor earth, nor Ocean can content,
Whose looks are hooks, whose bellies bottomless,
Whose hands are gripes to scrape with greediness,
Under whose command,
She brings to field a rough unruly band,
First, secret burning, mighty swoln ambition,
Whom Epicurus many worlds suffice not,
Whose furious thirst of proud aspiring dies not,
Whose hands transported with phantasticke passion,
Bear painted steeples in imaginaton.
Amongst the most, the worst, we best can chuse,
Tis easy to desire, but hard to use.
Desire hath philters, which desire procure.
If blind desire thy heart hath once embraced,
Inthrall'd it is, and honour so defaced.
Desire with small encouraging grows bold.
What can be said that Lovers cannot say?
Desire can make a Doctor in a day.
Things much retained, do make us much desire them,
And beauties seldom seen, makes us admire them.
