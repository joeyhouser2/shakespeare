The wrathful Winter proching on a pace,
with blustering blasts had all ybard the treene,
And old Saturnus with his frosty face,
with chilling cold had pierced the tender green;
The mantles rent wherein inwrapped been;
The gladsome Groves that now lay overthrown,
The Tapers torn, and every tree down blown;
The soil that erst so seemly was to seem,
was all despoiled of her beauties hew,
And stole fresh flowers (wherewith the somers Queen
Had clad the earth) now Boreas blast down blew,
And small fowls flocking in their songs did rew
The winters wrath, wherewith each thing defaced,
In woeful wise bewailed the Sommer past:
Hawthorne had lost his motly livery:
The naked twigs were shivering all for cold,
And dropping down the tears abundantly;
Each thing (me thought) with weeping eye me told,
The cruel season, bidding me withhold
My self within, for I was gotten out
Into the fields, whereas I walked about.
—When ye count ye free from fear,
Comes the bream Winter with chamfered browes,
Full of wrinkles and frosty furrows,
Shooting his grisly dart,
Which cruddles the blood and pricks the hart.
