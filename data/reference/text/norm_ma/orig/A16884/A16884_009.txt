— Greedy Avarice by him did ride,
Upon a Camel loaden all with gold,
Two iron coffers hung on either side,
With precious mettle, full as they might hold:
And in his lap a heap of coin he told,
For of his wicked pelf his god he made,
And unto hell, himself for money sold
Accursed usury was all his trade,
And right and wrong alike in equal balance weighed.
Forth of a Desart wood an ugly beast
There seemed to come, whose shape was thus defined,
Ears of an Ass, a Wolf in head and breast,
A carcase all with pinching famine pined,
A Lyons grisly jawe, but all the rest
To fox-like shape did seem to be enclined,
In England, France, in Italy and Spaeine,
Yea all the world this monster seemed to rain,
Where ere this cruel monster set his foot,
He killed and spoiled of every sort and state:
No height of birth or state with him did boot
He conquered Kings and crowns all in like rate.
Yea this beasts power had tane so deep a root,
It entered in Christs Vicars sacred gate,
And vexed Cardinals and Bishops chief,
And bred a scandal even in our belief.
Python whom Phoebus killed with thousand darts,
Was monster less then this by thousand parts.
Eriphilaes Armor.
In vain it were for to declare in Verse,
How sumptuously her armour all was wrought,
All set with stones, and set with Indian Gold,
Perfect for use, and pleasant to behold.
Mounted she was, but not upon a steed,
In stead whereof, she on a Wolf did sit:
A Wolf whose match Apulia doth not breed,
Taught to obey, although she used no bit.
And all of sandy colour was her weed,
Her arms were this, for such a Champion fit,
An ugly toad was painted on her shield,
With poison swollen, and in a sable field.
—Avarice, all armed in hooking •enters,
All clad in birdlime, without bridge she venters,
Through fell Charibdis and false Syrtes Ness,
The more her wealth, the more her wretchedness,
Cruel, respectless, friendless, faithless else,
Those foul base figures in each dunghill pool,
Like Tantalus starved in the midst of store,
Not that she hath, but what she wants she counts,
A well-winged Bird, that never lofty mounts.
Regard of worldly muck doth foully blend,
And low abase the hie heroic spirit,
That joys for crowns and kingdoms to contend.
We aged cark to live, and leave an overplus in store,
Perhaps for spend-alls: so amidst abundance live we poor.
Those which much covet, are with gain so fond,
That what they have not that which they possess:
They scatter and unloose from their bond.
And so by hoping more, they have but less,
Or gaining more, the profit of excess
Is but to surfeit, and such griefs sustain,
That they prove banckrout in this poor rich vain.
Those that will all devour, must all forgo.
Content thee with unthreatened mean, and play not Aesops dog
The gold that gentle Bacchus gave, did greedy Mydas clog:
Commit not treasure with thy child to greedy minded men,
Thou leavest Polydor a spoil to Polymnestor then.
