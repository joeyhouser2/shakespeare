Ears office is the troubled air to take,
Which in their mazes forms a sound or noise,
Whereof her self doth true distinction make.
The wickets of the soul are placed on hie,
Because all sounds do lightly mount aloft:
And that they may not pierce too violently,
They are delayed with turns and windings oft.
As streams which with their winding banks do play,
Stopped by their creeks run softly through the plain:
So in the ears labyrinth the voice doth stay,
And doth with easy notice touch the brain.
It is the slowest yet the daintiest sence,
For even the ears of such as have no skill,
Perceive a discord and conceive offence,
And knowing not what's good, yet find the ill.
These conduit pipes of knowledge the mind,
But th'other three attend the body still:
For by their services the soul doth find
What things are to the body good or ill.
The second bulwark was the hearing sence,
Gainst which the second troupe designment makes
Deformed creatures, in strange difference,
Some having heads like hearts, some like to snakes,
Some wild like boars, late roused out of the brakes.
Slanderous reproaches and foul infamies,
Leasings, backbitings, and vainglorious crake.
Bad counsels, praises and false flatteries,
All those against that first did send their batteries.
