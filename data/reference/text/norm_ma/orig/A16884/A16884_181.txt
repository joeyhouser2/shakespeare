The wit the pupil of the souls clear eye,
And in mans world the only shining star,
Looks in the mirror of the phantasie,
Where all the gathering of the senses ate,
From thence this power the shape of things abstract•
And them within her passive part receives,
Which are inlightened by that part which acts,
And so the form of single things receives:
But after by discoursing to and fro,
Anticipating and comparing things
She doth all universal natures know,
And all effects into their causes bring.
Our wit is given Almighty God to know,
Our will is given to love him being known,
But God could not be known to us below,
But by his works, which through the sence are known.
Wit is the minds cheefe judge, which doth control,
Of fancies Court the judgements false and vain,
Will, holds the royal scepter in the soul,
And on the passions of the heart doth reign.
Emulation the proud nurse of wit.
—Wit and learning are two Angels wings,
By which mean men soar up to mighty things.
Wit is with boldness prompt, with terror daunted,
And grace is sooner got of dames then granted.
Some lose their wit with love, some with ambition
Some running to the sea great wealth to get,
Some following Lords and men of high condition,
Some in fair jewelles, rich and costly set.
One hath desire to prove a rare magician,
Others with Poetry their wit forget:
Another thinks to be an Alchimist,
Till all he spent, and he his number mist.
Mans wit is monstrous, when the same from vertue doth decline.
Mans wit doth build for time but to devour,
But Virtue's free from time and fortunes power.
The wit not hurt, because not used more,
Grows dull and far less toward then before.
—Wits ambition longeth to the best,
For it desires in endless bliss to dwell.
Best loves are lost for wit, when men blame fortune.
— Careless wit is wanton beauties page.
The finest wits are soonest snarde with love.
A settled brain is worth a world of wit.
Wits want makes men desirous to seem wise.
