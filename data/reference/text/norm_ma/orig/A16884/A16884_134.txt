O poverty, chief of the heavenly brood.
—Such is the world, this cros-blis world of ours,
That vertue hardly hides her self in poor and desart bowers,
And such be best as seem not best, content exceeds a crown
—Powerful need (arts ancient dame, and keeper)
The early watch clock of the slothful sleeper.
—Lack is thrall and slave to every thing.
Need is mistress of all exercise.
A scholars want exceeds a clowns content.
No danger but in hie estate, none err in mean degree.
—Where imperious need doth tyrannize,
The holy heat through worldly cares doth pause
Its soiled with earthly thoughts and downward draws.
Hence come those dull conceits among the wise,
Which coy eared readers censure to proceed
From ignorance, whereas they grow by need.
The citizens like ponned pikes, the lesser feeds the great,
The rich for meat seek stomachs, and the poor for stomach meat.
Be as thou art, not as thou wouldst, it will be as it is,
Learn then to lack, and learn to live, for crosses never miss.
