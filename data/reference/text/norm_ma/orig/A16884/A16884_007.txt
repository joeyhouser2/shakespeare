What need we creep the cross to give unto a begging saint,
Tush, tush, a fig for book love, none be fortunate, that faint.
Things out of hope are compast oft with venturing,
Chiefly in love, whose leave exceeds commission:
Affection faints not like a pale faced coward,
But then woes best, when most his choice is froward.
Blushing and sighing Theseus never strove
To woo and win Antiope his love.
—When all is done that do we may,
Labour we sorrowing all the night, and suing all the day,
The female faulty custom yields less merit, greatest pay:
And venturous more then vertuous means doth bear the bel away.
