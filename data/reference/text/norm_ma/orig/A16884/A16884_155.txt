Lastly, the feeling power which is lifes root,
Through every living part it self doth shed,
By sinews which extend from head to foot,
And like a net all over the body spred.
Much like a subtle spider which doth sit
In middle of her web which spreadeth wide:
If ought do touth the outmost thred of it,
She feels it instantly on every side.
By touch the first pure qualities we learn,
Which quicken all things, hot, cold, moist, and dry:
By touch, hard, soft, rough, swoot, we do discern,
By touch, sweet pleasure and sharp pain we try.
These are the outward instruments of sence.
These are the guards which every one must pass,
Ere it approach the minds intelligence,
Or touch the phantasie, wits looking glass.
But the fift troupe most horrible of hue,
And fierce of force was dreadful to report:
For some like snails, some did like spiders shewe,
And some like ougly urchins thick and short,
Cruelly they assailed that fift fort▪
Armed with darts of sensual delight,
With strings of carnal lust and strong effect.
Of feeling pleasures, with which day and night
Against the same fift bulwark they continued fight.
