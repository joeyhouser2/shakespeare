The Spider with great skill, doeth travel day by day,
His limbs no time lie still, to set his house in state:
And when he hath it wrought, thinking therein to reign,
A blast of wind unthought, doeth drive it down again.

The proof whereof is true, to make his work indure,
He pains him self a new, in hope to dwell more sure:
And in some secret place, a corner of a wall,
He frameth him self a pace, to build and rest withal.

His pleasure sweet to stay, when he to rest is bent,
An ugly shamble Flee, approcheth to his tent:
And htere entendes by force, his labours great to win,
Or else to yield his corse, by fatal death therein.

Thus is the Spiders nest, from time to time thrown down,
And he to labour prest, with endless pain unknown:
So such as Lovers be, like travel do attain,
Those endless works ye see, are always full of pain.
