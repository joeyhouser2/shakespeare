IN search of things that secret are, my mated muse began,
What it might be, molested most the head and mind of man.
The bending brow of princes face, to wrath that doeth attend,
Or want of parents, wife or child, or loss of faithful friend.
The roaring of the Canon shot, that makes the peece to shake,
Or terrour such as mighty Jove, from heaven above can make.
All these in fine may not compare experience so doeth prove,
Unto the torments sharp and strange, of such as be in love.

Love looks a loft and laughs to scorn all such as grief annoy.
The more extreme their passions be, the greater is his joy.
Thus Love as victor of the field, triumphs above the rest,
And joys to see his subjects lie, with livyng death in brest.
But dire disdain lets drive a shaft, and gauls this bragging fool,
He plucks his plumes, unbendes his bow and sets him new to scole,
Whereby this boy, that bragged late, as conquerer over all,
Now yields him self unto disdain his vessall and his thrall.
