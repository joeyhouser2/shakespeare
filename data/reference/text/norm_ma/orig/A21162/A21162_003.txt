Amid the vale the sclender shrub, is hid from all mishap,
When taller tree that stands aloft, is rent with thunder clap.
The Turrets tops which touch the clouds, are beat with every blast,
Soon shivered are their stones with storm, and quickly over cast.
Best bodied tree in all the wood, for timber beam is found,
And to the axe the sturdiest Ox, doth yield and fall to ground.
The highest hill doth soonest feel, the flash of lightnings flame,
And soon decays the pomp and pride, of high renowmed name.
Of all the heard the huntman seeks, by proof as doth appear,
With double forked arrow head, to wound the greatest Dear.
The hautiest head of all the drove, enjoys the shortest life,
And stains the slaughter house with blood, at prick of butchers knife.
Thus what thing highest place attains, is soonest overthrown,
What ever Fortune sets a loft, she threats to throw it down.
And though no force resist thy power, nor seek thee to confound,
Yet doth the payse of weighty things, decline it self to ground.
For restless tipe of roulling wheel, example hath it tryde,
To heavy burden yield it must full soon, and slip aside.
What vails the rich his bed of down, that sighs for sleplesse thought,
What time in couch of flock the poor, sleeps sound and feareth nought.
At homely boord his quiet food, his drinks in treene be tane,
When oft the proud in cups of gold, with wine receive their bane.
The bed, the boord, the dread in dout, with train to be opprest,
When fortune frounes, their power must yield, as wire unto the wrest.
If Icarus had not presumed to high to take his flight,
He had not yet been drowned in Seas, that now Itarion hight.
If Phaeton had not enterprised to guide his fathers seat,
His fires had not enflamed the world, nor ben destroyed with heat.
But who so climes above the mean, there is no hope of stay,
The higher up the sonner down, and nearer his decay.
Then you that here in pomp or place, to guide the golden mase,
Let crown and Septer both obay the mean of Virtues race.
For neither shall renowmed Vertue see the pit of hell,
Nor yet in tomb of Marble stone, she may abide to dwell.
But from Sepulcher flies she hence, beyond the skies above,
And glistering in the blissful stares, she raines with mighty Jove.
