The mountains hie whose lofty tops, doeth meet the haughty sky
The craggy rock that to the sea, free passage doeth deny:
The aged Oke that doeth resist, the force of blustering blast,
The pleasant herb that every where, a fragrant smell doeth cast.
The Lions force whose courage stout, declares a princelike might,
The Eagle that for worthiness, is borne of kings in fight:
The Serpent eke whose poisoned jaws, doeth belch out venom vile,
The lothsome Tode that shumeth light, and liveth in exile.
These these I say and thousands more, by tracked of time decay,
And like to time do quite consume, and bade from time to clay:
But my true harte and service vowed, shall laste time out of mind,
And still remains as thine by dome, as Cupid hath assingde.
My faith lo here I vow to thee, my troth thou knowest right well,
My goods my friends, my life is thine, what need I more to tell?
I am not mine, but thine I vow, thy hests I will obey,
And serve thee as a servant ought, in pleasing if I may:
And sith I have no flying wings, to see thee as I wish,
Ne sinnes to cut the silver streams, as doeth the gliding fish.
Wherefore leave now forgetfulness, and send again to me,
And strain thy azured veins to write, then I may greeting see:
And thus farewell more deare to me, then chiefest friend I have,
Whose Love in harte I mind to shrine, till death his fee do crave.
