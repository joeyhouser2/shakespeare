Beauty (sweet Love,) is like the morning dew,
Whose short refresh upon the tender green:
Cheers for a time but till the Sun doth shew,
And straight tis gone as it had never been.
Soon doth it sade that makes the fairest flourish,
Short is the glory of the blushing Rose:
The hew which thou so carefully dost nourish,
Yet which at length thou must be forced to lose.
When thou surcharged with burthen of thy years,
Shalt bend thy wrinkles homeward to the earth,
When time hath made a passport for thy sears,
Dated in age the Kalends of our death.
But ah no more, this hath been often told,
And women grieve to think they must be old.
