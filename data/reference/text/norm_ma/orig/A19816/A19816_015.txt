Behold what hap Pigmalion had to frame,
And carve his proper grief upon a stone,
My heavy fortune is much like the same,
I work on Flint, and that's the cause I mone.
For hapless lo even with mine own desires,
I figured on the Table of mine hart,
The fairest form, that all the world admires,
And so did perish by my proper arte.
And still I toil, to change the Marble breast
Of her, whose sweetest grace I do adore,
Yet cannot find her breathe unto my rest,
Hard is her hart, and woe is me therefore.
O happy he that joyed his stone and arte,
Unhappy I to love a stony hart.
