Who craftly casts to steer his boat
and safely s•oures the flattering flood:
He cutteth not the greatest waves,
for why that way w•re nothing good.
Ne fleeteth on the crocked shore
lest harm him happen awaiting lest.
But wines away betwene them both,
as who would say the mean is best.
Who waiteth on the golden mean,
he put in point of sickerness:
Hides not his head in sluttish coats,
ne shrouds himself in filthiness.
Ne sits aloft in hye estate,
Where hateful hearts envy his chance:
But wisely walks betwixt them twaine,
ne proudly doth himself avance
The highest tree in all the wood
is rifest rent with blustering windes:
The higher hall the greater fall
such chance have proud and lofty minds.
When Jupiter from hye doth threat
with mortal mace and dint of thunder
The highest hills bene batrid eft
when they stand still that stoden under.
The man whose hed with wit is fraught
in wealth will fear a worser tide
When fortune fails dispaireth nought
but constantly doth still abide.
For he that sendeth grisly storms
with whisking windes and bitter blasts
And fowlth with hail the winters face,
and frotes the soil with hory frosts:
Even he adawth the force of cold
the spring in sends with somer hote:
The same full oft to stormy hearts
is cause of bale: of joy the root.
Not always yll though so be now
when clouds been driven, then rides the rack.
Phebus the fresh ne shooteth still,
sometime he harps his muse to wake.
Stand stiff therefore, pluck up thy hart,
lose not thy port though fortune fail.
Again when wind doth serve at will,
take heed to hye to hoist thy sail.
