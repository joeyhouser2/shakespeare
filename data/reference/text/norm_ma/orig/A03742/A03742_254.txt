Who so that wisely ways the profit and the price,
Of things wherein delight by worth is wont to rise.
Shall find no jewel is so rich ne yet so rare,
That with the friendly hart in value may compare.
What other wealth to man by fortune may befall,
But fortunes changed cheer may reve a man of all.
A frend no wrack of wealth, no cruel cause of woe,
Can force his friendly faith unfriendly to forgo.
If fortune friendly fawn, and lend thee wealthy store,
Thy friends conjoined joy doth make thy joy the more.
If frowardly she frown and drive thee to distress,
His aid releves thy ruth, and makes thy sorrow less.
Thus fortunes pleasant fruits by friends encreased be,
The bitter sharp and sowre by friends alayde to thee.
That when thou doest rejoice, then doubled is thy joy,
And eke in cause of care, the less is thy annoy.
Aloft if thou do live, as one appointed here,
A stately part on stage of worldly state to bere:
Thy friend as only free from fraud will thee advise,
To rest within the rule of mean as do the wise.
He seeketh to foresee the peril of thy fall.
He findeth out thy faults and warnes thee of them all.
Thee, not thy luck he loves, what ever be thy case,
He is thy faithful frend and thee he doth embrace.
If churlish cheare of chance have thrown thee into thrall,
And that thy need ask aid for to releve thy fall:
In him thou secret trust assured art to have,
And succour not to seek, before that thou can crave.
Thus is thy friend to thee the comfort of thy pain,
The stayer of thy state, the doubler of thy gain.
In wealth and woe thy frend, an other self to thee,
Such man to man a God, the proverb say to be.
As wealth will bring thee friends in louring woe to prove,
So woe shall yield thee friends in laughing wealth to love.
With wisdom chuse thy frend, with vertue him retain:
Let vertue be the ground, so shall it not be vain.
