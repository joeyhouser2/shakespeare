For love Apollo (his Godhead set aside)
Was servant to the king of Thessaley,
Whose daughter was so pleasant in his eye,
That both his harp and sawtrey he defied:
And bagpipe solace of the rural bride,
Did puff and blow• and on the holts hy,
His cattle kept with that rude melody,
And oft eke him that doth the heau•ns gide,
Hath love transsormed to shapes for him to base
Transmuted thus sometime a swan is he,
Leda taccoy, and eft Europe to please,
A mild white bull, unwrinkled front and face,
Suffreth her play till on his back leapeth she,
Whon in great care he ferieth through the seas.
