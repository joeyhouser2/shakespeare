Of all these bad, the best I think,
Is well to hope, though fortune shrink.
Desired things are not ay prest,
Nor things denied left all unsought,
Nor new things to be loved best,
Nor all offers to be set at nought,
Where faithful hart hath bene refused,
The choosers wit was there abused.
The woeful ship of careful sprite,
Fleeting on seas of w•lling tears,
With sails of wishes broken quite,
Hanging on waves of doleful fears,
By surge of sighs at wreck nere hand,
May fast no anker hold on land.
What helps the dial to the blind,
Or else the clock without it sound.
Or who by dreams doth hope to find,
The hidden gold within the ground:
Shall as free from cares and fears,
As he that holds a Wolf by the cares.
And how much mad is he that thinks
To clime to heaven by the beams,
What joy alas, hath he that winks,
At Titan or his golden streams,
His joys not subject to reasons laws,
That joyeth more then he hath cause.
For as the Phenix that climeth hye,
The sun lightly in ashes burneth,
Again, the Faulcon so quick of eye,
Soon on the ground the net masheth.
Experience therefore the mean assurance,
Prefers before the doubtful pleasance.
