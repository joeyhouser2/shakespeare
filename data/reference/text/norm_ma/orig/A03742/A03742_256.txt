Such wayward ways have some when folly stirs their brains
To fain and plain full oft of love, when lest they feel his pains.
And for to shew a grief such craft have they in store,
That they can halt and lay a salve whereas they feel no sore.
As hound unto the foot, or dog unto the bow,
So are they made to vent her out, whom bent to love they know.
That if I should discribe one hundred of their drifts,
Two hundred wits beside mine own I should put to their shifts
No woodman better knows how for to lodge his dere,
Nor shipman on the sea that more hath skill to guide the steer.
Nor beaten dog to herd can warer chose his game.
Nor scholeman to his fancy can a scholar better frame.
Then one of these which have old Ovids arte in ure,
Can seek the ways unto their mind a woman to allure.
As round about a hive the Bees do swarm alway,
So round about the house they prease wherein they seek their pray.
And whom they so besege, it is a wonderous thing,
What crafty engines to assault these wily warriors bring.
The eye as scout and watch to stir both to and fro,
Doth serve to stale her he•e and there where she doth come and go.
The tong doth plead for right as herald of the hart:
And both the hands as orators do serve to point their part.
So shows the countenance then with these fowre to agree,
As though in witness with the rest, it would hers sworn be.
But if she then mistrust it would turn black to white,
For that the woorrier lokes most smooth when he wold fainest bite.
Then wit as counselor a help for this to find:
Straight makes the hand as secretair forthwith to write his mind.
And so the letters straight embassadours are made,
To treat in haste for to procure her to a better trade.
Wherein if she do think all this is but a shewe,
Or but a subtile masking cloke to hide a crafty shrew:
Then come they to the larme, then shew they in the field,
Then muster they in colours strange, that ways to make her yield
Then shoot they batry of, then compass they her in,
At tilt and turney oft they strive this selly soul to win.
Then sound they on their lutes, then strain they forth their song.
Then romble they with instruments to lay her quite a long.
Then borde they her with gifts, then do they woo and watch.
Then night and day they labour hard this simple hold to catch,
As paths within a wood, or turns with in a mase:
So then they shew of wiles and crafts they can a thousand wayes•
