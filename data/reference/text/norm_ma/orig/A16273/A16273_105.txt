Shepherd, why do'st thou hold thy peace?
Sing, and thy joy to us report:
My joy good Shepherd) should be less,
If it were told in any sort.
Though such great favours thou do'st win,
Yet deign thereof to tell some part:
The hardest thing is to begin,
In enterprizes of such Art.
Come make an end, no cause omit,
Of all the joys that thou art in:
How should I make an end of it,
That am not able to begin?
It is not just, we should consent,
That thou should'st not thy joys recite:
The soul that felt the punishment,
Doth only feel this great delight.
That joy is small, and nothing fine,
That is not told abroad to many:
If it be such a joy as mine,
It never can never be told to any.
How can this hart of thine contain
A joy, that is of such great force?
I have it, where I did retain
My passions of so great remorce.
So great and rare a joy is this,
No man is able to withhold:
But greater that a pleasure is,
The less it may with words be told.
Yet have I heard thee heretofore,
Thy joys in open Songs report:
I said, I had of joy some store,
But not how much, nor in what sort.
Yet when a joy is in excess,
It self it will oft-times unfold:
Nay, such a joy would be the less,
If but a word thereof were told.
