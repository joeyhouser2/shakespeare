I See thee jolly Shepherd merry,
And firm thy faith, and sound as a berry.
Love gave me joy, and Fortune gave it,
As my desire could wish to have it.
What didst thou wish, tell me (sweet Lover,)
Whereby thou mightest such joy recover?
To love where love should be inspired:
Since there's no more to be desired.
In this great glory, and great gladness,
Thinkest thou to have no touch of sadness?
Good Fortune gave me not such glory:
To mock my Love, or make me sorry.
If my firm love I were denying,
Tell me, with sighs would'st thou be dying?
Those words (in jeast) to hear thee speaking:
For very grief this hart is breaking.
Yet would'st thou change, I prithee tell me,
In seeing one that did excel me?
O no, for how can I aspire,
To more, then to mine own desire?
Such great affection do'st thou bear me:
As by thy words thou seemest to swear me?
Of thy deserts, to which a debtor
I am, thou maist demaund this better.
Sometimes me thinks, that I should swear it,
Sometimes me thinks, thou should'st not bear it.
Only in this my hap doth greeve me,
And my desire, not to beleeve me.
Imagine that thou do'st not love mine,
But some brave beauty that's above mine.
To such a thing (sweet) do not will me:
Where feigning of the same doth kill me.
I see thy firmness gentle Lover,
More then my beauty can discover.
And my good fortune to be higher
Then my desert, but not desire.
