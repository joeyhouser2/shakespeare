The cause why that thou do'st deny
To look on me, sweet foe impart?
Because that doth not please the eye.
Which doth offend and greeve the hart.
What woman is, or ever was,
That when she looketh, could be moved▪
She that resolves her life to pass,
Neither to love, nor to be loved.
There is no hart so fierce and hard.
That can so much torment a soul:
Nor Shepherd of so small regard,
That reason will so much control.
How falls it out Love doth not kill
Thy cruelty with some remorce?
Because that Love is but a will,
And freewill doth admit no force.
Behold what reason now thou hast,
To remedy my loving smart:
The very same binds me as fast,
To keep such danger from my hart.
Why do'st thou thus torment my mind,
And to what end thy beauty keep?
Because thou callest me still unkind,
And pitiless when thou do'st weep.
Is it because thy cruelty
In killing me doth never end?
Nay, for because I mean thereby,
My hart from sorrow to defend.
Be bold so foul I am no way
As thou do'st think, fair Shepherdess:
With this content thee, that I say,
That I beleeve the same no less.
What, after giving me such store▪
Of passions, do'st thou mock me too?
If answers thou wilt any more.
Go seek them without more ado.
