How can the cripple get, in running race the game?
Or he in fight defend himself, whose arms are broken lame?
How can th'imprisoned man whofe legs be wrapt in chains,
Think this his life a pleasant time, who knoweth nothing but pains?
So how can I rejoice, that have no pleasant thing,
That may revive my doulfull spirits, or cause me for to sing.
My legs be lame to go, mine arms cannot embrace,
My hart is sore, mine eyes be blind, for lack of Fortunes grace.
All this is Fortunes fault, that keeps these senses so,
She may advance them if she list, and rid them of this woe.
It is her cruel will, always on me to lower,
To keep fron me her pleasant gifts, to make me know her power
Alas, alas, fi• Fortune, f•e: why art thou so unkind,
To me that fain would be thy son, and ever in thy mind?
Now do I thee beseech, with pleasures me to freight,
To temper this my woeful life, or else to kill me straight.
