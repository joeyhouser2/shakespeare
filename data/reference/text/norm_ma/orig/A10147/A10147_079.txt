Behold me here whose youth, to withered years,
Doth bow and bend, compelled by crooked age:
See here my lyms, whose strength benumbde weres,
Whose pleasure spent, gray hears, bids to be sage.

But lo to late I lothe my life lewd spent,
And wish in vain, I had foreseen in youth:
These drowsy days, which moves me to lament
My idle youth proved, what thereof ensueth.

Unstored old years, must serve for lusty prime,
These feebled joints, must seek to serve their want:
With tedious toil, because I used not time,
Lo thus I live, sufficed perforce to scant.

In flaunting years, I flaunting flourished forth,
Amid delight, puft up, with puffing pride:
Mean garments then, I deemed nothing worth,
Nay, scace the best, might serve, my flesh to hide.

I thought them foes, which told me of my fault,
And judged them speak, of rigor, not good will:
Who told of gain, me thought for hire did hault,
Then lo, I lothde what now I wish by skill.

Experience moves me mone, the more my grief,
In lively years, because I did not shun
Such idle steps, least void of such relief,
As might have helped my age, now youth is dun.

But what prevails to wish I would I had,
Sith time delayed, may not be called again:
A guerdon just, (for such as youth too bad
Consumes, (it is) in time therefore take pain.

Seek how in youth to serve contented age,
Learn, how to lead, your life in virtues lore:
Behold you me, attached with death his page,
Constrained through want, my lewdness to deplore.

What grief more great, unto a hauty hart,
Then is distress, by folly forste to fall:
What care more cruel or loathsome, (to depart
From wealth to want) it greeves us to the gall.

But what avails to boast, or vaunt of vain?
What profit ist, to praise a passed pride?
Sith it consumed, is but a pinching pain,
A heap of harms, whose hurt I wretch have tryde.

A direful dreed, a surge of sorrowing sobs,
A carking care, a mount of mestive mone:
A sack of sin, couched full of cankered knobs,
A wavering weed, whose force is soon orethrone.

For proof behold, the boast of breathing breath,
See see how soon, his valiaunst vaunt doth vade:
Our pleasant prime, is subject unto death,
By vices urgde, in waves of woe to wade.

I know the state, and trust of every time,
I see the shame, whereto each vice doth cum:
Therefore (by me) learn how to leave such crime,

Let me your Mirror, learn you leave whats lewd,
My fall forepassed, let teach you to beware:
My ancient years with trial tript, have viewed,
The vaunt of vice, to be but carking care.
