TO thee I write whose life and death, thy faith may save or spil:
Which faith observe, I live in joy, if not, your freend you kill:
Suspect not that I do misdoubt, your loyalty at all:
But pender how that lovers are, unto suspicion thrall.
Which thraldom breedeth furth thrall, if wonted faith do fail:
Against the Lover thus forlorn, do thousand Cares prevail:
It little helps to have begun, and there to set a stay,
They win more fame, that fight it out: then those that run away.
Like as the willing hound that doth, pursue the Dear in Chace:
Will not omit unto the end, his painful weary race:
So Love (if love it be indeed) will stedfast still remain:
What so betide, good hap or yll, and not revoult again.
Such faith of you, sweet hart I ask, such faith: why said I so?
What need I to demaund the thing. I have had long ago:
Your faith you gave, the case is plain, you may not seem to start:
And I in earnest of the match did leave with you my hart.
But now perhaps you may alleage, long distance may procure,
A cause whereby our former love, no longer may endure:
If so you Judge to far amiss, although that sail and wind,
Convey my corpse to country strange, my hart remains behind.
Examples many could I shew, but needles is that pain,
Mine own example shall• suffise, when I return again:
Mean while although to swim I want, Leanders cunning art,
In all things else (except the same) Ile play Leanders part.
In hope that thou wilt shew thy self, to me an Hero true,
And so although loth to depart, I say sweet hart adue.

A Ring I send, wherein is pende, a Posy (if you reed)
Whereby you may, perceive alway, of what I most have need.
By me your friend, unto the end, if you thereto agree,
Although not so, your loving foe, I still perforce must be.
