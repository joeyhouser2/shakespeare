IN doubtful dreading thoughts, as I gan call to mind,
This world, and eke the pleasures all, that Adams children find,
A place of pleasant hew appeared to my thought
Where I might see the wonderous works which nature for us wrought.
All things of any price, approached to my sight,
And still me thought that each man had, that was his most delight.
The rich man hath his joy: his riches to imbrace,
So hath the huntsman his desire, to have the Hart in chace.
And other have their sport to see the Falcon flee,
And some also in Princes court: in favor for to be.
The warring Knight at will, an horse doth run his race,
And eke the lover, in his arms, his Lady doth embrace.
When that I see each man enjoy his whole delite,
Save I alas poor cursed man whom Fortune doth so spite.
I fall straight to the ground, amazed with much grief,
With bloody strokes upon my brest, I strive to rid my lief.
And thus I think, how can fair pictures those delight:
Whom nature from their tender age, defrauded of their sight.
