Since needs ye will me sing, give ear unto the voice,
Of me poor man your bond servant, that knoweth not to rejoice.
Consider well my care, my pain and my unrest:
Which thou with force of Cupids Dart hast grafted in my brest.
Heale, and withdraw from me, the venim of that Dart
Have pity, and release this woe, that doth consume my hart:
The greatness of my grief, doth bid me seek release
I seek to find to ease my pain, yet doth my care encrease.

I cease not to behold, that doth augment my pain:
I see my self I seek my woe, yet can I not refrain.
That should my woe release, doth most encrease the same,
The cold that should acquench the heat, doth most enrage the flame
My pleasure is my pain, my game is most my grief
My cheefe delite doth work my woe, my hart is my relief
Such haps doth hap to them, that happeth so to love,
And hap most harden: so fast to bind, that nothing can remove.

For when the harm is fixed, and rooted in the hart,
No tongue can tell, nor pen may write, how grievous is the smart
I have thought love but play, until I felt the sore,
But now I felt a thousand griefs I never felt before.
To tell what pains I bide, if that I could devise,
I tell the truth, beleeve me well, the day will not suffise
Graunt now therefore some rest, since thus thou hast me bound,
To be thine own, till body mine, lie buried under ground.
