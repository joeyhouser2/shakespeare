Health I thee send, if he may give, that which himself doth miss:
For thy sweet brest, doth harbor whole, my bloody bale or bliss,
I need no scribe, to scry my care, in restless rigor spread:
They that behold, my changed cheare, already judge me dead.
My baned limbs, have yielded up, their wonted joy to die:
My healthless hand, doth nought but wring, and dry my dropping eye,
The deadly day, in dole I pass, a thousand times I crave
The noisome night: again I wish, the doleful day to have.
Each hour to me, most hateful is, each place doth urge my woe:
No food me feeds, close up mine eyes, to gastly grave I go.
No Physics art, can give the salve, to heal my painful part:
Save only thou, the salve and sore, of this my captive hart,
Thou art the branch that sweetly springs, whose hart is sound and true
Can only cheare me woeful wight, or force my want to rue.
Then give to me, the sap I thirst, which gift may give me joy,
I mean thy firm, and faithful love, whose want breeds mine annoy,
Remember yet sure friendship had, ypast betweene us twayne
Forget him not, for love of thee, who sighs in secret pain.
I oft do seem in company, a gladsome face to bear,
But God thou knowest my inward woes, and cares that rent me there:
And that I may, gush out my grief, in secret place alone,
I bid my friends farewell in haste, I say I must be gone.
Then haste I fast, with heavy hart, in this my doleful case:
Where walks no wight, but I alone, in drewsie desart place,
And there I empt, my laden hart, that swelled in fretting mone:
My sighs and plaints, and pangs I tell, unto my self alone.
What shall I say? do ask me once, why all these sorrows be▪

Then send me close, the hewing knife, my wider wound to stratch:

Thou wilt not be, so mercylesse, to slay a loving hart▪
Small praise it is to conquer him, that durst no where to start,
Thou hast the sword, that cut the wound, of my unhol•en pain:
Thou canst and art, the only help, to heal the same again.
Then heal the hart, that loves thee well, until the day he die:
And firmly fast thy love on him, thats true continually,
In thee my wealth, in thee my woe, in thee too save or spill:
In thee me life, in thee my death, doth rest to work thy will.
Let vertue mixed, with pity great, and loving mercy save
Him, who without thy salve, so sick, that he must yield to grave,
O salve thou then, my secret sore, sith health in thee doth stay:

Then shall I bless, the pleasant place, where once I took thy glove,
