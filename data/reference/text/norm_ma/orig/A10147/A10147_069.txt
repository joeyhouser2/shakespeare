Because my hart is not mine own, but resteth now with thee,
I greet thee well of heartiness, thy self mayest Carver be:
Muse not hereat but like hereof, first read and then excuse,
I wish to you a pliant hart, when you these lines peruse.
Hope bids me speak, fear stayes my tongue, but Cupid makes me bold,
And Fancy harps of good success, when that my plaint is tould:
Thus Hope doth prick, and fear doth kick, and fancy feeds my brain,
In you alone doth now consist, the salve to ease my pain.
You are my Paradise of joy, the heaven of my delight,
And therewithal (which thing is strange) the worker of my spight:
Which spight I seek not to revenge, but meekly to subdue,
Not as a foe, but as a freend, I do your love pursue.
I yield my self unto your power, and will not you relente?
In humble wise I mercy crave, and is your mercy spent?
No sure, as nature outwardly, hath showed in you her skill,
I doubt not but that inwardly, the like she doth fulfil.
So good a face, so trim a grace, as doth in you remain:
A Cressids cruel stony harte, I know may not retain:
Wherefore to ratefie my words, let deeds apparent be:
Then may you vaunt and prove it true, you freedom gave to me.
Consider of my restless care, and way blind Cupids ire:
Then shall you find my painful love, doth claim but earned hire.
Requite not this my curtesy, and friendship with disdain,
But as I love unfeignedly, so yield like love again.
Allow hereof as for the rest, that doth belong to love:
My self thereof will take the care, as time, in time shall prove.
Mean while, I wish a Thisbies hart, in you there may endure:
Then doubt not, but a Pyramus, of me you shall procure.

Yours at your will,
To save or spill.
