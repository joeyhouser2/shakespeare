The sturdy rock for all his strength, by raging seas is rent in twaine,
The marble stone is pierced at length, with little drops of drilling rain:
The Ox doth yield unto the yoke,
The Steel obeyeth the hammer stroke.

The stately stag that seems so stout, by yalping hounds at bay is set,
The swiftest bird that flees about, is caught at length in fowlers Net:
The greatest fish in deepest brook,
Is soon deceived with subtle hook.

Yea man himself, unto whose will, all things are bounden to obay,
For all his wit and worthy skill, doth fade at length and fall away:
There is nothing, but time doth wast,
The Heavens, the Earth, consume at last.

But vertue sits triumphing still, upon the trone of glorious fame,
Though spitfull death mans body kill, yet hurts he not his vertuous name:
By life or death, what so betides,
The state of vertue, never slides.
