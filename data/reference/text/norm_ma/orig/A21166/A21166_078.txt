Mine own good father thou art gone, thine ears are stopped with clay,
Thy ghost is fled, thy body dead, thou hearst not what I say:
Thy dearest friends may sigh and sob, thy children cry and call
Thy wife may wail, and not prevail, nor do the good at all.
Though reason would we should rejoice, and trickling tears restrain,
Yet kindliness and friendliness, enforce us to complain:
Thy life was good, our loss the more, thy presence cheered our heart,
Thy lack and absence turned therefore, our solace into smart.
I found thee both a kindly friend, and friendly father too,
Barnabie lacks breath, O cruel death, and couldst thou part us two:
But death derides my woeful words, and to my saying saith,
Thus foolish wight I did but right, I force no friend nor faith.
The Lord of life and Lord of death, my threatening hand did let,
Else when that he in cradle lay, I might have claimed my debt:
His corpse is clad with clods of the yearth, his soul doth soar on hye,
Before the throne of God above, whose servant he did die.
And thou his friend, and she his spouse, and they his children shall,
Behold the father, friend and mate, whose absence greeves you all:
But he nor can, nor will return, to thee, to her, or them,
For heaven is his, he lives in bliss, ye dwell with mortal men.
Ye dwell in dark, and dreadful den, in prison pent are ye,
He lives in light, and all delight, from thraldom frank and free:
wish not that he should come to you, for then ye do him wrong,
But wish that ye may go to him, the blessed saints emong▪
