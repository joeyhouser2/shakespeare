IN may by kind Dame Nature wills, all earthly wights to sing,
In may the new and coupled fowls, may joy the lively spring:
In May the Nightingale, her notes doth warble on the spray,
In May the birds their mossy nests, do timber as they may.
In May the swift and turning Hare her bagged belly slakes,
In May the little sucking Watts, do play with tender Flax:
All creatures may, in May be glad, no may can me remove,
I sorrow in May, since I may not, in May obtain my love.

The stately Harte in Maye doth mue, his old and palmed beams,
His state renews in May, he leaps to view Appollos streams:
In Maie, the Buck his horned tops, doth hang upon the pale,
In Maie, he seeks the pastures green, in ranging every Dale.
In Maie, the ugley speckled Snake, doth cast her lothsome skin,
In Maie, the better that he may increase his scaley skin:
All things in May I see, they may rejoice like Turtle dove,
I sorrow in Maie since I may not, in May obtain my love.

Now may I mourn in fruitful Maie, who may or can redress,
May may is sorrow since she that may, with holds my may a fresh:
Thus I must may in pleasant Maie, till I may May at will,
with her in Maie, whose may my life, now may both save and spill.
Contented hearts that have your hope, in May you may at large,
Untold your joys, expel your cares, and bask in pleasure barge:
Save I alone in Maie, that may lament for my behove,
I mourn in Maie, till that I may, in May obtain my love.
