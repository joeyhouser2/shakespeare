IN lothsome race, pursued by slippery life,
whose sugared guile, doth glistering joy present:
The careful ghost, oppressed sore with strife,
Yields ghostly groans, from painful passions sent.
The sinful flesh, that bears him here in vewe,
In steed of life, doth dreadful death pursue.

The way he seeth, by touch of merits grace,
Wherein to run, alas he gladly would:
But filthy flesh, his wretched dwelling place,
Doeth so rebel, at that which doe he should.
That silly soul, who feels his heavy need,
Can only will, but naught perform in deed.

Thy will through grace, doeth oft desire the good,
But all in vain, for that the fleshly foe:
Yields forth such fruits, as sinnes hath bred in bud,
And blindly sucks, the sap of deadly woe.
Esteeming shows of fickle fancies known,
And scorning fruit by grace, eternal sowen.

Though eye doth see, that death doth swallow all,
Both life and lust, and every sound delight:
Yet wretched flesh, through sin is made so thrall,
That nought it marks, apparent things in sight.
That might him train, to care of better grace,
Both doeth his bale, with greedy lust imbrace.

Then since desert, and all things wear away,
That nought remain, but fruit of grace or sin:
God build in us, such conscience, as can say,
This fruit not mine, but sin that dwelt in me,
For why to sin, I daily do in sight,
that unto Christ, I may revive my spright.
