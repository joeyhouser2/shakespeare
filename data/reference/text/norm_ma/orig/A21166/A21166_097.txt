What fond delight, what fancies strange,
what deep despight, what sudden change:
what stilling strife, what deep debates,
Do run so rife, in doltish pates.

Who views and sees, and takes no heed,
who seeks degrees, and can not speed:
In stead of joys, shall reap such woes,
As breed annoys, twixt friends and foes.

who wiving wants, and lives alone,
when thriving scants, is overthrown:
who seeks to thrive, and find no way,
May chance to strive, and mar the play.

who spends his wealth, and wins the wine,
Doth hurt himself, and help the swine:
who haunts the house, where Ale is sold,
May gain a croust, and lose his gold.

Who spins by spight, and reels to woe,
Who takes delight, in rolling so:
Doth dub himself, a drowsy head,
And brings drowsy fool to bed.

Who rides a loft, and cannot rule,
Who sits not soft, and keeps his stool:
Doth both content, themselves with wrong,
But wisemen will not use it long.
