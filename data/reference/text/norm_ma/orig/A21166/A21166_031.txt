I Read a Maiyng rime of late, delighted much my ear,
It may delight as many moe, as it shall read or hear:
To see how there is showed, how May is much of price,
And eke to May when that you may, even so is his advice.
It seems he ment to May himself, and so to use his skill,
For that the time did serve so well, in May to have his will:
His only May was ease of mind, so far as I can gesse,
And that his May his mind did please, a man can judge no less.

And as himself did reap the fruits, of that his pleasant May,
He wills his freend the same to use, in time when as he may:
He is not for himself it seems, but wisheth well to all,
For that he would they should take May, in time when it doth fall.
So use your May, you may, it can not hurtful be,
And May well used in time and place, may make you merry glee:
Modest Maiyng meetest is, of this you may be sure,
A modest Maiyng quietness, to Mayers doth procure.

Who may and will not take, may wish he had so doen,
Who may and it doth take, may think he took to soon:
So join your May with wisdoms lore, and then you may be sure,
Who makes his May in other sort, his vurest may procure.
Some May before May come, some May when May is past,
Some make their May too late, and some do make post hast:
Let wisdom rule I say your May, and thus I make an end,
And May, that when you list to May, a good May God you send.
