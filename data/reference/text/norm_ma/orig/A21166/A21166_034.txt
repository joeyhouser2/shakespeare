You Muses wear your mourning weeds, strike on the fatal drome,
Sound Triton out the trump of fame, in spite of Pareas dome:
Distil Parnassus pleasant drops possess Pierides place,
Apollo help with doleful tune, to wail this woeful case▪
Wring hard your hands, wail on your loss, lament the fate that fell,
With sobs and sighs to Saunders say, oh Saunders now farewell:
Whom Phaebus fed with Pallas pap, as one of Sibils seed,
Lo here where death did rest his corpse, the vermin foul to feed.
Whom Imps of Jove with Necter sweet, long in Libethres noursht,
Behold how dreadful death him brought, to the whence he came first:
Lycurgus he for learned laws, Radamantus race that ran,
An other Nector for advice, Zalucus fame that wan.
A Damon deare unto his frend, in faith like Phocion found,
A Cato that could counsel give, to Prince a subject sound:
Not Athens for their Solon sage, not Room for Numa wail,
As we for Saunders death have cause, in floods of tears to sail.
Nor Sparta card for Chilos death, nor proud Prienna prest,
To weep for Bias as we wail, our Saunders late possest:
His learned paths, his talents rare, so now by death appears,
As he that Salomon sought to serve, in prime and youthful years,
His counsel sad, his rules, his Laws, in country soil so wrought.
As though in Cuma he had bene, of sage Sibilla taught:
His vertuous life was such I say, as vertue did embrace,
By vertue caught in virtues school, to grow in virtues race.
Might tender babes, might orphantes weak, might widows rear the cry,
The sound thereof should pearce the clouds, to scale the empire sky:
To bid the Gods to battaile bend, and to descend in sight,
Though far unfit, and mates unmeet, with mortal men to fight.
Too late (alas) we wish his life, too soon deceives us Death,
Too little wit we have to seek, the dead again to breath:
What helpless is, most careless be, as Natures course doth show,
For death shall reap what life hath sowen, by nature this we know.
Where is that fierce Achilles fled, where is king Turnus shroud?
What is become of Priamus state, where is Periander proud?
Hector, Hanno, Hanniball, dead, Pompei, Pirrhus spilled,
Scipio, Cyrus, Caesar slain, and Alexander killed.
So long there Fortune fast did flow, and charged Fame to sound,
Till frowning Fortune foiled by fate, which fawning Fortune found:
Shun Fortunes feakes, shake Fortune of, to none is Fortune sound,
Sith none may say of Fortune so, I Fortune faithful found.
Behold where Fortune flowed so fast, and favoured Saunders lure,
Till fickle Fortune false again, did Saunders death procure:
Lo clothed cold in clods of clay, in drossy dust remain,
By fate returned from whence he came, to his mothers womb again,
Who wellnigh thirtie years was Judge, before a Judge did fall,
And judged by that mighty Judge, which Judge shall judge us all:
The heavens may of right rejoice, and earth may it bewail,
Sith heaven wan, and earth hath lost, the guide and ark of vail.
The gain is much, our loss is great, their mirth, our mone is such,
That they may laugh as cause do yield, and we may weep as much.
O happy he, unhappy we, his hap doth aye encrease,
Happy he, and hapless we, his hap shall never cease.
We live to die, he died to live, we want and he possest,
We bide in bands, he baths in bliss, the Gods above him blest:
Being borne to live, he lived to die, and died to God so plain,
That birth, that life, that death do shew, that he shall live again.
His youth to age, his age to death, his death to fame applied,
His same to time, his time to God, thus Saunders lived and died:
O happy life, O happier death, O ten times happy he,
Whose hap it was, such hap to have, a Judge this age to be.
Oh joyful time, Oh blessed soil, where Pallas rules with wit,
O noble state, O sacred seat, where Saba sage doth sit▪
Like Susan sound like Sara sad, with Hesters Mace in hand,
With Judithes sword Bellona like, to rule this noble land.
I had my will, you have your wish, I laugh, rejoice you may,
I wanne now much, you gain no less, to see this happy day.
Wherein I died, wherein you live, oh treble happy cost,
Wherein I joyed in glory great, wherein you triumph most.
Kneel on your knees, knock hard your breasts, sound forth the joyful drome,
Clap loud your hands, sound Echo say, the golden world is come.
Rejoice you Judges may of right, your mirth may now be such,
As never earst you Judges had, in England mirth so much.
Here Cuma is, here Sibill reigns, on Delphos seat to sit,
Here she like Phaebus rules, that can Gordius knot unknit.
I lived to Nature long inough, I lived to honour much,
I lived at wish, and died at will, to see my country such.
As neither needs it Numas laws, nor yet Apollos sword,
For mauger Mars yet Mars shall of this our Queen afeard.
O peerless pearl, O Diamond deare, O Queen of Queens farewell,
Your royal Majesty God preserve, in England long to dwell.
Farewell the Phoenix of the world, farewell my sovereign Queen,
Farewell most noble vertuous Prince, Mineruas mate I ween.
No Jewel, Gem, no Gold to give, no Pearls from Pactol•s lo,
No Persian Gaze, no Indian stone, no Tagus sands to show.
But faith and will to native soil, a live and dead I find,
My hart my mind, my love I leave unto my Prince behind,
Farewell you Nobles of this land, farewell you Judges grave,
Farewell my fellows, friends and mates, your Queen I say God save.
What rise in time, in time doth fall, what floweth in time doth ebb,
What lives in time, in time shall die, and yield to Parcus web.
The Sun to darkness shall turned, the stars from skies shall fall,
The Moon to blood, the world with fire shall consumed all.
As smoke or vapour vanish straight, as bubbles rise and fall,
As clouds do pass, or shadow shifts, we live, we die so all.
Our pomp, our pride, our triumph most, our glory great herein,
Like shattering shadow pass away, as though none such had bin.
Earth, Water, Air, and Fire, as they were earst before,
A lump confused, and Chaos called, so shall they once be more.
And all to earth, that came from earth, and to the grave descend,
For earth on earth, to earth shall go, and earth shall the end.
As Christ ascended up the clouds, so Christ in clouds shall come,
To Judge both good and bad on earth, at dreadful day of dome.
From whence our flesh shall rise again, even from the drossy dust,
And so shall pass I hope, unto the Mansion of the just.
