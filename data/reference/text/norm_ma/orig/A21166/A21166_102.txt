The wandering youth, whose race so rashly run,
Hath left behind, to his eternal shame:
The thriftless title of the Prodigal son,
To quench, remembrance of his other name.
Mate now devide, the burthen of his blame,
with me, whom wretchless thoughts enticed still:
To tread the tracked of his unruly will.

He took his childes part, at his fathers hands,
Of Gods free grace, his gifts I did receive:
He traveled far, in many foreign lands,
My restless mind, would never raging leave.
False queans did him, of all his coin bereave,
Fond fancies stuffed my brain with such abuse:
That no good hap could seek to any use.

They drave him out, when all his pense was spent.
My lusts left me, when strength with age was worn,
He was full fain, a Fermars hogs to tent:
My life misled, did reap deserved scorn,
Through hunger huge, where with his trips were torn,
He wisht for swaddes, even so wisht I most vain,
In fruitless pleasure, fondly to remain.

Now to come home with him, and pardon pray,
My God I say, against the heavens and thee,
I am not worthy, that my lips should say:
Behold thy handy work, and pity me,
Of mercy yet my soul, from faults set free.
To serve thee here, till thou appoint the time,
Through Christ, unto thy blessed joys to climb.
