The friendly shows, under whose shade, most craft doth often lurk▪
Enforceth me alas, with yernfull voice to say,
Woe worth the wily heads, that seeks the simple mans decay.

The bird that dreads no guile, is soonest caught in snare.
Each gentle harte devoid of craft, is soonest brought to care:
Good Nature soonest trapped, which gives me cause to say,
woe worth the wily heads, that seek the simple mans decay.

I see the serpent vile, that lurks under the green,
How subtilly he shrowds himself, that he may not be seen:
And yet his fosters bane, his learing looks bewray,
woe worth the wily heads that seeks, the simple mans decay.

Woe worth the feigning looks, on favour that we do wait,
woe worth the feigned friendly heart, that harbours deep deceit:
woe worth the Uipers brood, oh thrice woe worth I say,
All worldly wily heads, that seeks the simple mans decay.
