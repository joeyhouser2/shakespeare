Forlorn in filthy froward fate, wherein a thousand cares I find:
By whom I do lament my state, annoyed with fond afflicted mind.
A wretch in woe, and dare not cry,
I live, and yet I wish to die.
The day in dole, that seemeth long, to pass with sighs and heavy cheare:
And with these eyes I vewe the wrong, that I sustain by livyng here.
Where my mishaps as rise do dwell,
As plagues within the pit of hell.
A wailing wight I walk alone, in desart dens there to complain:
Among the savage sort to move, I flee my friends where they remain.
And pleasure take to shun the sight,
Where erst I felt my great delight.
A captive clapt in chains of care, leapt in the laws of lethal love:
My flesh and bones consumed bare, with crawling griefs full strange to prove.
Though hap doth bid me hope at least,
Whiles grass doth grow, yet starves the beast.
A sieged fort with forraine force, for want of aid, must yield at last,
So must my wearied pined corse, submit it self to bitter taste:
Of crawling care that cracks my brest,
Till hope of death, shall break my rest.
