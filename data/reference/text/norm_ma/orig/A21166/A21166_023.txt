Perhaps you think me bold that dare presume to teach,
As one that runs beyond his race, and rows beyond his reach,
Sometime the blind do go, where perfect sights do fall,
The simple may sometimes instruct, the wisest heads of all.

If needful notes I give, that unto vertue tend,
Me thinks you should of right, vouchsafe your listening ears to lend:
A Whetstone cannot cut, yet sharps it well we see,
And I though blunt, may whet your skills, if you attentife be.

First these among the rest, I wish you warely heed,
That God be served, your prince obeyed, and friends relieved at need:
Then look to honest thrift, both what and how to have,
At night examine so the day, that bed be thought a grave.

Seek not for others goods, be just in word and deed,
For got with shifts, are spent with shame, beleeve this as thy creed
Boast not of Natures gifts, nor yet of parents name,
For Uertue is the only mean, to win a worthy fame.

Ere thou doest promise make, consider well the end,
But promise past be sure thou keep, both with thy foe and friend:
Threat not revenge to much, it shows a cravens kind,
But to prevail, and then forgive, declares a noble mind.

Forget no friendships debt, wish to requite at least,
For God and man, yea all the world, condemns the ungrateful beast:
Bear not a friendly face, with hart of Judas kiss,
It shows, a base and vile conceit, and not where valure is.

Flye from a fawning flurt, and from a cogging mate,
Their loves breeds loss, their praise reproach, their friendship breeds but hate,
Seek not to lose by wiles, that law and duty binds,
They be but helps of Bankrupts heads, and not of honest minds.

The motions of the flesh, and Collers heat restrain,
For heaps of harms do daily hap, where lust or rage doth reign:
In diet, deed and words, a modest mean is best,
Inough sufficeth for a feast, but riot finds no rest.

And so to make an end, let this be borne away:
That vertue always be thy guide, so shalt thou never stray.
