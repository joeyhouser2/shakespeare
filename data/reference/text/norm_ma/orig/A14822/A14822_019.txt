O Golden bird and Phenix of our age,
whose sweet records and more then earthly voice
By wondrous force did then my grief asswage
When nothing else could make my heart retoyce,
Thy teunes (no doubt) had made a later end,
If thou hadst known how much they stood my frend.
When silence drowned the latter warbling neat,
A sudden grief eclipsed my former joy,
My life it self in calling Carons boat
Did sigh, and say, that pleasure brought annoy;
And blamed mine ear for listening to the sound
Of such a song, as had increased my wound.
My heaute heart remembering what was past
Did sorrow more then any tongue can tell;
As did the damned souls that stood agast,
when Orpheus with his wife returned from hell:
Yet who would think, that Music which is sweet,
In curing pains could cause delights te fleet?
