When Cupid is content to keep the skies,
He never takes delight in standing still,
But too and fro, and every where he flies,
And every God subdueth at his will,
As if his boaw were like to Fortunes wheel,
Him self like her, having no leaden heel.
When other whiles he passeth Lemnos Ile,
Unhappy boy he gybes the Clubfoote Smith,
Who threatens him, and bids him stay a while,
But laughing out he leaves him he forthwith,
And makes him self companion with the Wind•
To shew, his heals are of no leaden kind.
But in my self I have too trewe a proof:
For when he first espied my ranging Heart,
He Falcon like came sousing from aloof.
His swiftly falling stroake encreast my smart:
As yet my Heart the violence it feels,
Which makes me say, Love hath no leaden heals.
