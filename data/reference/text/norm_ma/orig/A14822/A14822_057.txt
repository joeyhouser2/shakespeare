SO great a Light hath set my mind on fire,
That flesh and boane consume with secret-flame,
Each vain dries up, wit yields to deep desire:
I scarce (alas) dare say, for very shame,
How fame my soul an interchange would make
Twixt this her present State and Limbo lake;
And yet she dread's, least when she paites from hence,
Her Heats be such, that Charon will retire,
And let her pass for prayer, nor for pence,
For fear his withered boat be set on fire;
So dangerous are the flames of Mighty Love
In Stix it self, in earth, or heaven above.
Wherefore deer Dame vouchsafe to rew my case,
And salve the soare which thou thy self hast made:
My Heats first grew by gazing on thy face,
Whose lights were such, that I could find no shade:
And thou my weary Soul bend all thy force,
By Plaints and Tears to move her to remorse.
