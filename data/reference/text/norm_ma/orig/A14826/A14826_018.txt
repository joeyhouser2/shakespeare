The private place which I did choose to wail,
And deer lament my loves pride was a grove:
Placed twixt two hills within a lowly dale,
Which now by fame was cald the vale of love.
The vale of love for there I spent my plainings,
Plaints that bewrayed my sick hearts bitter wounding:
Love sick hearts deep wounds with despair me paining,
The bordering hills my sorrowing plaints resounding.
Each tree did bear the figure of her name,
Which my faint hand upon their backs ingraved:
And every tree did seem herfore to blame,
Calling her proud that me of joys depraved.
But vain for she had vowed to forsake me,
And I to endless anguish must be take me.
