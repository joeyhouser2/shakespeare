Haand, hart, and eye, tucht thought and did behold,
The only glory that on earth doth grow:
Hand quaked, hart sighd, but eye was foolish bold,
To gaze till gazing wrought hearts grounded woe.
The object of these senses heavenly saint,
with such a majesty did me appall:
As hand to write her praise did fear and faint,
And hart did bleed to think me Beauties thrall.
But eye more hardy than the hand or hart,
Did glory in her eyes reflecting light:
And yet that light did breed my endless smart.
And yet mine eyes nill leave there former sight,
But gazing pine, which eye, hand, hart doth try,
And what I love, is but hand, hart, and eye.
