The tender buds whom cold hath long kept in,
And winters rage inforst to hide their head:
will spring and sprowt as they do now begin,
That every one will joy to see them spread.
But cold of care so nips my joys at root,
There is no hope to recover what is lost:
No sun doth shine that well can do it boot,
Yet still I strive but lose both toil and cost.
For what can spring that feels no force of ver,
what hower can flourish where no sun doth shine:
These balls deare love, within my brest I bear,
To break my bark and make my pith to pine.
Needs must I fall, I fade both root and rind,
My branches bow at blast of every wind.
