Three sorts of Serpents do resemble thee,
That dangerous eye-killing Cockatrice,
Th'inchaunting Syren, which doth so entice,
The weeping Crocodile: these vile pernicious three.

The Basilisk his nature takes from thee,
Who for my life in secret wait do'st lie,
And to my hart sendest poison from thine eye,
Thus do I feel the pain, the cause, yet cannot see.

Fair-maid no more, but Mayor-maid be thy name,
Who with thy sweet alluring harmony
Hast playd the thief, and stolen my hart from me.
And like a Tyrant makest my grief thy game.
Thou Crocodile, who when thou hast me slain,
Lamentest my death, with tears of thy disdain.
