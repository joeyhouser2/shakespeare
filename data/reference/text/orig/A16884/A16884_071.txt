Great things ſtill orewhelme themſelues by waight.
Greatneſſe like to the ſunnes reflecting powers,
The fier bred vapours naturally exhailes,
And is the cauſe that oft the euening lowers,
When foggy miſts enlarge their duskie ſailes.
That his owne beames he in the cloudes impailes,
And either muſt extinguiſh his owne light,
Or by his vertue cauſe his proper right.
To be huge is to be deadly ſicke.
O blinded Greatneſſe, thou with thy turmoile,
Still ſelling happy life, mak'ſt life a toile.
— He that ſtriues to manage mightie things,
Amidſt his triumphes, beares a troubled minde:
The greateſt hope the greateſt harueſt brings,
And poore men in content there glory finde.
The man that furthereth other men to thriue,
Of priuate greatneſſe doth himſelfe depriue.
