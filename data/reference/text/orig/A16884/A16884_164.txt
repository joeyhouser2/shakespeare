—Theſe two parts belong
Vnto true knowledge, words and teares haue force,
To mooue compaſsion in the ſauage mindes
Of brutiſh people reaſon wanting kindes.
Teares, vows, and prayers gaine the hardeſt hearts.
Teares worke no truce, but where the heart is tender.
Teares harden luſt, though marble weare with raine.
Seld ſpeaketh loue, but ſighes his ſecret paines,
Teares are his truch-men, words do make him tremble.
Teares cannot ſoften flint, nor vowes conuert.
A dolefull caſe deſires a dolefull ſong,
Without vaine art, or curious complement,
And ſquallid fortune into baſeneſſe flung,
Doth ſcorne the pride of wonted ornament.
