The fertile Nile which creatures new doth foame,
Long Rhodanus whoſe ſourſe ſprings from the skie,
Faire Ister flowing from the mountaines hie,
Diuine Scamander purpled yet with blood
Of Greekes and Troians which therein did lie:
Pactolus gliſtering with his golden flood,
And Tigris fierce, whoſe ſtreames of none may be withſtood.
Great Ganges and immortall Euphrates,
Deepe Indus, and Meander intricate,
Slowe Peneus and tempeſtuous Phaſides,
Swift Rhene, and Alpheus ſtill immaculate,
Oraxes feared for great Cyrus fate,
Tibris renowned for the Romains name.
The Cydnus ſtreame (who for his ſiluer flood,
Eſteemd a King) ran now with humane blood.
— Beſide their trickled ſoftly downe
A gentle ſtreame, whoſe murmuring waue did play
Amongſt the pumy ſtones and made a ſound
To lull himſelfe a ſleepe that by it lay.
The wearie traueller wandring that way,
Therein did often quench his thirſtie heate,
And then by it his wearie limmes diſplay,
Whiles creeping ſlumber made him to forget
His former paine, and wipe away his toylſome ſweate.
Faire Danubie is praiſd for being wide,
Nylus commended for his ſeuenfold head,
Euphrates for the ſwiftneſſe of the tide,
And for the garden whence his courſe is lead,
The bankes of Rhene with wines are ouerſpread.
Take Loyre and Po, yet all may not compare
With Engliſh Thameſis for building rare.
Great Nylus land where raine doth neuer fall.
Anon he ſtalketh with an eaſie ſtride,
By ſome cleare Riuers lillie paued ſide,
Whoſe ſands pure gold, whoſe pibbles precious gems,
And liquid ſiluer all the curling ſtreams:
Whoſe chiding murmure mazing in and out,
With Chriſtall ceſterns, moates a meade about.
And th'artleſſe bridges ouerthwart this torrent,
Are Rocks ſelfe-arched by the eating current.
Or louing palmes, whoſe luſtie females willing,
Their marrow-boiling loues to be fulfilling,
And reach their husband trees on th'other bankes,
Bow their ſtiffe necks, and ſerue for paſſing plankes.
