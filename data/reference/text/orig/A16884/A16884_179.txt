From idle witte, there ſprings a braine-ſicke will,
With wiſe men luſt, which fooliſh make a God,
This in the ſhape of vertue raigneth ſtill.
Will puts in practiſe what the wit deuiſeth.
Will euer acts, and wit contemplates ſtill,
And as from witte the power of wiſedome riſeth,
All other vertues daughters are of will.
Will is the Prince, and wit the counſellor,
Which doth for common good in councell ſit,
And when witte is reſolu'd, will lends her power,
To execute what is deuiſ'd by witte.
Will is as free as any Emperour,
Nought can reſtraine her gentle libertie,
No tyrant nor no torrent hath the power
To make vs will when wee vnwilling bee.
Euen as the will ſhould goodneſſe truely know,
VVe haue a will which that true good ſhould chooſe
Although will oft, when wit falſe formes doth ſhow,
Take ill for good, and good for ill refuſe.
It liues not in our power to loue or hate,
For will in vs is ouer-rul'd by fate.
A ſtronger hand reſtraines our wilfull powers,
A will muſt rule aboue this will of ours,
Not following what our vaine deſires doe woe,
For vertues ſake, but what wee onely doe.
—Headleſſe will true iudgement doth enſnare.
Selfe-will doth frowne, when honeſt zeale reproues,
Whereas our actions meaſure no regard,
Our lawleſſe will is made his owne reward.
—With a world of miſchiefes and offence,
Ʋnbridled will rebelles againſt the ſence.
Hee leaſt ſhould liſt that may doe what he will.
