Nature in which diuinitie doth ſhine,
Liuely preſenting vnborne deitie:
Is that ſame ſpirit of reaſon moſt diuine,
Which cauſeth euery naturall worke to be.
All things ſhe doth preſerue, and can refine
Muddy pollutions from impietie.
Philoſophy can teach no art nor ground,
Which Nature (elder borne) had not firſt found.
—Nature in mans heart her lawes doth pen,
Preſcribing truth to wit, and good to will,
Which do accuſe, or els excuſe all men,
For euery thought or practiſe good or ill.
Nature aboue all things requireth this,
That we our kind do labour to maintaine.
Nature which headlong into life doth throng vs
With our feete forward to our graue doth bring vs:
What is leſſe ours, then this our borrowed breath▪
We ſtumble into life, we go to death.
Inexplicable nature by the God of nature wroght,
Makes things ſeeme miracles to ſome, to ſome not wonders thoght.
And euery climates people both as they are men and liue,
Do differ: if obſeru'd, ſhe not admir'd doth giue
The workman rather thē the work extoll we, though in her
Not curiouſly, and all things to his prouidence refer.
Nature hath powr'd inough in each mans lappe,
Could each man learne to vſe his priuate happe.
— Markes deſcried in mens natiuitie,
Are natures faults, not their owne infamie.
Nature is Learnings eyes, ſhe natures thought,
Vſe wanting either, is imperfect made,
They without vſe, no better then a ſhade.
— Nature ſeemeth onely faire in chaunge.
—Where nature failes in ſtrength ſhe addes in wit.
Nature giues bewtie, fortune wealth in vaine.
—The deſire of nature is not vaine,
She couets not impoſſibilities,
Fond thoughts may fall into ſome Idle braine,
But one aſſent of all is euer wiſe.
Nature doth hate and ſhunne her contrarie.
—Nature teacheth euer
Who loues preferment, needs muſt loue the giuer.
