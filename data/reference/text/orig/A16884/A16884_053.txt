What God hath ſaid, that cannot but enſue,
Though all the world would haue it ouerthrowne:
When men ſuppoſe by fetches of their owne
To flie their Fate, they further on the ſame,
Like blaſts of winde, which oft reuiue the flame.
The heauens do rule in their continuall courſe,
That yeelds to Fate, that doth not yeeld to force.
Chaunce is vncertaine, fortune double faced.
—Demogorgon ruler of the Fates.
—The Fates can make
Waie for themſelues, their purpoſe to pertake.
—What the Fates do once decree,
Not all the gods can chaunge, nor Ioue himſelf can free.
—The lawes of Fate
Being grau'n in ſteele, muſt ſtand inuiolate.
Who can eſcape what his owne Fate hath wrought,
The work of heauens wil, ſurpaſſe all humane thought.
—Who can deceiue his deſtenie?
Or weene by warning to auoid his Fate?
That when he ſleepes in more ſecuritie
And ſafeſt ſeemes, him ſooneſt doth amate,
And findeth due effect, or ſoone or late
So feeble is the power of fleſhly arme.
—Indeed the Fates are firme,
And may not ſhrinke though all the word do ſhake:
Yet ought mens good endeuours them confirme,
And guide the heauenly cauſes to their cōſtant terme.
Each man they ſay his Fate hath in his hands,
And what he makes or marres to leeſe or ſaue,
Of good or euil, is euen ſelfe do, ſelfe haue.
The Fates farre off, foreſeene come gently neare.
Our Fate is not preuented though fore-knowne,
For that muſt hap decreed by heauenly powers,
Who worke our fall, yet make the fault ſtill ours.
—Fate
Keeps in eternall darke our fortunes hidden,
And ere they come to know them, tis forbidden.
All men are men in ignorance of Fate,
To alter chance, exceedeth humane ſtate.
The heauens do rule in their continuall courſe,
That yeelds to Fate, that doth not yeeld to force.
