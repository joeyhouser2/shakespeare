—Feare all arm'd from top to toe,
Yet thought himſelfe not ſafe enough thereby,
But fear'd each ſhadow mouing too and fro,
And his owne armes whom gliſtering he did ſpie,
Or claſhing heard, he faſt away did flie
As aſhes pale of hew, and winghie heeld,
And euermore on danger fixt his eie,
Gainſt whom he alwaies bent a brazen ſheeld,
Which his right hand vnarmed faithfully did weeld.
Who ſo for fickle feare from vertue ſhrinkes,
Shall in his life imbrace no worthy thing,
No mortall man the cup of ſuretie drinkes.
Feare is more paine then is the paine it feares,
Diſarming humane minds of natiue might:
Where each conceit an vgly figure beares,
Which were not euil, well viewd in reaſons light.
The gift being great, the feare doth ſtill exceed,
And extreame feare can neither fight nor flie,
But cowardlike with trembling terror die.
The feare of ill, exceeds the ill we feare.
Feare lendeth wings to aged folke to flie,
And made them mount to places that were hie.
Feare made the wofull child to waile and weepe,
For want of ſpeed, on foote and hands to creepe.
Feare in a fearefull heart, frets more then plagues that he feareth.
Feare that is wiſer then the truth doth ill.
Feare caſts too deepe, and neuer is too wiſe.
In vaine with terror is he fortified,
That is not guarded with firme loue beſide.
A fearefull thing to tumble from a throne.
Where crowned might, & croſſed right ſo near togither dwel
Behoues that forreſt flying feare whereof the Foxe doth tel,
Our factious Lancaſter & Yorke, thereof could witnes wel.
Thunder affrights the Infants in the ſchooles,
And threatnings are the conquerors of fooles.
Whom feare conſtraines to praiſe their Princes deeds,
That feare eternall, hatted in them feeds.
Feare miſinterprets things, each angury
The worſer way he fondly doth imply.
Weaknes is falſe, and faith in cowards rare,
Feare findes our ſhifts, timitidie is ſubtill.
Tis incident to thoſe whom many feare,
Many to them more greeuous hate to beare.
—He whom all men feare,
Feareth all men euery where:
(Hate inforcing them thereto)
Maketh many vndertake
Many things they would not do.
The only good that growes of paſſed feare,
Is to be wiſe, and ware of like againe.
A man to feare a womans moodie eire,
Makes reaſon lie a ſlaue to ſeruile feare.
Nothing ſeene fearefull, we the moſt ſhould feare,
Great amiſtes riſe before the greateſt raine:
The water deep'ſt, where we leaſt murmure heare,
In faireſt Cups men temper deadlieſt baine.
The nearer night, the ayre more cleare and ſtill,
The nearer to one deaths, leaſt fearing ill.
— Bloodleſſe, truſtleſſe, witleſſe feare,
That like an Aſpen tree, trembles each where,
She leads blacke terror, and blacke clowniſh ſhame,
And drowſie ſloth that counterfeiteth lame,
With ſnailelike motion meaſuring the ground.
Foule ſluggiſh drone, barren (but ſinne to breed)
Diſeaſed begger, ſteru'd with wilfull need.
The feare of euill doth affright vs more,
Then th' euill it ſelfe, though it ſeeme nere ſo ſore.
