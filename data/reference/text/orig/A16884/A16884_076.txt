Hate is the elder, loue the yonger brother,
Yet is the yonger ſtronger in his ſtate
Then th'elder, and him maſtereth ſtill in all debate.
Nor Hate nor loue, did euer iudge aright,
Innated hate will hardly be diſplaſte
Out of high hearts, and chiefly where debate
Happeneth amongſt great perſons of eſtate.
Hatred muſt be beguilde by ſome new courſe,
Where ſtates are ſtrong, & Princes doubt their force.
Spight bites the dead, that liuing neuer darde.
Sildome doth malice want a meane to worke.
Hate hits the hie, and windes force talleſt towers▪
Hate is peculiar to a Princes ſtate.
Hatred accompanies proſperitie,
For one man greeueth at an others good:
And ſo much more we thinke o•r miſerie,
The more that fortune hath with others ſtood.
So that we ſeld are ſeene as wiſedome would,
To bridle time with reaſon as we ſhould.
