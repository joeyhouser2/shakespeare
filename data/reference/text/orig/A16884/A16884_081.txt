He was an aged ſyre, hoary gray,
With lookes full lowly caſt, and gate full ſlowe,
Wont on a ſtaffe his feeble ſteps to ſtay,
Hight Humilta: they paſſe in ſtouping lowe,
For ſtraight and narrow was the way that he did ſhowe.
Humilitie to heauen, the ſtep, the ſtaire
Is, for deuotion, ſacrifice, and praier.
The bending knee in ſafetie ſtill doth goe,
When others ſtumble, as too ſtiffe to bowe.
As on the vnſauourie ſtocke, the Lillie is borne,
And as the Roſe growes on the pricking thorne,
So modeſt life with ſobs of grieuous ſmart,
And cries deuout, comes from an humble hart.
More honour in Humilitie, then ſafetie in walles,
Proud liuers proue not monuments, ſaue onely in their falles.
Ah God ſhield man that I ſhould clime,
and learne to looke aloft:
This reed is ripe, that oftentime:
great climers fall vnſoft.
In humble dale is footing faſt,
the trode is not ſo fickle:
And though one fall through heedleſſe haſte,
yet is his miſſe not mickle.
The lowly heart doth win the loue of all,
But pride at laſt, is ſure of ſhamefull fall.
