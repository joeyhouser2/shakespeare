WHat death maie bee, compared to Loue?
What grief therein, now doeſt thou proue?
My paines alas, who can expreſſe,
I ſee no cauſe of heauineſſe.
My Ladies looks, my woe hath wrought:
Then blame thyne eyes, that firſte hath ſought,
I burne alas, and blowe the fire,
A foole conſumes by his deſire,
What ſhall I doe than?
come out and thou can.
Alas I die,
what remedie?
My ſugred ſweete, is mixed with gall,
Thy Ladie can not doe with all:
The more I ſeeke, the leſſe I finde,
Then ſtriue not with the ſtreame and wind.
Her muſt I Loue, although I ſmarte,
With her owne ſworde, thou ſlaieſt thy hart
Suche pleaſaunt baits, who can refraine,
Suche baits will ſure breede thee greate paine,
What ſhall I doe than?
Come out and thou can.
Alas I die,
what remedie.
Her golden beames, myne eyes doe daze,
Vpon the Sunne thou maieſt not gaze:
She might reward my cruell ſmarte,
She thinks thou hardſt a fained harte.
She laughs to heare my wofull cries,
Forſake her then, in tyme bee wiſe:
No, no, alas, that maie not bee,
No wiſe man then, will pitie thee,
What ſhall I doe than?
come out and thou can.
Alas I die,
what remedie.
A liuyng death, loe thus I proue,
Suche are the fruits of froward Loue:
O that I might her Loue once gaine,
Thy gaine would not, halfe quite the paine.
Her will I Loue, though ſhe be coye,
A foole hym ſelf, will ſtill annoye:
Who will not die, for ſuche a one?
Bee wiſe at length, let her alone:
I can not doe ſo,
then be thy owne fo,
Alas I die,
what remedie.
