YE ſtately wightes, that liue in quiet reſt,
Through worldly wealth, which God hath giuen you.
Lament with teares and ſighes from doleful breſt:
The ſhame and power that vice obtaineth now.
Behold how God doth dayly profer grace,
Yet we diſdayne repentance to embrace.

The ſuddes of ſinne do ſoke into the mind,
And cancred vice doth vertue quite expel:
No change to good alas can reſting finde,
Our wicked harts ſo ſtoutly do rebel.
Not one there is that haſteth to amend,
Though God from heauen his dayly threates downe ſend.

We are ſo ſlow to change our blameful life,
We are ſo preſt to ſnach aluring vice:
Such greedy harts on euery ſide be rife,
So few that guide their will by counſel wiſe,
To let our teares lament the wretched caſe,
And call to God for vndeſerued grace.

You worldy wightes, that haue your fancies fixt,
On ſlipper ioy of terreine pleaſure here:
Let ſome remorſe in all your deedes be mixt,
Whiles you haue time let ſome redreſſe appeare.
Of ſodaine death the houre you ſhall not know,
And looke for Death although he ſeemeth ſlow.

Oh be no iudge in other mens offence,
But purge thy ſelfe and ſeeke to make thee free,
Let euery one apply his diligence,
A change to good within him ſelfe to ſee.
O God direct our feete in ſuch a ſtay,
From cancred vice to ſhun the hateful way.
