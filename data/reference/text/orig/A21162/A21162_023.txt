THE ſturdy Rocke for all his ſtrength, by raging Seas is rent in twayne:
The Marble ſtone is pearſt at length, with little droppes of drifling rayne.
The Oxe dooth yeeld vnto the yoke,
The Steele obeyeth the hammer ſtroke.

The ſtately Stagge that ſeemes ſo ſtout, by yalping hounds at bay is ſet:
The ſwifteſt bird that flees aboue, is caught at length in Fowlers net.
The greateſt Fiſh in deepeſt Brooke,
Is ſoone deceiued with ſubtill hooke.

•…e man him ſelfe, vnto whoſe will, all thinges are bounden to obey:
For all his witte and worthy ſkill, dooth fade at length and fall away.
There is nothing, but time doth waſt,
The Heauens, the Earth, conſume at laſt.

But Vertue ſittes triumphing ſtill, vpon the Trone of glorious Fame:
Though ſpitfull death mans body kill, yet hurtes he not his vertuous name.
By life or death, what ſo betides,
The ſtate of Vertue, neuer ſlides.
