THe fire ſhall freze, the froſt ſhall frie the frozen moūtains hie
What ſtraunge things hath dame natures force, to turne her courſe awrie
My Loue hath me left, and taken a newe man:
This is not ſtraunge, it happes ofte tymes, the truthe to ſcan,
The more is my paine,
her Loue then refraine.
Who thought ſhe would flitt,
eche one that hath witt,
Is this not ſtraunge,
light Loue will chaunge.
By ſkilfull meanes I here reclaime, to ſtoope vnto my lure,
Suche haggard Haukes will ſoare awaie, of them who can be ſure
With ſiluer bells and hoode, my ioye was her to decke,
She was full gorgde, ſhe would the ſoner giue the checke,
The more is my paine,
her Loue then refraine:
Who thought ſhe would flitt,
eche one that hath witt,
Is not this ſtraunge,
light Loue will chaunge.
Her chirpyng lips ſhould chirpe to me, ſwete words of her deſire
Suche chirpyng birdes who euer ſawe, to preach ſtill on one brire,
She ſaied ſhe loued me beſte, and would not till ſhe die,
She ſaied in wordes, ſhe thought it not, as tyme doeth trie.
The more is my paine,
her Loue then refraine:
Who thought ſhe would flitt,
eche one that hath witt,
Is not this ſtraunge,
light Loue will chaunge.
Can no man winne a woman ſo, to make her Loue endure,
To make the Foxe his wiles to leaue, what man will put in vre?
Why then there is no choiſe, but all women will chaunge,
As men doe vſe, ſo ſome women doe Loue to raunge.
The more is my paine,
her Loue then refraine,
Who thought ſhe would flitt,
eche one that hath witt,
Is not this ſtraunge,
light Loue will chaunge.
Sith ſlipper gaine falles to my lot, farewell that glidyng praie,
Sith that the dice doeth runne awrie, betymes leaue of thy plaie.
I will no more lamente, the thyng I maie not haue,
Then by exchaunge the loſſe to come, all ſhalt thou ſaue.
Loue will I refraine,
thereby thou ſhalt gaine,
With loſſe I will leaue,
ſhe will thee deceiue:
That is not ſtraunge,
then let her raunge.
