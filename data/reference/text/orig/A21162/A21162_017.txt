JN my accompt the promiſe that is vowed,
Among the good is holden ſuch a debt:
As he is thought no whit to be allowed,
That ſetteth light his promiſe to forget.
And for my part I will not linke in loue,
With fickle folke whoſe fancies ought remoue.

My happy gaine I do eſteeme for ſuch,
As fewe haue found in theſe our doubtful dayes:
To find a freend I thinke it be as much,
As to win a fort ful fraught of noble prayſe.
Of all the goods that there may be poſſeſt,
A faithfull freend I iudge to be the beſt.

O freendly league although to late begun,
Yet time ſhall trye our troth as well imployed:
And that we both ſhall ſee that we haue won,
Such faſtned faith as can not be deſtroyed.
By enuious rage or ſlaunders bitter blowe,
That alwayes ſeekes the good to ouerthrowe.
