A Hundreth ſonnes (in courſe but not in kind)
Can witneſſe well that I poſſeſſe no ioye:
The feare of death which fretteth in my mind
Conſumes my hart with dread of darke anoye.
And for eche ſonne a thouſand broken ſléepes
Deuide my dreames with freſh recourſe of cares:
The youngeſt ſiſter ſharpe hir ſheare ſhe kéepes,
To cut my thred, and thus my life it weares.
Yet let ſuch daies, ſuch thouſand reſtleſſe nights,
Spit forth their ſpite, let fates eke ſhowe their force:
Deathes daunting dart where ſo his buffet lights,
Shall ſhape no change within my friendly corſe:
But dead or liue, in heauen, in earth, in hell
I wilbe thine where ſo my carkaſe dwell.
