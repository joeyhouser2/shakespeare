I Lookt of late, and ſawe thée loke aſkance,
Vpon my dore, to ſée if I ſatte there.
As who ſhould ſay: If he be there by chance,
Yet maye he thinke I loke him euery where,
No cruell no, thou knoweſt and I can tell,
How for thy loue I layd my lokes a ſide:
Though thou (par caſe) haſt lookt and liked wel,
Some newe founde lookes amide this world ſo wide.
But ſince thy lookes my loue haue ſo in chaynd
That to my lokes, thy liking now is paſt:
Loke wh•re thou likeſt, and let thy hands be ſtaynd,
In true loues bloud, which thou ſhalt lack at laſt,
So looke, ſo lack, for in theſe toyes thus toſt,
My lookes thy loue, thy lookes my life haue loſt.
