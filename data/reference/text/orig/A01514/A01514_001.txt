WE prayſe the plough, that makes the fruiteleſſe ſoyle
To bring forth corne, (through helpe of heauenly might)
And eke eſteeme the ſimple wretches toyle,
VVhoſe painefull handes doe labour day and night.
VVe prayſe the ground, whereon the herbes do grow,
VVhich heale or helpe, our greeues and mortall paine,
Yea weedes haue worth, wherein we vertue know,
For natures Art, nothing hath made in vaine.
VVe prayſe thoſe floures which pleaſe the ſecrete ſenſe,
And do content, the taſt or ſmell of man,
The Gardners paynes and worke we recompence,
That skilfull is, or aught in cunning can.
But much more prayſe to Gaſcoignes penne is due,
VVhoſe learned hande doth here to thee preſent,
A Poſie full of Hearbes, and Flowers newe,
To pleaſe all braynes, to wit or learning bent.
Howe much the minde doth paſſe the ſenſe or ſmell,
So much theſe Floures all other do excell.
