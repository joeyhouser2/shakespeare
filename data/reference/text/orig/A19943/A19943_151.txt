WHat ſtrange aduenture? what now vnlook't for arriuall,
Hath drawne the Muſes from ſweete Booetia mountaines,
To chuſe our country, to ſeeke in London abiding?
Are faire Castalian ſtreames dride? ſtands Cyrrha no longer?
Or loue the Muſes, like wantons, oft to be changing?
Scarſe can I that ſuppoſe, ſcarſe thinke I thoſe to be Muſes.
No ſound of melody, no voyce but drery lamenting.
Yet well I wot too well, Muſes moſt dolefully weeping.
See where Melpomene ſits hidde for a ſhame in a corner.
Heare ye the carefull ſighes, fetcht from the depth of her entrailes?
There weepes Calliope, there ſometimes luſty Thaleia.
Ay me! alas, now know I the cauſe, now ſeeke I no further,
Heere lies their glory, their hope, their onely reioycing.
Dead lies worthy Philip, the care and praiſe of Apollo,
Dead lies his carcaſe, but fame ſhall liue to the worldes end.
