WE reade what paines the powers deuine,
Through wrath conceiued by ſome offence,
To mortall creatures they aſſigne
Their due deſartes for recompence.
What endleſſe paine they muſt endure,
Which their offences did procure.

A Gripe doth Titius Liuer teare
His greedie hungrie gorge to fill,
And Sisiphus muſt euer beare
The rowling ſtone againſt the hill.
A number moe in hell be found,
Which thus to endleſſe paine are bound.

Yet all the woe that they ſuſtaine,
Is nothing to the paine of me,
Which cometh through the proude diſdaine
Of one, that doth to loue repine:
Therefore I crie woe worth the houre,
Since firſt I fell in Venus power.

The gnawing gripes of irkſome thought,
Conſumes my heart with Titius griefe:
I alſo haue full vainly wrought,
With Sisiphus without reliefe.
Euen when I hope to ende my paine,
I muſt renue my ſute againe.

Yet will I not ſeeme ſo vntrue,
To leaue a thing ſo late begone:
A better happe may yet inſue,
The ſtrongeſt towres in time be wonne.
In time therefore, my truſt I place,
Who muſt procure deſired grace.
