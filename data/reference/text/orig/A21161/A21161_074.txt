WHat death maie be, compared to loue?
What grief therein, now doeſt thou proue?
My paines alas who can expreſſe,
I ſee no cauſe of heauineſſe.
My Ladies looks, my wo hath wrought:
Then blame thyne eyes that firſt haue ſought,
I burne alas, and blowe the fire,
A foole conſumes by his deſire,
What ſhall I do than?
come out and thou can.
Alas I die,
what remedie?
My ſugred ſweete, is mixed with gall,
Thy Ladie can not doe with all:
The more I ſeeke, the leſſe I finde,
Then ſtriue not with the ſtreame and winde.
Her muſt I loue, although I ſmarte,
With thy owne ſworde, thou ſlaieſt thy harte:
Suche pleaſaunt baites, who can refraine,
Suche beats will ſure brede the greate paine.
What ſhal I do than?
Come out and thou can.
Alas I die,
what remedie.
Her golden beames, myne eyes doe daze,
Vpon the Sonne thou maieſt not gaze:
She might reward my cruell ſmarte,
She thinks thou beareſt a fained harte.
She laughs to heare my wofull cries,
Forſake her then, in tyme be wiſe:
No no alas, that maie not bee,
No wiſe man then will pitie thee,
What ſhall I do than?
come out and thou can.
Alas I die,
what remedie.
A liuyng death, loe thus I proue,
Suche are the fruts of froward loue:
O that I might her loue once againe,
Thy gaine would not, halfe quite the paine.
Her will I loue though ſhe be coye,
A foole hym ſelf will ſtill anoye:
Who will not die for ſuche a one,
Be wiſe at length, let her alone:
I can not doe ſo,
then be thy owne foe,
Alas I die,
what remedie.
