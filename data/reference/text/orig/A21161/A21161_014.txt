IN my accompt, the promiſe that is vowed,
Among the good, is holden ſuch a debt:
As he is thought, no whit to be alowed,
That ſetteth light his promiſe to forget.
And for my part, I wyl not linke in loue,
With fickle folke, whoſe fancies oft remoue.

My happy gaine, I doo eſteeme for ſuch,
As fewe haue found, in theſe our doutful dayes:
To finde a freend, I thinke it be as much,
Aſte winne, a fort full fraught of noble praiſe.
Of all the goodes, that there may be poſſeſt,
A faithfull freend, I iudge to be the beſt.

O freendly league, although to late begunne,
Yet time ſhall try our troth, is well imployed:
And that we both ſhall ſee, that we haue wonne,
Such faſtned faith, as can not be deſtroyed.
By enuious rage, or ſlaunders bitter blowe
That ſeekes the good, to ouerthrowe,
