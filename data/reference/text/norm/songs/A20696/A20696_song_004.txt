Leave a wretch in whom all woe
Can abide to keepe no measure.
Merry flocke such one forgoe,
Unto whom Myrth is displeasure,
Only rich in measures treasure.

Yet alas before you go
Heare your wofull Maisters story,
Which to stones I else would shew,
Sorrow only then hath glory
When tis excellently sorry.

Stella, fayrest Shepherdesse,
Fayrest but yet cruelst ever.
Stella, whom the heau'ns still blesse,
Though against me she persever,
Though I blisse inherit never.

Stella, hath refused me:
Stella, who more Love hath proved
In this Catiffe hart to be
Then can in good to us be moved
Towards Lambe-kins best beloved.

Stella hath refused me
Astrophel, that so well served,
In this pleasant spring (Muse) see
While in pride Flowers be preseru'd
Himselfe only Winter-starved.

Why alas then doth she sweare
That she loveth me so deerely,
Seeing me so long to beare
Coales of Love that burne so cleerely,
And yet leave me hopelesse meerely.

Is that Love? forsooth I trow
If I saw my good Dogge grieved
And a help for him did know
My Love should not be believed
But he were by me relieved.

No she hates me (well away)
Fayning Love, somewhat to please me,
Knowing, if she should display
All her hate, Death soone would seize me,
And of hideous torments ease me.

Then my flocke now adew,
But alas, if in your straying
Heavenly Stella meet with you,
Tell her in your pittious blaying,
Her poore slaves just decaying.
