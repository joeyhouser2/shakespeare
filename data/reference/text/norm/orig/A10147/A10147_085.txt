LO here the state of every mortall wight,
See here, the fine, of all their gallant joyes:
Beholde their pompe, their beauty and delight,
Wherof they vaunt, as safe from all annoyes:
To earth the stout, the prowd, the ritch shall yeeld,
The weake, the meeke, the poore, shall shrowded lye
In dampish mould, the stout with Speare and Sheeld
Cannot defend, himselfe when he shall dye.
The prowdest wight, for all his lyvely showes,
Shall leave his pompe, cut of by dreadfull death:
The ritch, whose Hutch, with golden Ruddocks flowes,
At lenght shall rest, uncoynd in dampish earth:
By Natures law, we all are borne to dye,
But where or when, the best uncertayne be:
No time prefixt, no goods our life shall buye,
Of dreadfull death, no freends shall set us free.
We subiect be, a thousand wayes to death,
Small sicknesse moves the valiaunts hart to feare:
A litle push bereaves your breathing breath,
Of brave delights, wherto you subiect are:
Your world is vayne, no trust in earth you finde,
Your valyaunst prime, is but a brytle glasse:
Your pleasures vade, your thoughts a puffe of winde,
Your auncient yeres, are but a withered grasse.
