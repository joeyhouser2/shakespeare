Health I thee send, if he may give, that which himself doth misse:
For thy sweet brest, doth harbor whole, my bloody bale or blisse,
I neede no scribe, to scry my care, in restlesse rigor spread:
They that behold, my chaunged cheare, already judge me dead.
My baned limmes, have yeelded up, their woonted joy to dye:
My healthles hand, doth nought but wring, and dry my dropping eye,
The deadly day, in dole I passe, a thousand times I crave
The noysome night: agayne I wish, the dolefull day to have.
Each howre to me, most hatefull is, each place doth urge my wo:
No foode me feedes, close up mine eyes, to gastly grave I go.
No Phisickes art, can give the salve, to heale my paynfull part:
Save only thou, the salve and sore, of this my captive hart,
Thou art the branch that sweetly springs, whose hart is sound and true
Can only cheare me wofull wight, or force my want to rue.
Then give to me, the sap I thirste, which gift may give me joy,
I mean thy firme, and faythful love, whose want breeds mine annoy,
Remember yet sure freendship had, ypast betweene us twayne
Forget him not, for love of thee, who sighes in secret payne.
I oft do seeme in company, a gladsome face to beare,
But God thou knowst my inward woes, and cares that rent me there:
And that I may, gush out my greefe, in secret place alone,
I bid my freends farewell in haste, I say I must be gone.
Then haste I fast, with heavy hart, in this my dolefull case:
Where walkes no wight, but I alone, in drewsie desart place,
And there I empt, my laden hart, that sweld in fretting mone:
My sighes and playnts, and panges I tell, unto my selfe alone.
What shall I say? do aske me once, why all these sorowes be▪
I answere true, O foe or freend, they all are made for thee.
Once knit the lynck, that love may last, then shall my dollors ceas•
It lyes in thee, and wilt thou not, the yeelding wight release?
O would to God, it lay in me, to cure such greefe of thine:
Thou shouldst not long, be voyd of helpe, if twere in power of mine,
But I would run, and range in stormes, a thousand miles in payne:
Not fearing foyle, of freends to have, my countenance whole agayn
And wilt thou then, all mercylesse, more longer torment me?
In drawing backe, sith my good helpe, is only whole in thee?
Then send me close, the hewing knife, my wider wound to stratch:
And thou shalt see, by wofull greefe, of life a cleane dispatch.
When thou shalt say, and proove it true, my hart entirely lou'd,
Which lost the life, for countnance sweet fron whon he never mou'd
Write then upon my wofull Tombe, these verses graven above,
Heere lyes the hart, his truth to trie, that lost his life in love.
Loe, save or spill, thou mayst me now, thou sitst in judgment hie,
Where I poore man, at Bar do stand, and lowd, for life do cry.
Thou wilt not be, so mercylesse, to slea a loving hart▪
Small prayse it is to conquer him, that durst no where to start,
Thou hast the sword, that cut the wound, of my unhol•en payne:
Thou canst and art, the only helpe, to heale the same agayne.
Then heale the hart, that loves thee well, until the day he dye:
And firmely fast thy love on him, thats true continually,
In thee my wealth, in thee my woe, in thee too save or spill:
In thee me lyfe, in thee my death, doth rest to worke thy will.
Let vertue myxt, with pitty great, and loving mercy save
Him, who without thy salve, so sicke, that he must yeeld to grave,
O salve thou then, my secret sore, sith health in thee doth stay:
And graunt with speed, my just request, whose want works my decay
Then shall I blesse, the pleasant place, where once I tooke thy glove,
And thanke the God, who gives thee grace, to graunt me love for love.
