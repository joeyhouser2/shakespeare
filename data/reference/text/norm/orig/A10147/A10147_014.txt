BE stedfast to thine own
As he is unto thee,
Regard not men un knowen
But love thine own truly
For oft deceyts are sowen
By them that unknowen be
Wherfore cast of the rest:
And thine own love thou best.

For though that their false suite
Seeme pleasant in thine eare,
Thou knowst oft times ill fruit
A pleasant tree doth beare.
If thou chaunce to repute
A rotten Apple cleare,
Better to love thine own
And forsake men unknowne.

Thou doost well understand
These wordes not spoken seilde
More suer a birde in hand,
Then twenty in the feild.
Thou knowest thine own sure band
And how that it hath helde
Then chaunge it for no new:
But love him that is trew.

If suters do thee move
Or dayly to thee write,
Yet graunt to them no love
Their paynes for too requite.
But thinke it doth behoove
Thee alwayes to do right
Then must thou love thine own
And forsake men unknowne.

This counsayle I thee give
As farforth as I can,
As I that whiles I live
Wilbee thine only man.
For sure it would me greene,
To see thee out of frame
Or chaunge at any time:
Thine own not to be thine.

Thus written by thine own
To thee with all his harte,
Disiringe the unknowen
Of thee may have no part.
For if sutch chaunge be sowen
No doubt thou killest my hart
Wherfore I say beware:
Alwayes the unknown snare.
