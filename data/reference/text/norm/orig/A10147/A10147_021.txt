ALL wealth I must forsake, and pleasures eke forgo,
My life to ende in wo and greefe, my desteny is so
For where I had perfixt, with sute to win my joy,
I found I had right speedy death, all welth for to distroy.
Whose Image lo I am, though lyving I appeare,
Both body and soule be seperate, my heaven it is not here.
My harte I have bestowed, wheras it is not found
Thou body then depart thou hence, why pleasurest thou the ground?
And Death draw thou me neare, O Death my dearest freend,
Then with thy dart, shoot through my hart, my sorrows so to ende▪
And when that death did hears the thing that I did crave,
He weighed me, even as I was, a man fit for the grave.
Come follow me sayth he, thou man be not agast,
He that delighteth in earthly things, shall feele these panges at last
All ye then that list to love, this lesson learne by me,
Or ye begin, noate well, the ende, is payne and misery.
