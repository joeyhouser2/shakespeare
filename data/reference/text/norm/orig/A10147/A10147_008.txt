How can the criple get, in running race the game?
Or he in fight defend himselfe, whose armes are broken lame?
How can th'imprisoned man whofe legs be wrapt in chaynes,
Thinke this his life a pleasant time, who knoweth nothing but paines?
So how can I reioyse, that have no pleasant thing,
That may revive my doulfull sprits, or cause me for to singe.
My legs be lame to go, mine armes cannot embrace,
My hart is sore, mine eyes be blinde, for lacke of Fortunes grace.
All this is Fortunes fault, that keepes these sences so,
She may aduaunce them if she list, and rid them of this wo.
It is her cruell will, alwayes on me to lower,
To kepe fron me her pleasant giftes, to make me know her power
Alas, alas, fi• Fortune, f•e: why art thou so unkinde,
To me that fayne would be thy sonne, and ever in thy minde?
Now do I thee beseech, with pleasures me to frayght,
To temper this my wofull life, or els to kill me strayght.
