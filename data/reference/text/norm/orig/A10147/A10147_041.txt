Behold you Dames the raigne in fames, whose lookes mens harts do leade
And triumph in the spoyle of those, upon whose brests you trede.
A myrt or make of M, whose molde, Dame Nature in disdayne,
To please her self, and spight her foes, in beauty raysd to raigne:
Whose sunny beames and starry eyes, presents a heavenlyke face,
And shewes the world a wonderous worke, sutch are her giftes of grace
In forhed, feature beareth, brunt in face doth favor guyde,
In lookes is life, in shape is shame, in cheekes doth coulor hyde:
In boddy seemelynesse doth shew, in wordes doth wisdome shade,
All partes of her doth prayse deserve, in temprance is her trade.
In humble porte is honor plaste, in face is maydens smyles
Her life is grafte with Golden giftes, her deedes devoyd of gyles.
And as the Star to Marriners, is guyde unto the Port,
So is this M, a heavenly joy, to Lovers that resort:
Who run and rome with inward wounds, and folded armes acrosse,
And hide their harms with clokes of care, and feed their hope with losse.
Her lookes doth lift above the skyes, her frowns to Hel doth throw
All sues to her, she seekes on none, that daily proofe doth show:
Wherfore her saying late set forth, she burnt and could not flee,
Though ment in prayse, yet far amis, I take it written be.
She is none such as if she would, that any would disdayne:
But for the smartes of others greefes, of pitty she did playne,
As one most lothe of any lyfe, for love of her be loste,
Or that with blud or cruell deedes, men write her beauties boste:
For mercy is in M, her brest, and modest is her life,
A courtuous mayd, and like to proove, a constant worthy wife.
