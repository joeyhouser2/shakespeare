AY me, ay me, I sighe to see, the Sythe a fielde,
Downe goeth the Grasse, soone wrought to withered Hay:
Ay me alas, ay me alas, that beauty needes must yeeld,
And Princes passe, as Grasse doth fade away.

Ay me, ay me, that life cannot have lasting leave,
Nor Golde, take holde, of everlasting joy:
Ay me alas, ay me alas, that time hath talents to receyve,
And yet no time, can make a suer stay.

Ay me, ay me, that wit can not have wished choyce,
Nor wish can win, that will desires to see:
Ay me alas, ay me alas, that mirth can promis no reioyce,
Nor study tell, what afterward shalbee.

Ay me, ay me, that no sure staffe, is given to age,
Nor age can give, sure wit, that youth will take:
Ay me alas, ay me alas, that no counsell wise and sage,
Will shun the show, that all doth marre and make.

Ay me, ay me, come time, sheare on, and shake thy Hay,
It is no boote, to baulke thy bitter blowes:
Ay me alas, ay me alas, come time, take every thing away,
For all is thine, be it good or bad that growes.
