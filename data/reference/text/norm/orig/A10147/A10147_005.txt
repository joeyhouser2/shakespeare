Twice hath my quaking hand withdrawen this pen away
And twice againe it gladly would, before I dare beewray
The secret shrined thoughts, that in my hart do dwell,
That never wight as yet hath wist, nor I desire to tell.
But as the smoothered cole, doth wast and still consume,
And outwardly doth geve no heate, of burnyng blaze or fume:
So hath my hidden harmes, been harbred in my corpce,
Till faintyng limmes and life and all, had welnigh lost his force:
Yet stand I halfe in doubt, which of these two to choose,
To hide my harmes still to my hurt, or els this thraldome loose.
I will lay feare aside, and so my tale beginne:
Who never durst assaile his foe: did never conquest win.
Lo here my cause of care to thee unfolde I will:
Help thou Minerua, graunt I pray, some of thy learned skill.
Help all you Muses nine, my wofull Pen to write:
So stuffe my verse with pleasant wordes, as she may have delight,
With heedyng eares to reade my greeif and great unrest:
Some wordes of plaint may move perhaps, to pitty my request.
Oft have I hard complaint, how Cupid beares a sway
In brittle youth, and would commaund: and how they did obay.
When I with skorning eares did all their talke dispise:
But well I see the blinded boy: in lurking den he lies,
To catch the careles sorte: awayting with his Darte:
He threw at me when I unwares, was wounded to the harte.
To speake and pray for helpe, now love hath me constrainde:
And makes me yeeld to serve the sorte, that lately I disdainde.
Sith beggars have no choyce: nor neede had ever law
The subiecte Oxe doth like his yoke: when he is driven to draw.
That Ruphilus this wrote: thou wonder wilt I know,
Cause never erst in lovinge vearse: my labor I bestowe,
Well, woful love is mine, and weeping lines I wright,
And doubtfull wordes with driery cheere: beseemes a careful wight
O thou Elrisa fayre, the beuty of thine eyes
Hath bred such bale within my drest, and cau'sde such strife to ryse.
As I can not forget: until devouring death
Shall leave to me a senceles goast: and rid my longer breath,
Or at the least that thou: do graunt me some releefe
To ease the greedy gripes I feele, and end my great mischeefe.
As due to me by right, I can no mercy crave,
Thou hast the power to graunt me life: refuse not for to save.
Put to thy helping hand, to salve the wounded sore,
Though thou refuse it for my sake: yet make thine honour more,
Too cruell were the facte: if thou shouldst seeke to kill
Thy faythful freend that loves thee so: and doth demaund no ill.
Thy heavenly shape I saw: thy passing bewty bright,
Enforst me to assay the bayt: where now my bane I bight
I nought repent my love: nor yet forthinke my facte,
The Gods I know were all agreed: and secretly compacte.
To frame a worke of prayse: to show their power devine
By good aduice this on the earth: above the rest to shine.
Whose perfecte shape is such: as Cupid feares his fall,
And every wight that hath her seene, I say (not one) but all
With one consent they cry: lo here dame Venus ayer,
Not Danae nor she dame Lede: was ever halfe so faire.
Though Princes sue for grace: and ech one do thee woo,
Mislyke not this my meane estate: wherwith I can nought do,
As highest seates we see: be subiect to most winde,
So base and poore estates we know, be hateful to the minde.
The happy meane is mine: which I do haply holde,
Thy honor is to yeeld for love: and not for heape of golde.
If ever thou hast felte: the bitter panges that stinges
A lovers br•st: or knowest the •ares, that Cupid on us flinges.
Then pitty my request: and wayle my wofull case,
Whose life to death with hasty wheeles: do toumble on apace.
Uouchsafe to •ase the paine: that love on me doth whelme,
Let not thy freend to shipwracke go: sith thou doost hold his helme.
Who yeel•eth all he hath: as subiect to thy will,
If thou commaund he doth obey, and all thy heastes fulfill.
But if thou call to minde: when I did part thee fro,
What was the cause of my exile: and why I did forgo
The happy life I held, and lost there with thy sight,
Well mayst thou wayle thy want of troth: and rue thy great unright
If thou be found to fayle thy vow that thou hast sworne
Or that one jot of my good will, out of thy minde be worne.
Or if my absence long: to thy disgrace hath wrought me
Or hindering tales of my back freends: unto such state hath brought me.
I can and will accurse the cause of my ill speede:
But well, I hope, my feare is more: then is the thing indeede.
Yet blame me not though I do stand somewhat in feare
The cause is great of my exile, which hardly I do beare.
Who hath a sternles ship amidst the trustles Seaes,
Full greedely desires the porte: where he may ride at ease.
Thy bewty bids me trust, unto thy promise past,
My absence longe and not to speake: doth make me doubt as fast.
For as the sommers sonne, doth make each thing to spring:
Even so the frosen winters blast, as deadly doth them wring.
Unsuer thus I live in dreade I wot not why
Yet was there never day so bright, but there be cloudes in sky.
Who hath of puer Golde, a running streame or flud
And is restraind for comming nigh, this treasure great and good.
He must abide a time: till Fortune graunt him grace,
That he have power by force to win: his riche desired place.
I neede not thus to do: nor yet so much mistrust,
I know no time can change thy minde: or make thee be uniust.
No more then water soft, can stir a stedfast rocke:
Or seely flyes upon their backes can beare away a blocke.
Each beast on earth we see: that living breath doth draw,
Be faythfull found unto their mates: and keepes of love the law.
My wretched life to ease: when I do seke to turne,
Thy bewty bright doth kindle me, in greater flame to burne.
No day, no night, nor time, that geves me mirth or rest,
Awake, asleape, and at my meales, thou doost torment my brest.
Though weary lothsome lyfe: in care and wo have clad me,
Remembrance of thy heavenly face, gives cause again to glad me.
Thus Joyfull thoughtes a while, doth lessen much my payne
But after calme and •ayer tides, the stormes do come agayne.
And I in cares do flame, to thinke of my exile,
That I am barred from thy sight: I curse and ban the while.
Would God I had the craft a Laborinth to frame,
And also had a Mynotaure: inclosed in thesame:
And that our enemies all, might therin take some paine,
Till Dedales line I did them bringe, to helpe them out againe.
Then should my sorowes seace, and drowne my deepe dispaire,
Then should my life be blest with Joyes: and raisde above the ayre▪
But as the mazed birde, for feare dare skantly fly,
When he hath scapte the Falcons foote: even so I know should I
Scarse able be to speake, or any word to say,
Least Argus wayting jelous eyes, might haply me bewray
But oh Elrisa mine, why do I stir such war
Within my selfe to thinke of this: and yet thy love so far?
Why rather should not I: give up the life I have
And yeeld my weary wretched corps: unto the gaping grau
If I hopte not that thou with faith didst binde thy life,
This hand of mine with bloody sworde, should stint my cruel strife▪
No length of lingring time: no distance can remoove,
The fayth that I have have vowed to thee: nor alter once my love.
Beleeve this to be true, that streames shall soner turne,
Or frosen Ice to fier coales, on blasing flame to burne.
Then I will seke to change: or alter once my minde,
All plagues I pray may fall on me, if I be found unkinde.
Or if I meane to swarue while I have living breath
God graunt my end then may be such as Agamemnons death.
I wish thy life no harme: but yet I woulde thou knew
The wofull ende that Cressed made, because she was untrue.
These angry gods or men, asonder that do set us,
Shall never pearce our mindes in twaine nor eke to love can let us
As well they may devide the fier from the flame,
And every beast that now is wilde, as soone shalbe made tame.
Let not this pistle long, my sute with thee deface,
Who pleadeth for his life thou knowest: at large must tel his case.
And all these wordes I write, to one effect do tende,
I am all thine, and not mine own: and herewithal to ende.
I pray thee to regarde: thy health and my request,
And that my love do never fleet out of thy secret brest.
