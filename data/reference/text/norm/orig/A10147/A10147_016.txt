Since needes ye will me singe, give eare unto the voyce,
Of me pore man your bond seruant, that knoweth not to reioyce.
Consider wel my care, my paine and my unrest:
Which thou with force of Cupids Dart hast grafted in my brest.
Heale, and withdraw from me, the venim of that Darte
Have pitty, and release this wo, that doth consume my hart:
The greatnes of my greefe, doth bid me seeke release
I seeke to finde to ease my payne, yet doth my care encrease.

I cease not to beholde, that doth augment my payne:
I see my selfe I seeke my wo, yet can I not refrayne.
That should my wo release, doth most encrease the same,
The colde that should acquench the heat, doth most enrage the flame
My pleasure is my payne, my game is most my greefe
My cheefe delite doth worke my wo, my hart is my releefe
Sutch haps doth hap to them, that happeth so to love,
And hap most harde: so fast to binde, that nothing can remoove.

For when the harme is fixed, and rooted in the hart,
No tongue can tell, nor pen may write, how grevous is the smart
I have thought love but play, until I felte the sore,
But now I felte a thousand greefes I never felt before.
To tell what paynes I bide, if that I could devise,
I tel the truth, beleeve me wel, the day will not suffise
Graunt now therfore some rest, since thus thou hast me bound,
To be thine own, til body mine, lye buried under ground.
