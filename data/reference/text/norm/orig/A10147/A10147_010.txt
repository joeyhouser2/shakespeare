The hugie heape of cares, that in this world I finde,
The sodayne sighes that sore molest my hart
The foolish fansies that still run in my minde:
Makes me to lay all joy and myrth apart,
Lamenting still the causes of my smart.
But oh, alas, the more I weepe and wayle,
The more my greefe to me seemes to prevayle.

The more I seeke my pinchinge panges to swage,
By divers wayes, such as I thinke be best
The more it frets, the more it gins to rage,
So that my senceles head can take no rest:
Ah seely wretch, what doth thee thus mollest
Or what doth thus perturbe thy restlesse braynes,
And from thy harte all worldly joye detaynes.

Alas what this should be I can not tell,
My youthfull yeares can skill of no such change
But if some ugly shape of fury fell:
Or wicked wight that in this world doth range
Hath witched me with this disease so strange.
Or Cupid with his force of cruell dart,
Hath stricken me and wounded thus my hart.

Hath Cupid then sutch power on mortall wightes?
And strikes the blinded boy his dart so sure?
That no man can avoyd his subtill s•ightes,
Nor ought agaynst his fury may indure?
Hath Venus force men thus for to allure?
And why then? doth she not her sonne commaund
To shoote alike and strike with equall hand?

Is this the guise of powers that raigne above,
Us seely soules in snares thus for to trap
And care they not to yeeld us death for love?
Joy they in woes our corses for to trap?
And passe they not what unto us doth hap?
Can Gods above to man beare any hate,
Or do they mocke and jest at our estate?

Ah foolish foole? what fancy rules thy head.
Or what doth cause thee now this talke to move?
What fury fell doth thee poore wretch now lead?
To rayle on all the Gods doth it behoove?
Sith it is only Cupid God of love.
That guiltlesse she with stroke of goulden shafte,
Hath wounded thus and thee of joyes berafte.

Even as the slender Barke that long is tost
By surging waves cast up from deepest seas:
And Saylars still in daunger to be lost,
Do hale and pull in hope to take their ease:
When stormy fluds begin once to appease.
Even so fare I beeing in Cupids power
In hope at last to see that happy hower.

Wherin I shall my wished joyes obtayne,
And placed be within her gentill hart,
Then shall I take my sorrowes all for gayne.
When I have her that causeth now my smart,
Then farewell Cupid with thy cruell darte
And welcome she that pearst me with her sight,
She is my Joy, she is my hartes delight.
