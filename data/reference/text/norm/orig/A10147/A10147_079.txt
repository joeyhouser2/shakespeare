Behold me here whose youth, to withered yeres,
Doth bow and bend, compeld by crooked age:
See here my lyms, whose strength benumbde weres,
Whose pleasure spent, gray heares, bids to be sage.

But loe to late I lothe my life lewd spent,
And wish in vayne, I had foreseene in youth:
These drowsie dayes, which mooves me to lament
My idle youth prou'd, what therof ensueth.

Unstorde olde yeres, must serve for lusty prime,
These feebled joynts, must seeke to serve their want:
With tedious toyle, because I usde not time,
Loe thus I live, suffisde perforce to scant.

In flaunting yeres, I flaunting florisht forth,
Amid delight, puft up, with puffing pryde:
Meane garments then, I deemed nothing worth,
Nay, scace the best, might serve, my flesh to hide.

I thought them foes, which tolde me of my fault,
And judgd them speake, of rigor, not good will:
Who toulde of gayne, me thought for hire did hault,
Then loe, I lothde what now I wish by skill.

Experience mooves me mone, the more my greefe,
In lyvely yeres, because I did not shun
Such idle steps, least voyd of such releefe,
As might have helpt my age, now youth is dun.

But what prevayles to wish I would I had,
Sith time delayd, may not be calde agayne:
A guerdon just, (for such as youth too bad
Consumes, (it is) in time therfore take payne.

Seeke how in youth to serve contented age,
Learne, how to lead, your life in vertues lore:
Beholde you me, attacht with death his page,
Constraynd through want, my lewdnes to deplore.

What greefe more great, unto a hauty hart,
Then is distresse, by folly forste to fall:
What care more cruell or lothsom, (to depart
From wealth to want) it greeves us to the gall.

But what avayles to boast, or vaunt of vayne?
What profit ist, to prayse a passed pryde?
Sith it consum'd, is but a pinching payne,
A heape of harmes, whose hurt I wretch have tryde.

A direfull dreed, a surge of sorowing sobs,
A carking care, a mount of mestive mone:
A sacke of sin, coucht full of cankered knobs,
A wavering weed, whose force is soone orethrone.

For proofe behold, the boast of breathing breath,
See see how soone, his valiaunst vaunt doth vade:
Our pleasant prime, is subiect unto death,
By vices urgde, in waves of wo to wade.

I know the state, and trust of every tyme,
I see the shame, wherto each vice doth cum:
Therfore (by me) learne how to leave such crime,
Foe•ix quem faciunt, aliena pericula cautum.

Let me your Mirror, learne you leave whats lewd,
My fall forepassed, let teach you to beware:
My auncient yeres with tryall tript, have vewd,
The vaunt of vice, to be but carking care.
