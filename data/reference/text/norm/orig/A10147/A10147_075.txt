Now cease to sing your Syren songes, I leave ech brave delight
Attempt no more the wounded corps, which late felt fortunes spight:
But rather helpe to rue, with sorowing sobs come mone,
My lucklesse losse from wealth to woe, by fickle fortune throwne.
I once had freends good store, for love, (no drosse I tryde)
For having lost my goods on Seas, my freends would not abide,
Yet having neede I went to one, of all I trusted moste:
To get releefe, he answerd thus, go packe thou pevish poste.
His wordes did pearce my tender brest, and I as mazde did stand
Requesting him with pitteous plaints, to give his helping hand:
Content thy selfe (quoth he) to serve my own estate,
I have not I, yet am I greeu'd to see thy lucklesse fate.
Ah fie of fawning freends, whose eyes attentive be,
To watch and warde for lukers sake, with cap and bended knee:
Would God I had not knowne, their sweet and sugered speach,
Then had my greefe the lesser bin, experience me doth teach.
