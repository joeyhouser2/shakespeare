The tedious toyle, the cares which Lovers taste,
The troubled thoughts, which moves their mindes to feare:
The pinching pangs, the dole which seemes to waste,
Their lothsome life, deepe plungd in gulfes of care:
Would moove ech shun, such snares of vayne delight,
Which irksome be, though pleasant to the sight.
The minde full fraught, with care enioyes no ease,
A boyling brest, desires unlawfull lust:
The hart would have, what best the minde doth please,
And fancy craves, the thing which is uniust.
Beside each frown, which eftsoones moves them deeme,
They abiect are, if sad their Lovers seeme.
Or if occasion shun, their usuall sight,
Not seene, they thinke themselves unminded be:
And then in dumps, as mazd they leave delight,
And yeeld to greefe, till one, each others see:
So that with feare, their mindes are alwayes fraught.
That live in love, experience some hath taught.
Each lowring frown, from mirth doth move the minde,
One jesting worde, procures a thousand woes:
So that lyke greefe or more, through sight they finde,
Then absence sure, such cares fro fancy flowes:
Such goring gripes, such heapes of hideous harmes,
Such sorowing sobs, from daunted lovers swarmes.
Rosamond a spowsed Dame, her husbands death procurde,
For speaking but a worde in jest:
Itrascus too, full thyrty yeares indurde,
The panges of love, within his boyling brest:
So that in greefe they harbor, still their mindes are cloyd with care,
They dive in dole, they plunge in payne, and live in cruell feare.
And divers moe, as Axeres whose beauty passing faire,
So Iphis hart, and boyling brest allurde:
That for her sake, he liude in extreame care,
And cruell greefe, while breathing breath indurde:
But at the length, disdayne upon a tree,
He honge himselfe, where sh•e his corps might see.
