YE grisly ghostes which walke below in black Cocistus Lakes,
Mi•s D•tis dennes, Erebus Dames, with heare of ugly Snakes
Medusa with thy monstrous mates, assist me now a while,
In dyre wamenting verse to shew, and drierie dolefull stile.
The fayre untimely fatall ende of Fletcher, now by death,
Unto the Ayre his soule with Jove, resignde his latest breath:
Whose life fu•l d•e we must commend, as it deserves the same,
And conuersation to each one, did seldome meryt blame.
A faythfull freend to each he was, to none an oppen foe,
Unto his Prince a subiect true, till fates had lodgd him loe.
His actes did tend to no mans harmes, no Parasite to prayse,
For greedy gayne but still the troth, mayntaynd at all assayes.
His time he spent in Uertues lore, as seemd his state full wel,
By serious study what he could, he sought for to excel.
But what of all this same? the fates no wight in time will spare,
When gastly death hath pearst in earth, then must our bodyes weare
In age aswell in youthes, in youthes aswell in age,
No certayne time we have to bide, when death with us will wage.
No thing can still abide, but comes to nought in ende,
The craggy Rocks the sturdiest okes: starke rotten once is rend.
And so hath Fletcher, now to death payd his due,
What he is now we must be all, his Funerall then vew.
