Beholde these high and mighty men,
Their chaunging state and tell me then:
Where they or we, best dayes do see,
Though we seeme not and they to be
In wealth.
Their pleasant course straung traces hath,
On tops of trees that groundles path:
Full waveringly.
For be it calme they tread not fast,
Blow roughe, blow soft, all helpe is past:
Appearingly.
With us, ye see, it is not so,
That clime not up, but kepe below:
In calmes our course is faire and playne,
Huge hilles defendes from stormy rayne:
For why?
The raging winde and stormy shower,
On mountaynes high it hath most power
Naturally.
But we that in lowe valleis lye,
Beholde may such as wander hye:
So slydingly:
Then what is he that will aspire,
To heape such woe to please desire:
That may in wealth by staying still,
Spend well his dayes and fly from yll:
To good.
By having his recourse to God
To love his lawes to feare his rod:
Unfaynedly.
To do that in his worde we finde,
To helpe the poore, the sicke, the blinde:
Accordingly.
But though gaynsayd this can not be,
Deeme men by deedes, and ye shall see:
That these low valleies they can not bide,
But up will clyme, though downe they slyde:
Agayne.
The poore the riche mans place doth crave,
The riche would fayne hyer places have:
Ambiciously.
The Squyre, the Knight, a Lorde would be,
The Lorde, the Erle would hyer then he:
Full dangerously.
When these attayne to their desire,
Then meaner men are set on fire:
To have the roomes which they in were,
So that ye see all times some there:
In hart.
When one is gon, another is come,
The third catching the secondes roome:
Full speedely.
Thus clyming one to others tayle,
The bowes either breake, or footing fayle:
Full totteringly.
For when the top they have attaynd,
And got is all they would have gaynd:
Then downe they come wit sodayne fall,
In doubtfull case of life and all.
And thus.
Ambition reapeth worthy hyre,
Because he would such sporte aspyre
Unequally.
And there his bragge is layd full low,
That thought on hie, himself to show.
Deservedly.
