What motion more, may moove a man to minde
His own estate, then proofe, whose dayes unsure,
Accounted are unto a puffe of winde,
A breathing blast, whose force can not endure:
Whose lyvely showes consumes, whose pompe decayes,
Whose glory dyes, whose pleasures soone be spent:
Whose stoutest strength, to weakenes subiect stayes,
Whose thoughts be vaine, and vade as though unment.
What have we then to vaunt, or glory in?
Sith all is vayne, wherin we take delight:
Why should we boast or brag, sith nought we win
In fine, but death? to whom yeeldes every wight.
To equall state, he bringeth each degree,
He feareth none, all subiects yeeldes to death:
To d•nkish dust, he driveth all we see,
Which in the world, enioyeth any breath:
Why vaunt we then, in that we see is vayne,
Or take delight, in that we prove but drosse?
Why glory we, or seeke for golden gayne?
Sith at the length, we reape therof but losse.
We lothe to leave, our hutches filde with golde:
Our annual rents, it greeves us to forgo,
Our buildings brave, which glads us to beholde:
Our pleasant sport, it greeves us to forgo.
We nothing brought, ne ought shall carry hence,
Lyfe lost, behinde goods, mony, land, we leave:
And naked shall returne, assured whence
Before we came, when death doth life bereave:
Line then, to leave thy life in every how•r,
Learne how to lead thy minde, from vayne desire,
Of filthy drosse, whose sugerest sweet is sower,
When dreadfull death, shall yeeld our earthly hire.
What is our world but vayne, fraught full of vice,
Wherin we live, allured by disceat:
Which us in youth, to error doth entice,
And sturs us up, in flamed by follyes heat.
Our mindes are mooved, with every fond desire,
We gloze in that, the which we see unsure:
We usuall seeke great honor to aspire,
Whose greatest pompe, doth but a while endure:
For proofe the flower, bedect with gorgious hew,
As soone with heate, of scorching sun doth fade:
As doth the weede, the which unseemly grew,
And showes it selfe, uncoverd with the shade.
The stately ship, which floates on f•ming fluds,
With wave is tost, as soone to surging Seas:
Doth yeeld his pompe, though fraught with store of goods,
As vessell weake, whose force the streame assayes:
Our selves may show, the state of each degree,
As Sampson stout, whose force Philistians felt:
For wealth, let Dives, glut with golde our Mirror be,
Marke Nemrods fall, whose hart with pride was swelt.
And divers mo, whose preter pathes may learne,
Our future steps, our vayn unsteady stay:
Whose elder lyves, already past may warne,
Us shun such snares, which leades us to decay.
