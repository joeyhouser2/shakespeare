Imagine when these blurred lines, thus scribled out of frame,
Shall come before thy careles eyes, for thee to read the same:
To be through no default of pen, or els through prowd disdayne,
But only through surpassing greefe, which did the Author payne
Whose quiuring hand could have no stay, this carful bil to write
Through flushing teares distilling fast, whilst she did it indite:
Which teares perhaps may have some force (if thou no tigre be,
And mollifie thy stony hart, to have remorse on me.
Ah periurde wight reclaime thy selfe, and save thy loving mate,
Whom thou hast left beclogged now, in most unhappy state:
(Ay me poore wench) what luckles star? what frowning god above
What hellish hag? what furious fate hath changd our former love?
Are we debard our wonted joyes? shall we no more embrace?
Wilt thou my deare in country strang, ensue Eneas race:
Italians send my lover home, he is no Germayne borne,
Unles ye welcome him because he leaves me thus forlorne.
As earst ye did Anchises sonne, the founder of your soyle,
Who falsely fled from Carthage Queene, releever of his toyle:
Oh send him to Bryttannia Coastes, unto his trusty feere,
That she may v•w his cumly corps, whom she estemes so deere:
Where we may once againe renue, our late surpassed dayes,
Which then were spent with kisses sweet, and other wanton playes:
But all in vayne (forgive thy thrall, if she do judge awrong)
Thou canst not want of dainty Trulles Italian Dames among.
This only now I speake by gesse, but if it happen true,
Suppose that thou hast seene the sword, that me thy Lover slue:
Perchance through time so merrily with dallying damsels spent,
Thou standst in doubt and wilte enquire fron whom these lines were sent:
If so, remember first of all, if thou hast any spowse,
Remember when, to whom and why, thou earst hast plited vowes,
Remember who esteemes thee best, and who bewayles thy flight,
Minde her to whom for loyalty thou falshood doost requight.
Remember Heaven, forget not Hell, and way thine own estate,
Revoke to minde whom thou hast left, in shamefull blame and hate:
Yea minde her well who did submit, into thine only powre,
Both hart and life, and therwithall, a ritch and wealthy dowre:
And last of all which greeves me most, that I was so begylde,
Remember most forgetfull man, thy pretty tatling childe:
The least of these surnamed things, I hope may well suffise,
To shew to thee the wretched Dame, that did this bill devise.
I speake in vayne, thou hast thy will, and now sayth Aesons sonne,
Medea may packe up her pypes, the golden Fleese is wonne:
If so, be sure Medea I will, shew forth my selfe in deede,
Yet gods defend though death I taste, I should distroy thy seede:
Agayne, if that I should enquire, wherfore thou doost soiurne,
No answere fitly mayst thou make, I know to serve thy turne:
Thou canst not say but that I have, obseru'd my love to thee,
Thou canst not say, but that I have, of life unchast bin free.
Thou canst not cloak (through want) thy flight, since riches did abound
Thou needes not shame of me thy spouse, whose byrth not low is found,
As for my beauty, thou thy self, earwhile didst it commend
And to conclude I kn•w no thing, wherin I dyd offend:
Retier with speed, I long to see, thy barke in wished bay,
The Seas are calmer to returne, then earst to fly away.
Beholde the gentill windes do serve, so that a frendly gayle,
Would soone conuay to happy Porte, thy most desired sayle:
Return would make amends for all, and bannish former wronge,
Oh that I had for to entice, a Scyrens flattering songe.
But out alas, I have no shift, or cunning to entreat,

Demaund not how I did disgest, at first thy sodayne flight,
For ten dayes space I tooke no rest, by day nor yet by night:
But like to Baccus beldame Nonne, I sent and rangde apace,
To see if that I mought thee finde, in some frequented place:
Now here, now there, now up, now down, my fancy so was fed,
Until at length I knew of troth, that thou from me wert fled.
Then was I fully bent with blade, to stab my vexed harte,
Yet hope that thou wouldst come agayn, my purpose did conuart:
And so ere since I liu'd in hope bemixt with dreadful feare,
My smeared face through endles teares, unpleasant doth appeare:
My slepes unsound with ugly dreams, my meats are vayn of taste
My gorgious rayment is dispisde, my tresses rudly plaste.
And to be breefe: I bouldly speake, there doth remayne no care:
But that therof in amplest wise, I do possesse a share:
Lyke as the tender sprig doth bend, with every blast of winde,
Or as the guidelesse Ship on Seas, no certaine Porte may finde.
So I now subiecte unto hope, now thrall to carefull dread,
Amids the Rocks, tween hope and feare, as fancy mooves, am led:
Alas returne, my deare returne, returne and take thy rest,
God graunt my wordes may have the force, to penytrat thy brest.
What doost thou thinke in Italy, some great exployt to win?
No, no, it is not Italy, as sometimes it hath bin:
Or doost thou love to gad abroad, the forrain costes to vew,
If so, thou hadst not doone amisse, to bid me first a dew:
But what hath bin the cause, I neede not descant longe,
For sure I am, meane while poore wench, I only suffer wrong.
Wel thus I leave, yet more could say: but least thou shouldst refuse,
Through tediousnesse to reede my lines, the rest I will excuse:
Until such time as mighty Jove doth send such luckye grace,
As we therof in freendly wise, may reason face to face.
Till then farwell, and he thee keepe, who only knowes my smart
And with this bill I send to thee, a trusty Lovers harte.
