IF only sight suffise, my hart to loose or binde,
What cause have I to moove debate, wherby no peace I finde?
If that my restlesse will, by payne doth still renue,
What force have I? but she consent, my fo for to subdue?
To yeeld and suffer then, I thinke it for the best,
And by desert as time shall serve, to purchase quiet rest.
Let jelous enuy lowre with browes, and visage bent,
I know the worst, no shameles tongue, shall alter mine intent.
The Dice of Love are throwen, god speede the doubtfull chaunce
Misdeeme who lyst, so she at last, my seruice will aduaunce.
To aske and to o•tayne, that Fortune were so swifte,
Sith travaill is the ready way, unto each noble gyfte.
And feeble is the joy, that lightly is begonne,
As tender Flare can beare no stresse, before that it be sponne.
Wherfore with sad aduice, in hope my harte shall dwell,
And all the tale that I confesse, in silence will I tell
Unto her selfe alone, whose favour I require,
None els shall know her name for me, to constre my desire.
