Since first I heard my Soveraigne speake his mind,
Ful fraught with Eloquence and learned Counsel,
A sodaine joy did so possesse my soule,
As that in words I cannot utter forth
The explanation of my willing thoughts:
In all our Victories and Conquests wonne,
Subduing many Regions, many Kings,
Nothing at all in honour have we gain'd,
If that we suffer the proud-minded Romanes,
And hautie Germaines to usurpe upon us,

Enacted on our friends and countrymen.
And sith Occasion now is profered,
And Libertie to trie our force of Armes,
I do reioyce to see this happie day,
Wherein we may but meet and joyne with them:
I thrist my Lord in heart for sweet revenge,
As if three dayes I had been kept from drink• ▪
The wounds I should receive upon that day,
Would be as pleasant to my labouring soule,
As Water to a thirstie Traveller,
Or else Releasement to a man condemn'd,
Nay Death it selfe were welcome to my bosome,
For to revenge our Fathers iniuries,
Defend our libertie, aduance our King:
Let us give onset on that meacocke Nation▪
Those fond effeminate unruly people,
And fight it out unto the latest man;
That after we have spread our waving Colours,
In signe of Triumph and of Victorie,
We may enioy the Honors they possesse,
And for my part renowned valiant King,
Two thousand armed horsemen will I bring.
