Who so doth marke the carelesse life, of these unhappy dayes,
And sees what small and slender hold, the state of vertues stayes:
He findes that this accursed trade, proceedeth of this ill,
That men be given too much to yeld, to their untamed will.

In lacke of taming witlesse will, the poore we often see,
Enuies the riche, because that he, his equall can not be:
The riche aduaunced to might by wealth, from wrong doth not refrayne,
But will oppresseth weaker sort, to heape excessive gayne.

If Fortune were so blind, to give to one man what he will,
A world would not suffice the same, if he might have his fill:
We wish, we searche, we strive for all, and have no more therein,
Then hath the slave, when death doth come, though Cresus wealth he win.

In getting much, we get but care, such brittle wealth to keepe,
The rich within his walles of stone, doth never soundly sleepe:
When poore in weake and slender house, do feare no losse of wealth,
And have no further care but this, to keepe them selves in health.

Affection may not hide the sword of sway, in judgement seate,
Least partiall favour execute, the law in causes great:
But if the mynde in constant state, affection quite do leave,
The higher state shall have their rightes, the poore no wrong receive.

It is accompted greater prayse, to Caesars lofty state,
Agaynst his vanquest foes, in warres to bridle wreckfull hate:
Then when to Rome he had subdued, the people long unknowne,
Whereby as farre as land was found, the same abroad was blowne.

If honour can selfe will refuse, and Justice be upright,
And private state desires but that, which good appeares in sight:
Then vertue shall with soveraigne shew, to every eye reveale,
An heavenly life, a wealfull state, a happy common weale.

Let vertue then the triumph win, and governe all your deedes,
Your yelding to her sober hestes, immortall glory breedes:
She shall upreare your worthy name, shinyng into the skies,
Her beames shall blaze in grave obscure, where shrived carkasse lyes.
