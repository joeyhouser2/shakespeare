I Am a virgin fayre and free, and freely do reioyce,
I sweetly warble sugred notes, from silver voyce:
For which delightfull joyes, yet thanke I curteous Love,
By whose almightie power, such sweete delightes I prove.

I walke in pleasaunt fieldes, adorn'd with lively greene,
And vewe the fragrant flowers, most lovely to be seene:
The purple Columbine, the Couslippe and the Lillie,
The Uiolet sweete, the Daizie and Daffadillie.

The woodbines on the hedge, the red Rose and the white,
And each fine flowers els, that rendreth sweete delight:
Emong the which I chose, all those of seemeliest grace,
In thought, resembling them, to my deare lovers face.

His lovely face I meane, whose golden flouring giftes,
His everliving fame, to loftie skye upliftes:
Whom loving me I love, only for vertues sake,
When vertuosly to love, all only care I take.

Of all which fresh fayre flowers, that flower that doth appeare,
In my conceipt, most like to him I hold so deare:
I gather it, I kisse it, and eke devise with it,
Such kinde of lovely speach, as is for lovers fit.

And then of all my flowers, I make a garland fine,
With which my golden wier heares, together I do twine:
And set it on my head, so taking that delight,
That I would take, had I my lover still in sight.

For as in goodly flowers, mine eyes great pleasure finde,
So are my lovers giftes, most pleasaunt to my mynde:
Upon which vertuous giftes, I make more repast,
Then they that for love sportes, the sweetest joyes do tast.
