IN choyse of frends what hap ha• I▪ to chuse one of Sirens kinde,
Whose harpe, whose pipe, whose melodie could feede my eares and make me blind:
Whose pleasaunt voyce made me forget, that in sure trust is great deceipt,
In trust I see is treason found, and man to man deceiptfull is.
And where as treasure doth abounde, of flatterers there do not misse,
Whose painted speach, and outward shew, do seeme as frendes and be not so.

Would I have thought in thee to be, the nature of the Crocadill,
Which if a man a sleepe may see, with bloudy thirst desires to kill:
And then with teares a while gan weepe, that death of him thus slaine a sleepe
O favell false, thou traitour borne, what mischief more might thou devise:
Then thy deare frend to have in scorne, and him to wound in sundry wise,
Which still a frend pretendes to be, and art not so by proofe I see.
Fie, fie, upon such trecherie. W.H.
If such false shippes do haunt the shore,
Strike downe the sayle and trust no more.
