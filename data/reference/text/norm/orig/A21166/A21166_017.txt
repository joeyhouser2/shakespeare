The day delayed, of that I most do wish,
Wherewith I feede, and starve in one degree:
With wish and want, still served in one dish,
A live as dead, by proofe as you may see:
To whom of old, this Proverbe well it serves,
While grasse doth grow, the silly horse he sterues:
Tweene these extremes, thus do I rome the race,
Of my poore life, this certainely I know:
Tweene would and want, unwarely that do passe,
More swift then shot, out of the Archers vow:
As Spider drawes her line all day,
I watch the net, and others have the pray.

And as by proofe, the greedy Dogge doth gnaw,
The bared bone, all only for the tast:
So to and fro, this lothsome life I draw,
With fancies forst, and fed with vayne repast:
Narsissus brought, unto the water drinke,
So aye thirst I, the more that I do drinke.

Loe thus I dye, and yet I seeme not sicke,
With smart unseene my selfe, my selfe I weare:
With prone desire, and power that is not quicke,
With hope a loft, now drenched in disprayre:
Trayned in trust, for no reward assignde,
The more I hast, the more I come behinde.

With hurt to heale, in frozen Ise to frie,
With losse to laugh, this is a wonderous case:
Fast fetred here, is forst away to flie,
As hunted Hare, that Hound hath in the chase:
With winges and spurres, for all the hast I make,
As like to lose, as for to draw the stake.

The dayes be long, that hang upon desart,
The life is irke of joyes that be delayed:
The time is short, for to requite the smart,
That doth proceede, of promise long unpayed:
That to the last, of this my fainting breath,
I wish exchaunge of life, for happy death.
