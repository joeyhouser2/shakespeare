Sweet were the joyes, that both might like and last,
Straunge were the state, exempt from all distresse,
Happie the life, that no mishap should tast:
Blessed the chaunce, might never chaunge successe,
Where such a life to lead, or state to prove,
Who would not wishe, that such a life were love.

But O the sowrie sauce of sweet unsure,
When pleasures flie, and flie with wast of winde:
The trustlesse traines, that hoping harts allure,
When sweet delightes, do but allure the minde.
When care consumes, and wastes the wretched wight,
While fancie feedes, and drawes of her delight.

What life were love, if love were free from paine?
But O that paine, with pleasure matcht should meet:
Why did the course, of Nature so ordaine,
That sugred sowre, must sauce the bitter sweet?
Which sowre from sweet, might any meanes remove,
What hap, what heaven, what life were like to love?
