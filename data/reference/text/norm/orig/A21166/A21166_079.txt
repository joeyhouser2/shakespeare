IF care or skill, could conquere vayne desire,
Or reasons raignes, my strong affection stay:
Then should my sighes, to quiet breast retire,
And shunne such sighes, as secret thoughtes bewray,
Uncomely love, which now lurkes in my brest,
Should cease my grief, through wisedomes power opprest.

But who can leave, to looke on Venus face,
Or yeldeth not to Junos high estate:
What witte so wise, as gives not Pallas place,
These vertues rare, ech Gods did yeld a mate.
Save her alone, who yet on earth doth raigne,
whose beauties string, no God can well destraine.

What worldly wight, can hope for heavenly hire,
when only sighes, must make his secret move:
A silent sute, doth seeld to grace aspire,
My haplesse hap, doth roule the restlesse stone.
Yet Phoebe faire, disdaine the heavens above,
To joy on yearth, her poore Edimions love.

Rare is reward, where none can justly crave,
For chaunce is choyse, where reason makes no claime:
Yet lucke sometimes, dispairing soules doth save,
A happie starre, made Giges joye attaine.
A slavish Smith, of rude and raskall race,
Found meanes in time, to gaine a Goddesse grace.

Then loftie Love, thy sacred sailes aduaunce,
My sighing seas, shall flowe with streames of teares:
Amidst disdaine, drive forth my dolefull chaunce,
A valiant minde, no deadly daunger feares.
who loves a loft, and sets his heart on hye,
Deserves no paine, though he do pyne and dye.
