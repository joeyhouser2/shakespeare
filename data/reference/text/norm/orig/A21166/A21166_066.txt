I Have no joye, but dreame of joye, and joy to thinke on joye,
A joye I withstoode, to finish mine annoye:
I hate not without cause alas, yet love I know not why,
I thought to hate, I cannot hate, although that I should dye.
A foe most sweete, a friend most sower, I joy for to embrace;
I hate the wrong, and not the wight, that workt my woefull case:
What thing it is I know not I, but yet a thing there is,
That in my fancie still perswades, there is no other blisse.
The joyes of life, the pangues of death, it makes me feele each daie,
But life nor death, this humor can, devise to weare awaye:
Faine would I dye, but yet in death, no hope I see remaines,
And shall I live? since life I see, a course of sory paines.
What is it then that I do seeke, what joye would I aspire,
A thing that is divine belike, too high for mans desire.
