IN youthfull yeares, when first my young desires began,
To pricke me forth, to serve in court, a slender tall young man:
My fathers blessing then, I asked upon my knee,
Who blessing me with trembling hand, these wordes gan say to me:
My sonne, God guide thy way, and shield thee from mischaunce,
And make thy just desartes in Court, thy poore estate to aduaunce:
Yet when thou art become, one of the Courtly trayne,
Thinke on this Proverbe old (quoth he) that faire wordes make fooles faine.

This counsell gravely given, most straunge appeares to me,
Till tract of time with open eyes, had made me plainly see:
What subtill sleightes are wrought, by painted tales devise,
When hollow hartes with frendly shewes, the simple do entise,
To thinke all gold that shines, to feede their fond desire,
Whose shivering cold is warmde with smoke, in steed of flaming fire:
Sith talke of tickle trust, doth breed a hope most vaine,
This proverb true by proofe I find, that fayre wordes make fooles faine.

Fayre speech alway doth well, where deedes insue faire wordes,
Faire speech againe alway doth evill, that bushes give for birdes:
Who hopes to have fayre wordes, to trie his luckie lot,
If I may counsell, let him strike it while the Iron is hot.
But them that feed on cloddes, in steed of pleasant grapes,
And after warning often given, for better lucke still gapes:
Full loath I am, yet must I tell them in wordes plaine,
This proverb old proves true in them, that faire wordes make fooles faine.

Wo worth the time, that wordes so slowly turne to deedes,
Wo worth the time that fayre sweet flowres, are growne to rotten weedes:
But thrise wo worth the time, that truth away is fled,
Wherein I see how simple hartes, with wordes are vainely fed.
Trust not fayre wordes therefore, where no deedes do insue,
Trust wordes as skilfull Falkners do, trust Haukes that never flue:
Trust deedes, let wordes be wordes, which never wrought me gaine,
Let my experience make you wise, and let wordes make fooles faine.
