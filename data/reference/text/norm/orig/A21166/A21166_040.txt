IN every wight, some sundry sort of pleasure I do finde,
Which after he doth seeke, to ease his joyling minde▪
Diana with her trainyng chase, of hunting had delight,
Against the fearefull Deare, she could direct her shot a right.
The loftie yeares in every age, doth still imbrace the same,
The sport is good, if vertue do assist the chearefull game.

Minerua in her chattering armes, her courage doth aduaunce,
In triall of the bloudy warres, she giveth luckie chaunce:
For savegard men imbrace the same, which do so needefull seeme,
That noble hartes their chief delightes, in use therof esteeme.
In warlike games to try or ride, the force of armes they use,
And base the man we do attempt, that doth the same refuse.

The silver sound of Musickes cordes, doth please Apollos wit,
A sentence which the heavens aduaunce, where it deserves to sit:
A pleasure apt for every wight, relief to carefull mynde,
For woe redresse, for care a salve, for sadnesse helpe we finde.
The soveraigne prayse of Musicke still, doth cause the Poetes fayne,
That whirling Spheres, and eke the heavens do hermonie retayne.

I heard, that these three powers, at variaunce lately fell,
Whiles ech did prayse his own delight, the other to excell:
Then fame, as an indifferent Judge, to end the case they call,
The prayse pronounced by her to them, indifferently doth fall.
Diana health and strength maintaine, Minerua force doth tame,
And Musicke gives a sweete delight, to further others game.

These three delightes to hautie myndes, the worthiest are esteemed,
If vertue be annexed to them, they rightly be so demed:
With joy they do revive the wit, with sorrow oft opprest,
And never suffer solemne grief, too long in minde to rest.
Be wise in mirth, and seeke delight, the same do not abuse,
In honest mirth a happy joy, we ought not to refuse.
