The Marigold so likes the lovely Sunne,
That when he settes the other hides her face,
And when he ginnes his morning course to •unne,
She spreades abroad, and showes her greatest grace;
so shuts or spreuts my joy, as doth this flow're,
when my •heesune doth either laugh or lowre.
When she departes my sight, I die for pame,
In closing up my hearte with cloudie care;
And yet when once I viewe her face againe,
I streight revive, and joye my wonted fare:
Therewith my heart ofte saies, when all is done,
That heau'n and earth have not a brighter sunne,
A jealous thought yet puttes my minde in feare,
Lest Jove him selfe descending from his throne
Shoulde take by stealth and place her in his spheare,
Or in some higher globe to rule alone:
Which if he should, the heau'ns might boast their praye
But I (alas) might curse the dismall day.
