Ah cruel windes why call you hence away:
Why make you breach betwixt my soule and me?
Ye trayterous floodes why 〈◊〉 your floates delaie,
Until my latest moanes discoursed be?
For tho ye salte sea-Gods with hold the raigne:
Of all your floates. And gentle windes befall;
Uvhile I have wept such teares, as might restraine
The rage of tides and windes against then will.
Ah shall I lose your sight bright shining eyes?
And must my soule his life and glory leave?
Must I forsake the bower where solace lives,
To trust to tickle fates that still deceive?
" Ah-las so willes the wanton Queene of chaunge,
" That each man tract this laborinth of life,
" With slippery steps, now wrongd by fortune strange,
" Now drawne by counsell from the maze of strife?
" Ah joy no joy because so so soone thou fleetest,
Houres, dayes, and times inconstant in your beeing.
Oh life, no life since with such chaunce thou meetest,
Oh eies, no cies since you must loose your seeing:
Soule be thou sad, dissolve thy living powers.
To christall teares, and by their pores expresse,
The griefe, that my destressed soule devoures:
Cloath thou my body all in heavinesse,
My sonnes appeard faire smiling full of pleasure.
But now the vale of absence over cloudes them:
They fed my heart with joyes exceeding measure
Which now shall dy, since absence needs must shroud them
Yea die, oh death, sweet death, vouchsafe that blessing,
That I may die the death whilest she regardeth,
For sweet were death, and sweete, were deathes oppressing,
If she looke on who all my life awardeth.
Oh thou that art the portion of my joy,
Yet not the portion, for thou art the prime:
Suppose my griefes, conceive the deepe anoy,
That wounds my soule upon this sorrye time:
Pale is my face, and in my pale confesses,
The paine I suffer, since I needes must leave thee,
Redde are mine eyes through teares that them oppresses,
Dul'd are my sprits since fates do now bereve thee.
And now, ah now, my plaintes are quite prevented,
The windes are faire the sailes are hoysed hie,
The Anckers waid, and now quite discontented,
Griefe so subdewes my hart as it should dye.
A faint farewell, with trembling hand I tender,
And with my teares my papers are distained,
Which closed up, my heart in them I render,
To tell thee how at parting I complained.
Vouchsafe his message that doth bring farewell,
And for my sake let him with beautie dwell.
