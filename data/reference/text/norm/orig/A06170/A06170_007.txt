It is not death which wretched men call dying,
But that is very death which I endure:
When my coy looking Nimph (her grace enuying,)
By fatall frownes my domage doth procure.
It is not life which we for life approve,
But that is life when on her woul-soft pappes,
I seale sweet kisses, which do batten love:
And doubling them do treble my good happes.
Tis neither love the sonne, nor love the mother,
Which lovers praise and pray to; but that love is:
Which she in eye and I in heart do smother,
Then muse not tho I glory in my misse.
Since she who holdes my heart, and me in durance,
Hath life, death, love and all in her procureance.
