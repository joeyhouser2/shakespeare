Oh pleasing thoughts, apprentises of love,
Fore-runners of desire, sweet Methridates
The poison of my sorrowes to remove,
With whom my hopes and fearefull oft debates.
Inritch your selves and me by your selfe riches,
(Which are the thoughts you spend on heaven bred beauty,)
Rowse you my muse beyond our Poets pitches,
And working wonders yet say all is duty.
Use you no Eglets eyes, nor Phenix feathers,
To tower the heaven from whence heavens wonder fallies:
For why your sonne singes sweetly to her wethers:
Making a springe of winter in the vallies.
Show to the world tho poore and scant my skill is,
How sweet thoughts be, that are but thought on Phillis.
