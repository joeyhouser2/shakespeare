The gentle season of the yeere,
Hath made my blooming branch appeere,
And beautified the land with flowres,
The aire doth savor with delight,
The heavens do smile, to see the sight,
And yet mine eies, augments their showres.

The meades are mantled all with greene,
The trembling leaves, have cloth'd the treene,
The birds with feathers new do sing,
But I poore soule, when wrong doth wrack,
Attyres my slfe in mourning black,
Whose leafe doth fall amid his spring.

And as you see the skarlet Rose,
In his sweete prime, his buds disclose,
Whose hewe is with the Sun revived,
So in the Aprill of mine age,
My lively colours do asswage,
Because my Sun-shine is deprived.

My hart that wonted was of yore,
Light as the winde abroad to sore,
Amongst the buds when beautie springs,
Now only hovers over you,
As doth the birde thats taken new,
And mourns when all her neighbours sings.

When every man is bent to sport,
Then pensive I alone resort,
Into some solitarie walke,
As doth the dolefull Turtle dove,
Who having lost her faithfull love,
Sits mourning on some withered stalke.

There to my selfe, I do recount,
How far my woes, my joyes surmount,
How Love requiteth me with hate:
How all my pleasures end in paine,
How hate doth say, my hope is vaine,
How fortune frownes upon my state.

And in this moode, charg'd with despaire,
With vapored sighes, I dim the aire,
And to the Gods make this request:
That by the ending of my life,
I may have truce with this strange strife,
And bring my soule to better rest.
