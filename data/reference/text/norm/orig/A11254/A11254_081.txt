Virginitie resembleth right the Rose,
That gallantly within the garden growes,
Whilst in the mothers bodie it doth stand,
Of nibling sheep untoucht, or shepherds hand.
The aire thereon, and ruddie morne doth smile,
The earth and waters, favours it that while,
Brave lustie youth, and the inamord Dame,
Even so doth age, and temples crave the same.

But when from naturall stalke, it is remou'd,
And place where it, so highly was belou'd,
The grace that earth, and heaven thereon did cast,
With beautie, favor, love, and all, is past.

Even so the Maid, when once her flowre is lost,
More deere than eie, or life, or what is most,
The love and liking which she had before,
Forgoeth quite, and she esteem'd no more.

Ladies Lenuoy to you that have this prize,
I reed ye hold your wone, if yiou be wise.
