I Hope and feare, that for my weale or woe,
That heau'nly lampe, which yeelds both heat and light,
To make a throne, for gods on earth belowe,
Is cut in twaine, and fixt in my delight,
Which two faire hemyspheres, through light and heat,
Planting desire, drive reason from her seate.

No, no, my too forgetfull toong blaspheames,
I should have said, that where these hemispheres,
In harts, through eies, fixe hot and lightsome beames,
There reason works desire, and hopes breed feares,
O only obiect, for an Eagles eie,
Whose light, and heate, make men to live and die.

Twixt these, a daintie paradise doth lie,
As sweete as in the Sunne the Phenix Bowre,
As white as snowe, as smooth as Ivorie,
As faire, as Psyches bosome, in that howre,
When she disclosde the boxe of Beauties Queene,
All this and more, is in Sibilla seene.
