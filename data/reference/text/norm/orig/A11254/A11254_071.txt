Set me where Phoebus heate, the flowers slaieth,
Or where continuall snowe withstands his forces,
Set me where he his temprate raies displaieth,
Or where he comes, or where he never courses.

Set me in Fortunes grace; or else discharged,
In sweete and pleasant aire, or darke and glooming,
Where daies and nights, are lesser, or inlarged,
In yeeres of strength, in failing age, or blooming.

Set me in heaven, or earth, or in the center,
Lowe in a vale, or on a mountaine placed,
Set me to daunger, perill, and aduenture,
Graced by Fame, or infamie disgraced.

Set me to these, or any other triall,
Except my Mistres anger and deniall.

I sawe the eies, that have my seeing bounde,
I harde the toong, that made my speech to staie,
Her wit, my thoughts did captive and confounde,
And with her graces, drew my life away,
Unto her life, in whom my sences lives,
My spirit up himselfe, for tribute gives.

She sawe mine eies, and they recover'd light,
She spake to me, and I had powre to speake,
She graced me, and I regained spright,
She freed my hart, that readie was to breake,
My life, that erst beginning had in me,
Now by her being, doth begin to be.

Mine eies, behold the beautie raignes in her,
Speake toong of her, that nothing is but wonder,
To honor her, my spirits only stir,
Serve her my hart, or hart devide asunder:
And life, live in the favor she hath showne,
Whereby thou hast more stength than was thine own.

Mistres, this grace, unto your seruant give,
Thus for to live, or not at all to live.
