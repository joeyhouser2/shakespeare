Would I were chaung'd into that golden showre,
That so divinely streamed from the skies,
To fall in drops upon the daintie floore,
Where in her bed, she solitarie lies,
Then would I hope such showres as richly shine,
Would pearce more deepe than these wast teares of mine.

Or would I were that plumed Swan, snowe white,
Under whose forme, was hidden heavenly power,
Then in that river would I most delite,
Whose waves do beate, against her stately bower,
And in those banks, so tune my dying song,
That her deafe ears, would think my plaint too long.

Else would I were, Narcissus, that sweete boy,
And she her selfe, the sacred fountaine cleere,
Who ravisht with the pride of his own joy,
Drenched his lims, with gazing over neere:

So should I bring, my soule to happie rest,
To end my life, in that I loved best.
