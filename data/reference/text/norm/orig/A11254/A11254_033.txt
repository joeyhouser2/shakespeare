OF ceasles thoughts my mind hath fram'd his wings,
Wherewith he soares and climes above conceit,
And midst his flight for endles joy he sings,
To spie those double lampes, whose sweete receit
Must be the heaven where as my soule shall rest,
Though by their shine my bodie be deprest.

Her eies shrowd pitie, pietie, and pure,
Her face shields Roses, Lillies, and delight,
Her hand hath powre, to conquere and allure,
Her hart, holds honor, love, remorce, and right,
Her minde is fraught, with wisdome, faith, and love,
All what is hirs, is borrowed from above.

Then mount my minde, and feare no future fall,
Exceed conceit, for she exceeds conceit!
Burne lovely lamps, to whom my lookes are thrall,
My soule shall glorie in so sweete receit,
Tho in your flames my corse to cinders wend,
Yet am I proud to gaine a Phoenix end.
