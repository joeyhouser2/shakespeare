Cease restles thoughts, surcharg'd with heavines,
Love, fortune, and disdaine, with their endever,
The forces of my life will soone dissever,
Without the sting of your unquietnes.

And thou oh hart, guiltie of my distresse,
To harbor these faire foes, doost still persever,
Whereby thou shewst false traitor, thou hadst lever
Their conquest, than mine ease and happines.

In thee, Loves messengers have taken dwelling,
Fortune in thee, her pompe triumphant spreadeth,
Disdaine hath spent on thee, her bitter swelling,
Thus thou the root, from whence my woes proceedeth.

Cease then vain thoughts, no more my sorows double.
Love, fortune, and disdaine, ynough of trouble.
