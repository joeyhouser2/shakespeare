Sir painter, are thy colours redie set,
My Mistresse can not be with thee to day,
She's gone into the field to gather May,
The timely Prymrose, and the Violet:
Yet that thou maist, not disapointed be,
Come draw her picture by my fantasee.

And well for thee, to paint her by thine eare,
For should thine eie, unto that office serve,
Thine Eie, and Hand, thy Art, and Hart, would swerve,
Such maiestie her countenance doth beare,
And where thou wert Apelles thought before,
For failing so, thou shouldst be praisd no more.

Drawe first her Front, a perfect Ivorie white,
Hie, spatious, round, and smooth on either side,
Her temples brancht with vains, blew, opening wide.
As in the Map, Danubius runs in sight:
Colour her semicircled browes with jet,
The throne where Love triumphantly doth set.

Regard her Eie, her eie, a woondrous part,
It woundeth deepe, and cureth by and by,
It drives away, and draweth curteously,
It breeds and calmes, the tempest of the hart,
And what to lightning Jove, belongeth too,
The same her lookes, with more effect can do.

Her Cheeke, resembleth everie kinde of way,
The Lillie stainde, with sweete Adonis blood,
As wounded he strai'd up and downe the wood,
For whome faire Venus languisht many a day,
Or plainly more to answere your demaune,
Her cheekes are Roses, overcast with lawne.

Her lovely Lip, doth others all excell,
On whom it please (ay me) a kisse bestoe,
He never tasteth afterward of woe,
Such speciall vertue in the toutch doth dwell:
The colour tempred of the morning red,
Where with Aurora doth adorne her head.

Her ample Chest, an heavenly plot of ground,
The space betweene, a Paradise at least,
Parnassus like, her twifolde mounting breast,
Her heavenly graces, heapingly abound,
Love spreads his conquering colours in this feeld,
Whereto the race of Gods and men do yeeld.

The other parts, which custom doth conceale,
Within a sarcenet vaile thou must conuay,
So due proportion well discerne I may,
What though the garment do not all reveale,
The shadow of a naked thigh may fraight,
His head brim full, hath any fine conceit.

Before her Feete, upon a Marble stone,
Inflamed with the Sunbeames of her eie,
Depaint my hart that burneth passionately,
And if thy pensill can set downe such mone,
Thy picture selfe, will teeling semblance make,
Of ruthe and pitie for my torments sake.

How now Apelles, are thy senses tane?
Hast drawne a picture, or drawne out thy hart?
Wilt thou be held a Master of thine art,
And temper colours tending to thy bane?
Happie my hart, that in her Sunshine fries,
Above thy hap that in her shadow dies.

I Pray thee Love, say, whither is this posting,
Since with thy deitie first I was acquainted,
I never saw thee thus distracted coasting,
With countenance tainted.

Thy conquering arrowes broken in thy quiver,
Thy brands that woont the inward marrow sunder,
Fireles and forceles, all a peeces shiver,
With mickle wonder.

That maketh next my staylesse thoughts to hover,
I cannot sound this uncouth cause of beeing,
The vaile is torne that did thy visage cover,
And thou art seeing.

A stranger, one (quoth Love) of good demerit,
Did sute and seruice to his Soveraine proffer,
In any case she would not seeme to heare it,
But scornd the offer.

And very now upon this Maying morrow,
By breake of day, he found me at my harbour,
I went with him, to understand his sorrow,
Unto her Arbour

Where he Love torments dolefully unfolded,
With words, that might a Tigers hart have charmed,
His sighes and teares, the mountaine ye had moulted,
And she not warmed.

Her great disdaine against her Lover proved,
Kindled my brand, that to her brest I seated,
The flame betweene her paps, them often moved.
Nor burnt, nor heated.

My arrowes keene I afterward assaied,
Which from her brest without effect rebounded.
And as a ball, on Marble floore the plaied,
With force confounded.

The brand that burnt, old Pryams Towne to asshes,
Now first his operation, wants it than,
The darts that Emerald skies in peeces dasshes,
Skornd by a woman.

Thus while I said, she toward me arrived,
And with a tutch of triumph, never doubted,
To teare the vaile, that use of sight bereaved,
So Love was louted.

The vaile of erroe, from mine eies bereaved,
I sawe heavens hope, and earth her treasurie,
Well maist thou erre said I, I am deceived,
Bent to pleasure thee.

Cease haples man, my succors to importune,
She only she, my stratagemes repelleth,
Vainly endeour I, to tempt her Fortune,
That so excelleth.

Content thee man, that thou didst see and suffer,
And be content, to suffer, see, and die,
And die content, bicause thou once didst moove her,
She displeasd thereby.

And herewithall I left the man a dyeng,
For by his passions I perceiu'd none other,
I hie me thus asham'd with speedie flyeng,
To tell my Mother.
