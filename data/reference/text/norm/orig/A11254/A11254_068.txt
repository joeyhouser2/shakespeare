BY wracke late driven on shoare, from Cupids Crare,
Whose sailes of error, sighes of hope and feare,
Conueied through seas of teares, and sands of care,
Till rocks of high disdaine, her sides did teare,
I write a dirge, for dolefull doves to sing,
With selfe same quill, I pluckt from Cupids wing.

Farewell unkinde, by whom I fare so ill,
Whose looks bewitcht my thoughts with false surmise,
Till forced reason did unbinde my will,
And shewed my hart, the follie of mine eies,
And said, attending where I should attaine,
Twixt wish and want, was but a pleasing paine.

Farewell unkinde, my floate is at an ebbe,
My troubled thoughts, are turnd to quiet wars,
My fancies hope hath spun and spent her webbe,
My former wounds, are closed up with skars,
As ashes lie, longe since consumde with fire,
So is my love, so now is my desire.

Farewell unkinde, my first and finall love,
Whose coie contempts, it bootes not heere to name,
But gods are just, and every star above,
Doth threat revenge, where faith's reward is blame,
And I may live, though your despised thrall,
By fond mischoyce, to see your fortunes fall.

Farewell unkinde, most cruell of your kinde,
By whom my worth, is drowned in disdaines,
As was my love, so is your judgement blinde,
My fortune ill, and such hath bene my gaines,
But this for all, I list no more to saie,
Farewell faire proude, not lifes, but loves decaie.
