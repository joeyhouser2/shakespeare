B•t call to mind how long Ulisses was,
In lingring absence, from his loving make:
And how she deigned then her dayes to passe,
In sollitary silence for his sake.
Be thou a true Penelope to me,
And thou shalt soone thine own Ulisses see.

What said I? soone? yea soone I say againe,
I will come soone and sooner if I may:
Beleve me now it is a pinching payne,
To thinke of love when lovers are away.
Such thoughts I have, and when I thinke on thee,
My thoughts are there, whereas my bones would be.

The longing lust which Priames sonne of Troy,
Had for to see his Cressyde come againe:
Could not exceede the depth of mine anoye,
Nor seeme to passe the patterne of my payne.
I fryse in hope, I thaw in hot desire,
Farre from the flame, and yet I burne like fire.

Wherfore deare friend, thinke on the pleasures past,
And let my teares▪ for both our paynes suffise:
The lingring joyes, when as they come at last,
Are bet then those, wh•ch passe in posting wise.
And I my selfe, to prove this tale is true,
In hast, post hast, thy comfort will renew.
