You that have spente the silente nighte
In sleepe and quiet reste,
And joye to see the cheerefull lighte
That ryseth in the East:
Nowe cleere your voyce, now cheare your heart,
Come helpe me nowe to sing:
Each willyng wight come beare a parte,
To prayse the heavenly King.

And you whome care in prison keepes,
Or sickenesse dothe suppresse,
Or secrete sorrowe breakes youre sleepes,
Or dolours do distresse:
Yet beare a parte in dolefull wyse,
Yea thinke it good accorde,
And acceptable sacrifice,
Each sprite to prayse the Lorde.

The dreadfull night with darkesome storme•
Had overspread the lyght,
And sluggishe sleepe with drowsynesse,
Had overpreste our myght:
A glasse wherein we maye beholde
Each storme that stoppes our breath,
Our bedde the grave, oure cloathes lyke molde,
And sleepe lyke dreadfull death.

Yet as this deadly nyghte did laste,
But for a little space,
And heavenly daye nowe night is paste,
Doth shewe his pleasant face:
So muste we hope to see Gods face,
At laste in heaven on hie,
When we have chaung'd this mortall place,
For Immortalitie.

And of such happes and heavenly joyes,
As then we hope to holde,
All earthly sightes, all worldly toyes,
Are tokens to beholde:
The daye is lyke the daye of doome,
The sunne, the Sonne of man,
The skyes the heavens, the earth the toombe
Wherein we reste till than.

The Raynbowe bending in the skye,
Bedeckte with sundrye hewes,
Is lyke the seate of God on hye,
And seemes to tell these newes:
That as thereby he promised
To drowne the worlde no more,
So by the bloud which Christe hath shead,
He will oure health restore.

The mistie clowdes that fall sometyme,
And overcaste the skyes,
Are lyke to troubles of oure tyme,
Which do but dimme oure eyes:
But as such dewes are dryed up quite,
When Phoebus shewes his face,
So are such fansies put to flighte,
Where God dothe guyde by grace.

The carrion Crowe, that lothesome beast,
Whyche cryes agaynst the rayne,
Bothe for her hew and for the reste,
The Devill resembleth playne:
And as with goonnes we kill the Crowe,
For spoylyng oure reliefe,
The Devill so muste we overthrowe,
With goonshot of beliefe.

The little Byrdes which syng so sweete,
Are lyke the angels voyce,
Which render God his prayses meete,
And teache us to reioyce:
And as they more esteeme that myrthe,
Than dreade the nightes anoye,
So muste we deeme oure dayes on earthe,
But hell to heavenly joye.

Unto which Joyes for to attayne,
God graunte us all his grace,
And sende us after worldly payne,
In heaven to have a place.
Where we may still enioy that lyght,
Which never shall decaye:
Lorde for thy mercie lende us myghte
To see that joyfull daye.
