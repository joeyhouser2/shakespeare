Light, Rage and Griefe, limmes of unperfect love,
By over-acting ever lose their ends;
For Griefe while it would good affection move,
With selfe-affliction doth deface her friends;
Putting on poore weake Pitties pale reflexion,
Whereas Good-will is stirr'd with good complexion.

Rage againe fond of her inflam'd desire,
Desire which conquers by close inuasion,
Forgetting light and heat live in one fire,
So overblowes the temper of Occasion,
That scorch'd with heate, by light discovered,
Untimely borne is, and untimely dead.

Poore fooles, why strive you then since all hearts feele
That idle Chance so governes in affection,
As Cupid cannot turne his fatall wheele,
Nor in his own orbe banish her election?
Then teach Desire hope; not rage, feare, griefe,
Powers as unapt to take, as give reliefe.
