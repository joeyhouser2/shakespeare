Princesse of me! I finde care in your face,
Woe smothered up; I came to know your will;
Nothing which you command me can be ill.

That which I least did feare is fall'n on me,
Wrong and mishap; which needing others love,
Makes them unlovely that unhappy be.
From Kings themselves when fortune turnes her face;
Then need they most, yet least may use their own.
So dearly Mans unthankfulnesse is knowne.

What is the cause that makes you thus accuse
The world of faults, your selfe of inward feare?

The little faith which all the world doth use;
The iniuries which strength of heart must beare:
Ewi'd of all, if it be set above;
If humble, then too low for men to love.

Do not forsake your selfe: For they that do,
Offend, and teach the world to leave them too.
Mortall our God shall be; the truth shall lie;
Darkenesse shall see her selfe; fame lose her voyce;
Er'e I will leave my love, or my love you:
Afflictions wounds affection do renew.

Perchance you love both those I hate, and me;
Affections then against affection be.
Perchance a vow, good turnes, and good beleefe
Are mists betweene your love, and my releefe.

You know I love: speake plaine, and do not feare,
That reason other is than kindnesse there.

Then heare: and if my judgement you disprove,
You shall have cause to thinke I trust, and love.
Mahomet the faultie is, his faults be these:
Enuious of thee, to my love treacherous;
The King must lose his sight, his Crowne, his Sonne:
This wickednesse hath Mahomet begunne.

O hell! and is thy seate in fleshly hearts?
Be mans ill thoughts his own ill spirits become?
I well can thinke that Mahomet aspires;
For love of greatnesse may with goodnesse go:
But cannot thinke that he our death conspires.
Perchance he seekes to do your brother Right,
Which makes our own desires to do us spite.

Mischiefe that may be help'd, is hard to know;
And danger going on still multiplies.

Let care as fast then adde unto her eyes.

Where harme hath many wings, care armes too late:

Hastie attempts make chance precipitate.
What shall I do?

Go forward in thy feare:
Danger doth give thee choice to do, or beare.

My love of him, and truth, do make me loth
To thinke them wrong'd: And shall I wrong them both?

The good beleefe of mankinde is a sea
Where honor drownes, iniquitie goes free;
Whose thoughts (like sailes) for every weather be.

With shaking thoughts no hands can draw aright:
True hearts, to do unnobly, have no sp'rit.

The feare of some, is guilt with honestie;
Others, with love; thine, with false noblenesse.
Yet thinke not (Coward) wit can hide the shame
Of hearts, which while they dare not strike for feare,
Would make it vertue in them to forbeare.

No Caine: In men we women, when we love,
Aske faith, and heart. Our selves have feare, and wit.
In love how can thy soule, and mine agree?
I seeke revenge, thou preachest pietie.

More easie motions gentle hearts receive:
His fault was great; yet you may have redresse
In State, and honor, without such excesse.

Excesse the reason is, and meane of love;
And in the same excesse is malice joy'd:
I would be safe, and yet have him destroy'd.

If leave, or left the fate of kindnesse be,
By his example, what becomes of me?

If blinde to all, but to it selfe, be love;
Whence do your vowes, or whence this question move?
Since the true state of true affection is
Wonder, at others worth; Faith, without hire;
Unwearied Paine; unrecompens'd Desire.

Great hearts thus given away, in prison are;
Their strength, their bands; and good beleefe, their smart:
Love never severs reason from the heart.

My shame againe then unto me impart;
Restore my faith; and I do render thee
Those faithlesse vowes, which thou hast made to me.
For since, I see, the spungie hearts of men
Their hollowes gladly fill with womens love,
And nothing yeeld to them uncrusht againe:
What nature workes 'tis folly to complaine.

Mahomet, that wretch, hath done me iniurie;
He left my love, and he my life hath sought:
Caine! Line at ease; fame is an idle breath;
My body is enough 'twixt thee, and death.

Distract I am: my reason (like a cloud,
Before a winters storme) rides here, and there:
Like reedes, my thoughts are straight and crooked too;
With divers breaths, which divers passions blow.
Against the streame of truth must love still go?
Resolv'd I am that Mahomet shall die.

Shame spake this word: danger appeares not yet;
Time, like a med'cine, will asswage this paine,
And feare perchance bring backe good will againe.

It is not I that live in me, but you;
Whose will hath fashion'd all my thoughts anew.

Then on: When thoughts unite all care is ceas'd;
The heart, unfetter'd and the hope increas'd.
Out of his death I see occasion borne,
To greater power than needs to cover scorne.
For he the Justice rules, you rule the Warre;
His death divided powers will unite:
And in a broken course where dangers be,
Only the Crowne can put off miserie.

Farre be it off, our hopes should be so vaine.
Our secret love already tempteth God,
To warre him more with infidelity,
Would hasten vengeance, and make sharpe his rod.

God made strict lawes for vertues exercise,
An idle word, a wish transgresseth them:
Yet in a Throne remorse hath glorious eyes.
Alaham doth undermine the present State:
When he corrupted hath the peoples faith,
Thou hast the sword: authority makes way,
Her hand is next when Crownes become a prey.

We God and man will first trie with the death
Of Mahomet: If that do passe for good,
Hope easily makes occasion understood.

The end agree'd, the meanc is yet in doubt.

By sword.

That will be easie to descrie;
Danger to misse; and hard to do without.

By poyson then; wherof though doubts may grow,
What one alone may do, is hard to know.

It often failes: For instruments are base;
Slaves have too slavish hearts; a Basshas name
Is like a superstitious hallowed place.
Men must be forc'd, or wise that force the same.

By these two hands, that will not faile their heart,
It shall be wrought.
If poyson misse, the sword shall compasse it:
When chances often scape, at last they hit.

Fortune, and Love! Both Gods of humane might,
You like aduenture, see it rightly plac'd:
You live in kindnesse, see it not disgrac'd:

What I have vow'd, both God, and Nature hate;
My heart misgives; my soule doth prophecie,
That evill thoughts procure an evill fate.
But ah! my love I gave, and it gave me.
The choice is past: Thoughts now must thinke to do
Not what I freely am, but forc'd unto.
