CAElica, you that excell in flesh and wit,
In whose sweet heart Love doth both ebb and flow
Returning saith more than it tooke from it,
Whence doth the Change, the World thus speakes on, grow?

If Worthinesse do joy to be admired,
My soule, you know, only be-wonders you;
If Beauties glorie be to be desired,
My heart is nothing else; What need you new?

If loving joy of worths, beloved be,
And joyes not simple, but still mutuall,
Whom can you more love, than you have lou'd me?
Unlesse in your heart there be more than all;
Since Love no doomes-day hath, where bodies change,
Why should new be delight, not being strange?
