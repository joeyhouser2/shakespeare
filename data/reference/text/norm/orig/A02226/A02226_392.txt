The greatest pride of humane kind is Wit,
Which all Art out, and into methode drawes,
Yet Infinite, is farre exceeding it,
And so is Chance, of unknowne things the cause,
The feet of men against our feet do move,
No wit can comprehend the wayes of Love.

He that direct on paralells doth saile,
Goes Eastward out, and Eastward doth returne;
The shadowed man, whom Phoebus light doth faile,
Is blacke like him, his heat doth overburne;
The wheeles of high desire with force do move,
Nothing can fall amisse to them that love.

Vapours of earth which to the Sunne aspire,
As Natures tribute unto heate or light,
Are frozen in the midst of high desire,
And melted in sweet beames of selfe-delight,
And who to flye with Cupids wings will prove,
Must not bewaile these many ayres of Love.

Men that do use the Compasse of the Sea,
And see the Needle over Northward looke,
Some do the vertue in the Loadstone lay,
Some say, the stone it from the North-starre tooke,
And let him know that thinks with faith to move,
They once had eyes, that are made blind by love.
