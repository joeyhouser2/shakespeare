IF I had been in Troyan ground
When Ladie Venus tooke her wound:
IF I in Greekish campe had been,
Or clad in armour had been seene:
If Hector had by me been slaine,
Or Prince Aeneas put to paine:
If I the Machin huge had brought,
By Grecian guile so falsely wrought,
Or raysed it above the wall,
Of Troie that procurde the fall:
Then could I not thee (Cupid) blame,
If thou didst put me to this shame.
But I have alwaies been as true
To thee and thine in order due,
As ever was there any Wight,
That fayth and truth to Cupid plight.
I never yet despise thy lawe,
But aye of thee did stand in awe:
I never callde thee Bussard blinde,
I no such fault in thee did finde,
But thought my time well spent to be
That I imploide in seruing thee.
I wiste thou wert of force and powre
To conquere Princes in an howre
When thou retaindst me as thy man
I thought my selfe most happie than.
Since this is true that I have said,
Good Cupid let me have thy aide,
Helpe me to wreake my wrath aright
And succor me to worke my spight.
To thee it appertaines of due
Him to assist that is so true:
And thou of reason shouldst torment
Such as by wilfull will are bent
To triumph over those that serve
Thee in the field, and never swerve,
Go bend thy Bowe with hastie speede,
And make her Tigers hart to bleede.
Cause her that little sets by me,
Yet still to stand in awe of thee.
Let her perceive thy feruent fire,
And what thou art in raging ire,
Now showe thy selfe no man to be,
Let her a God both feele and see.
She forceth not my cutting paine,
Her vowed othes she wayes as vaine.
She sits in peace at quiet rest,
And scornes at me so dispossest.
She laughes at thee, and mocks thy might,
Thou art not Cupid in her sight.
She spites at me without cause whie,
She forceth not although I die.
I am her captive bounde in Give
And dare not once for lyfe to strive.
The more to thee I call and crie,
To rid me from this crueltie,
The more she seekes to worke her ire,
The more she burnes with scalding fire.
And all for Cupids sake I bide,
From whose decrees I do not glide.
Wherefore (I say) go bende thy Bow,
And to her hart an Arrow throw:
That Dart which breaketh harts of flint
And gives the cruell crasing dint,
Upon her crabbed breast bestow,
That she thy force and powre may know:
That she a Myrrour may be knowne
To such as be thy deadly fone,
So shall they good example take,
How to abuse men for thy sake.
Let her (good Cupid) understande,
That I am thine both hart and hande.
And to play quittance force a fire,
That she may frie with whote desire
Of me, whome earst she put to paine,
And this is all that I would gaine.
