Here graved is a good and Godly Wight,
That yeelded hath her cynders to the soyle,
Who ran her race in vertues tylt aright
And never had at Fortunes hand the foyle:
The guide was God whome she did aye ensue,
And Vertue was the marke whereat she thrue.
Descending of a house of worthie fame
She linckt at length with one of egall state,
Who though did chaunge her first and former name,
Did not enforce her vertues to rebate:
For Dannat she Dame Arhundle was hight,
whose Feere was knowne to be a worthy Knight.
Her beautie I not blaze ne brute at all,
(Though with the best she might therein compare)
For that it was to age and fortune thrall:
Her thewes I touch which were so passing rare,
As being earthde and reaft her vitall breath,
Her chiefest part doth live and conquer death.
Let Spite not spare to speake of her the wurst,
Let Enuie feede upon her godly life,
Let Rancour rage, let Hatreds bellie burst,
Let Zoill now unsheath his cutting knife:
For death hath closde her corse in Marble grave,
Her soule is fled in Skies his seate to have.
Let Leyster laugh that such a Mirrour bred:
Let Matrons mourne for losse of their renowne,
Let Cornewall crie since Dannat now is ded,
Let Vertue eke do on her mourning gowne:
For she is rest that was at Vertues beck
Whome Fortune had no powre to give the check.
