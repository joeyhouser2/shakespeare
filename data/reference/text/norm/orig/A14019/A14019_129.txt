For to revoke to pensive thought
And troubled head my former plight,
How I by earnest sute have sought
And griefull paines a loving Wight
For to accoy, accoy,
And breede my joy
Without anoy, makes saltish bryne
To flush out of my vapord eyne.
¶ To thinke upon the sundrie snares
And privie Panthers that were led
To forge my daylie dolefull cares,
Whereby my hoped pleasures fled,
Doth plague my hart, my hart,
With deadly smart,
Without desart, that have indurde
Such woes, and am not yet recurde,
¶Was never day came on my hed
Wherein I did not sue for grace,
Was never night but I in bed
Unto my Pillow tolde my case,
Bayning my brest, my brest,
For want of rest,
With teares opprest, yet remedie none
Was to be found for all my mone.
¶If she had dained my good will
And recompenst me with her Love,
I would have been her Vassell still,
And never once my hart remove:
I did pretend, pretend,
To be her Friend
Unto the end, but she refusde
My loving hart, and me abusde.
¶I did not force upon the spite
And venemous stings of hissing Snakes,
I wayed not their words a Mite,
That such a doe at Lovers makes:
I did reioyce, reioyce,
To have the voyce
Of such a choyce, and smild to see
That they reported so of me.
¶Oh me most luckie Wight (quoth I)
At whome the people so repine,
I trust the rumor that doth flie
Will force her to my will incline,
And like well me, well me
Whome she doth see,
Her love to be, unfainedly,
In whome she may full well affie.
¶But now at length I plainely vew
That woman never gave her brest,
For they by kindly course will rue
On such as seeme to love them best:
And will relent, relent
And be content,
When nought is ment, save friendly hart
And love for never to depart.
¶Some cruell Tiger lent her Te•
And fostred her with savage Pap,
That can not finde in hart to let
A man to love her, since his hap
Hath so assignde, assignde
To have his minde
To love inclinde, in honest wise
Whome she should not of right despise.
¶But since I see her stonie hart
Cannot be pierst with pitties Launce,
Since nought is gainde but wofull smart,
I do intende to breake the daunce,
And quite forgo, forgo
My pleasaunt Fo,
That paines me so, and thinkes in fine
To make me like to Circes Swine:
¶I cleane defie her flattering face,
I quite abhorre her luring lookes:
As long as Jove shall give me grace
She never comes within my bookes,
I do detest, detest
So false a Guest
That breedes unrest, where she should plant
Her love, if pittie did not want.
¶Let her go seeke some other Foole,
Let her inrage some other Dolt:
I have been taught in Platos Schoole
From Cupids Banner to revolt:
And to forsake, forsake
As fearefull Snake,
Such as do make, a man but smart
For bearing them a faithfull hart.
