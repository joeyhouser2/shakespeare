What shold I shed my teares to show mine inward pain
Since that the Jewell I have lost may not be had again.
Yet bootelesse though it be to utter covert smart
It is a meane to cure the griefe, and make a joyfull hart.
Wherefore I say to you that have enioyde your Love,
Lament with me in wofull wise for loosing of my Dove.
You Turtle Cocks that are your loving Honnes bereft,
And do bewaile your cruell chaunce that you alive are left:
Come hither, come I say, come hie in haste to me,
Let eyther make his dolefull plaint amid this drearie tree.
A fitter place than this may no where else be found
For friendly Eccho here will cause ech cry to yeeld a sound.
In youth it was my luck on such a Dove to light,
As by good nature wan my love, she was my whole delite
A fresher fowle than mine for shape and beauties hue,
Was never any man on earth that had the hap to vewe.
Dame Nature her had framde so perfite in her kinde
As not the spiteful man himself one fault in her could finde.
Her eie so passing pure, her beake so brave and fit,
The stature of her lims so small, her head so full of wit,
Her neck of so good fyse, her plume of colour white,
Her legs and feete so finely made, though seldom sene in sight:
Each part so fitly pight as none mought chaunge his place.
Nor any Bird could lightly have so good and brave a grace.
But most of all that I did fansie, was her voyce,
For swete it was unto mine care, and made the hart reioyce.
No sooner could I come in place where she was set,
But up she rose, and joyfull would her Make and lover met.
About my tender neck she would have clasped tho,
And laid her beake betwixt my lips, sweete kisses to besto.
And ought besides that mought have pleasurde me at all,
was never man that had a birde so fit to play withall.
when I for joy did sing, she would have song with me,
when I was wo, my grief was hirs, she wold not plesant be
But (oh) amid my joyes came cruell canckred Death.
And spiting at my pleasures rest my loving bird her breath
who finding me alack, and absent on a day,
Caught bow in hand, and strak her down, a breding as she lay
Since I have cause to waile the death of such a Dove.
(Good Turtles) help me to lament the losse of my true love.
The Tree whereon she sat shall be the place where I
will sing my last, and end my life: for (Turtles) I must die.
you know it is our kinde, we can not live alone,
More pleasant is the death to us then life when love is gone
To tell a farther tale my famting breath denies,
And selfe same death the slue my Dove, begins to close mine eies.
