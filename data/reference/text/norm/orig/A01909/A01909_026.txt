Synce I so long have lyved in pain and burnt for love of the,
(O cruel hart) doste thou no more esteame the Love of me,
Regardst thou not, the health of hym? that the, above the rest
Of Creatures all, and next to God, hath dearest in his brest.
Is pytie placed from the so farre is gentlenes exylde?
Hast thou ben fostred in the Caves, of Wolves or Lyons wylde?
Hast thou ben so? why then no force, the lesse I meruayle I,
Such as the Damme, such is the yong experyence trewe doth trye.
Syth thou art of so fyerce a mynde, why dyd not God then place
In the, with such a Tygers Harte, a fowle yll faverde face?
Sure for no other ende but that, he lykes no Lovers trade,
And the therfore a ragynge Fende, an Angels face hathe made.
Such one as thou, was Go•gon ones as auncient Poets tell,
Who with her Beautie maz•d men, and nowe doth raygne in H•ll.
But mercye yet, of the I crave, if ought in the remayne,
And let me not so long the force, of flamyng fyre sustayne,
Let pytie joynde with beautie be, so shall I not dysdayne.
My blud, my hart, my lyfe to spende with toyle, with stryfe, and payne,
To do the good, my breath to loose, if nede shall so requyre,
But for my seruyce and my paynes thou gyvest me hate for hyre.
Well now take this for ende of all, I love and thou doste hate,
Thou lyvest in pleasures happely. and I in wretched staie.
Paynes can not last for evermore, but tyme and ende will trye,
And tyme shall tell me in my age, How youth led me awrye.
Thy face that me tormented so, in tyme shall sure decaye,
And all that I do lyke or love, shall vanysh quyte awaye,
Thy face in tyme shall wrynckled be, at which I shall be glad,
To see thy forme transformed thus, that made me once so sad,
Than shall I blame my foly moch and thanke the mightyest kyng
That hath me saved till such a daye, to se so fonde a thyng.
And till that tyme I will keepe close my flames and let them blase,
All secretly within my brest, no man on me shall gase.
I will not trespasse synfully, for God shall geve me grace
To se the tyme wherin I shall neglecte thy folysh face,
And till that tyme adieu to thee, God keepe thee far from me,
And sende thee in that place to dwell, that I shall never see.
