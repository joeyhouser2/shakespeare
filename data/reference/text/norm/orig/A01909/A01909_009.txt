Sirenus Shephard good and thou, that hast yll lucke in love,
The cause of all my hurt by whom my sutes could never prove.
God never let that I shuld seeke, to be revenged of the,
For when I might have ben with case, yet wold not suffer me.
The Love that I Duerda bare, on the to showe my Spyte:
On the in whom my Ladye fayre, had once her whole delyght,
If thy myshaps do not me greve, my mischiefes never ende,
Thynke not sirenus that bycause, Diana was thy frend,
I beare the worser wyl assure thy self so base my love never semde
That only I shuld favour her. but all that she estemde.
Thou eyther art siluanus borne, Example for to gyve,
To us that know not how, when Fortune frownes to lyve,
Or els hath Nature placed in the so strong and stoute a mynde.
Suffysynge not, thine yls alone to beare, but meanes to fynde,
That may the Griefes of others help, I se thou art so bent,
That Fortune can the not amase, for all her mysciefes ment,
I promys the siluanus heare, tyme playne in the doth show,
How dayly she dis•overs things, that erst dyd men not know.
I can not beare the Gryefes I feale, my force is all to faynt,
I never could as thou canst stynt, the teares of my complaynt.
Diana hath procured the paynes, that I shall never ende,
When fyrst she falst her troth to me, she kyld a faythfull frende.
I meruayle how she could so soone, put the out of her mind,
I well remembre synce thou wents•e alone I dyd her fynd.
In place that sorow semde to shape, where no man stood her nye,
But only (I unhappy wretche,) that herd her wofull crye,
And this with teares alowde she said, O wretche in yll tyme borne.
What chaunce hast thou? that thus thou hast Sirenus swete forlorne.
Gyve over pleasures now, Let never Joye the please,
Seke all the cruell meanes thou canst that may thy hart dysease.
When thou doste hym forget I wysh, all mischifes on the lyght,
And after death, the Fendes of Hell, torment thy lyvyng spryght.
What man wold here beleve? that she that thus could speake,
In so shorte tyme as I have bene awaye. wolde promys breake.
O stedfastnes and Constancy, how seldome are you founde:
In womens harts to have your seats, Or long abydyng ground?
Who looke how much more earnest they, at fyrst their hearts do set,
So much more sooner ever more, where late they loved, forget:
Full well could ever I beleve, all women gylty of this:
Save her alone, in whom I judge, never nature wrought amis:
But sins her maryage how she speeds Siluanus I pray the tell?
Some say she lykes it very ill, and I beleve it well:
For Delius he that hath her now, although he welthy be,
Is but a lout and hath in hym, no hansome qualytie:
For as for all, such shynges wherin, we Shepeheardes have delyght,
As in Quaiting, Leaping, Singing or to sound a Bagpype ryght:
In all these thinges he is but an Asse, and nothynge do he can,
They saye tys quallities but tush, Its ryches makes a man:
What woman is that that commeth here, Siluanus canst thou tell?
Its one hath sped as well in Love, as we, I knowe her well:
She is one of fayre Guerdas frendes, who keeps her beasts below,
Not far from hence bi her thou maist, Dianas State wel know.
She loved hear a Shephearde cald, Alanius longe a go:
Who favers one ysmenia now, the cause of all her wo:
No place so fyt for the as this, Lo heare Siluanus stands,
Who hath receaved lyke luck to thine at cruel Fortunes hands,
This company besemes the well, Fayr Shepheards both good deane,
To the Seluagina eke of Hope, whom Love hath spoyled cleane:
A thousande better dayes I wysh, than thou hast had before,
At length may better Fortune fall, For worse can not be more.
To truste the fayned words of men, Loe, thus poore womens speeds,
And men do smarte not through your words but your unconstant deeds.
For you when earnestlyest you love, no thyng can chaunce so lyght.
But if a toye com in your Brayne, your mynde is altered quyght.
If we but ones, absent our selves, the shortest tyme we maye,
So much unconstant is your minds Love soreth strayght awaye,
Example take Sire•us here, whom once Diana lovd,
As all we know, and looke how soone her mynd is now removd:
No, no, there is not one of you, that constant can remayne:
You judge but of malicious hart, and of a Jalouse brayne.
All thyngs you do your selves esteme, and men must beare no blame.
Of your dissemblyng noughty deeds, we women beare the shame.
Fayre Damesell if you can perceyve Siluanus true doth saye
There is not one amongst you all, but doth from reason straye.
What is the cause that women thus? in their unconstancye,
Do cast a man from hyest hap, to deepest myserye?
Its nothyng els, I you assure, but that you know not well,
What thing is love, and what you have, in hand you can not tell.
Your symple wyts are all to weake, Unfayned love to know,
And therof doth forgetfulnes, in you so shortly grow.
Si•enus judge not so of us, our wyts be not so base,
But that we know as well as you, whats what in every case.
And women eke, there are ynow that could if they were brought
Teache men to lyve, and more to love, if love myght well be tought,
And for all this, yet do I thynke, no thyng can worser be.
Than womens state. it is the worst, I thynke of each degree.
For if they show but gentle words you thynke for love they dye.
And if they speake not when you list, than strayght you say, they are hye.
And that they ar, disdainfull Dames, and if they chaunce to talke.
Than cownt you then for chatring Pies whose tongs must alwayes walke.
And if perhaps they do forbeare, and Sylence chaunce to keepe,
Than tush, she is not for company, she is but a symple sheepe.
And if they beare good will to one, then strayght they are judged nought
And if yll name to shun they leave, Unconstant they are thought.
Who nowe can please these Jalouse heads, the faute is all in you,
For women never wold chaunge their minds if men wold styll be true,
To this, I well could answere you, but tyme doth byd me staye,
And women must the last worde have no man may say them naye.
Passe over this, and let us here, what lucke you have had in love,
And showe if ever love of man, your constaunt hart could move.
No fytter place can be than this, here maye you safely rest,
Thus syttyng here, declare at large, the secretes of your brest.
Naye: lenger here we maye not byde, but home we must awaye,
Loe how the Son denies his Beames deprivyng us of daye.
