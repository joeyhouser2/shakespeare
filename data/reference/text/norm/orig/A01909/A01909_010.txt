We fere not we, the tomblyng world we breake no sleaps by nyght.
Both place and tyme my Coridon exhorteth me to synge,
Not of the wretched Lovers lyves, but of the immortall kynge.
Who gyves us pasture for our beasts and blesseth our encrease:
By whom, while other cark and toyle we lyve at home with ease.
Who keepes us down, from climyng hye where honour breeds debate,
And here hath graunted us to lyve in symple Shephards state,
A lyfe that sure doth fare exceade, each other kynd of lyfe:
O happy state, that doth content, How farre be we from stryfe?
Of hym therfore, me lyst to synge, and of no wanton toyes,
For hym to love, and hym to prayse, surmounts all other Joyes.
O Shephards leave Cupidoes Camp, the ende wherof is vyle,
Remove Dame Venus from your eies and harken here a whyle.
A God there is, that guyds the Globe, and framde the fyckle Spheare,
And placed hath, the Starres above, that we do gase on here,
By whon we lyve, (unthankful beasts) by whom we have our health,
By whom we gayne our happy states by whom we get our wealth.
A God: that sends us that we nede, a God: that us defends.
A God: from whom the Angels hye, on mortall men attends.
A God: of such a Clemencie, that who so hym doth love
Shall here be sure to rest a whyle, and alwayes rest above.
But we, for hym do lytell care, His Heasts we nought esteme,
But hunt for thyngs that he doth hate most pleasaunt those do seme,
(Unthankfull mysers) what do we? what meane we thus to straye?
From such a God, so mercyfull, to walke a worser waye?
Maye nought his benefyts procure? maye nought his mercyes move?
Maye nothynge bynde, but nedes we must? gyve hate to hym for love?
O happy (ten tymes) is the man, (a Byrde full rare to fynde)
That loveth God with all his hart, and kepes his lawes in mynde.
He shalbe blest in all his works, and safe in every tyme,
He shall swete quietnes enioye, whyle other smarte for Cryme.
The threatnyng chaunces of the world shall never hym annoye,
When Fortune frowns on foolish men he shalbe sure to joye.
For why? the Aungels of the Lorde, shall hym defende alwayes,
And set hym free, at every harmes, and hurts at all assayes.
Even he that kept the Prophet safe, from mouthes of Lyons wylde,

And fyrst know thou that every man, that from this God doth go,
And folows lust, hym he acountes, to be his deadly foe,
This myghty Kyng of whom we talk, as he is mercyfull,
And suffers long, revengyng slow, So when we be thus dull,
That we wyl not perceave in tyme, the goodnes of his grace,
His favour straight, he doth withdraw and tournes a way his face.
And to him selfe then doth he say, How long shall I permit
These stubburne beastes, for to rebell? and shall I love them yet,
That hate me thus? or have I nede their lovynge mynds to crave?
I aske no more but only love, and that I can not have.
Well, wel, I will not care for them, that thus do me dyspyse,
Let them go lyve, even as they lyst, I turne awaye mine eyes.
When God hath thus said to him self, Then doth the braynlesse foole,
Cast Brydle of, and out he runnes, neglectynge vertues Scoole,
Then doth the Devyl geve him lyne, and let him rune at large,
And Pleasure makes his Mariner, to row in vyces Barge,
Then up the Sayles of wilfulnes, he hoyses hie in hast,
And fond Affection blowes hym forth, a wynd that Pluto plast,
Then cuttes he swyft, the seas of sin, and through the Chanell deape,
With Joyful mynd, he fleets a pace, whom Pleasure bryngs a sleape,
Then who so happy thinks hym selfe? who dreames of joy but he?
Tush, tush, sayth he: to thynk of God, Inage suffiseth me,
Now will I passe my pleasaunt youth, Such toyes becomes this age,
And God shall followe me sayth he, I will not be his page,
