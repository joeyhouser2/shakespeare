Fye Fye, I lothe to speake wylt thou my lust,
Compell me nowe, to do so foule an acte.
Nay rather God, with Flame consume to dust.
My carry on vyle, then I perfourme this facte
Let rather thoughtes, that long, have weryed me:
Or sycknes such as Fancy fonde hath brought,
O gapyng Hell, dryne me now downe to the,
Let boylyng syghes, consume me all to nought.
Ons musynge as I sat, and Candle burnynge bye,
When all were husht I myght discern a symple selye Flye.
¶That flewe before mine eyes, with free reioysynge Hart,
And here and there, with wings did play as voyde of payne and smart,
¶Somtyme by me she sat, when she had playde her fyll,
And ever when she rested had aboute she flyttered styll.
¶When I perceyud her well, reioysyng in her place,
O happy Flye quoth I, and eake, O worme in happy case.
¶Which two of us is best? I that have reason? no:
But thou that reason art without and therwith voyde of woe.
¶ I lyve and so doste thou, but I lyve all in payne,
And Subiect am to her alas, that makes my Gryefe her gayne.
When I do heare thy name, alas my hart doth ryse:
And seekes fourthwith to se the salve that most contentes mine eys.
But when I se thy Face, that hath procured my payne,
Then boyles my blud in every part, and beates in every vayne?
Thy voice when I do heare, then collour comes and goes,
Some tyme as pale as Earth I looke, some tyme as red as Rose.
If thy sweete Face do smyle, then who so well as I?
If thou but cast a scornefull looke, then out alas I dye.
But styll I lyve in payne, my fortune wylleth so,
That I shuld burne and thou yet know, no whytt of all my wo.
Unhappye tonge, why dydste thou not consent
When fyrst mine eyes dyd vewe that Princely face,
To show good will, that hart opprest than ment.
And whylst tyme was, to sewe for present grace.
O fayntyng Hart, why dydst thou then conceale?
Thine inwarde Fyers, that flamde in every vayne,
When pytie and gentlenes, were bent to heale.
Why dydst thou not, declare thy ragyng payne?
When well thou mightst have moved her gentle mynde,
Why dydste thou than, kepe backe thy wofull playn?
Thou knewste full well, redres is hard to fynde,
When in thy own affayres, thy corage faynts.
But synce she is gon, bewaile thy grief no moore
Synce thou thy selfe, wart Causer of the Soore.
