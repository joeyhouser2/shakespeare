Olde Socrates, whose wysdome dyd excell,
And past the reache, of wysest in his tyme,
Surmounted all, that on the earth dyd dwell,
That Craggye Hyls, of vertue hye dyd clyme,
That Socrates, my Cobham dyd allowe,
Each man in youth, hym selfe in Glasse to vew,
And wyld them oft, to use the same, but how?
Not to delyght, in forme of fadyng hew.
Nor to be proude therof, as many be,
But for to stryve, by beautie of the mynde,
For to adourne, the beautie he doth se.
If warlyke forme, Dame Nature hym assygnde,
By vertuous lyfe, than countenaunce for to get,
That shall deface, the fayrest of them all,
Such Beautie as no age nor yeares will fret:
That flyes with fame, when fyckle forme doth fayle,
Thus much I saye, that here to the present,
My wordes a Glasse for the to looke upon.
To the whom God, in tender yeares hath lent,
A towardenes, that maye be mused upon,
Such towardenes, as in more graver yeares,
Doth sure a hope, of greater thyngs pretende,
Thy noble mynde, that to thy frendes appeare,
Doth showe the blud, wherof thou doste descende,
The gentlenes, thou usest unto all such,
As smallye have deserved good will of the,
Doth showe the grace, thou hast that sure is much,
As ever yet, in any I dyd se,
Thy wyt as rype, as Nature well can gyve,
Declares a greater hope than all the rest,
That shall remayne, to the whilst thou doste lyve,
In desperate yls, a Medycyne ever prest.
The good behavyour, of thy selfe in place
Whersoever that thou chauncest for to lyght,
So much both beautie, mynde and wyt doth grace
As well can be requyred of any wyght.
What resteth now? but only God to prayse,
Of whom thou hast receaved these Gyftes of thyns,
So shalt thou long, lyve heare with happye dayes,
And after Death, the starrye Skyes shalt clyme,
Let noughtye men, saye what they lyst to the,
Trade thou thy selfe, in seruyng hym above,
No sweter seruyce can devysed be,
Whom if thou fearst, and faythfully doste love,
Be sure no thyng, on earth shall the annoye,
Be sure he will, the from each harme defende,
Be sure thou shalt, long tyme thy lyfe enioye,
And after many yeares to have a blessed ende.
