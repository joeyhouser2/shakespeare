Syth Phebus now begins to flame,
O frende Amintas deare:
And placed hath his gorgeous globe in midste of all the Spheare
And fron that place doth cast his Beames; where (they that fratres defyne)
Lyes poynt (do saye) that termed is, ryght Equinoctial lyne.
wheras the Rain doth cause to spring, each herbe and floure in fyelde
A•d forceth ground (the spoyld of grene Old lye,) newe grene to yelde.
Let shepherds us yelde also tales, as best becommes the tyme:
Such tales as Winter stormes have stayde in countrey Phets Ryme.
Begyn to synge Amintas thou, for why? thy wyt is best:
And many a laged sawe lies hyd within thine aged brest.
Ofte have I heard, of Shephards old, thy fame reported true,
No Herdman lives: but knowes the praise, to olde Amintas due:
B•gyn therfore, and I gyve care, for talke doth me delyght,
Go Boye: go dryve the Beasts to fede whyle he his mynde resyght.
Thy prayses Da••nes are to great, and more for me than meete:
Nor ever I, such saged sawes, coulde synge in Verses sweete.
And now, to talke of spring time tales my heares to hoare, do growe
Such tales as these, I tolde in, tyme, when youthfull yeares dyd flowe.
But synce, I can not the denye, thy Fathers love doth bynde:
In symple Songe I will adresse my selfe, to showe my mynde.
Longe hast thou Daphnes me requyred the state of Love to tell,
For in my youth, I knewe the force, and passions all, full well.
Nowe Love therfore I will defyne and what it is declare,
which way poore souls it doth entrap and howe it them doth snare.
My Boie, remove my beasts fron hens and dryve them farther downe,
Upon the Hylles, let them go feade, that joyne to yender towne,
O Cupyde kynge of fyerye Love, ayde thou my syngynge Verse,
And teache me heare the cause and case, Of Lovers to reherse,
Direct my tong, in trothe to treade, with Furye fyll my brayne,
That I may able be to tell, the cause of Lovers payne.
Opinions divers coulde I showe, but chiefest of them all,
I will declare: and for the rest, with silence leave I shall.
A servent Humour, (some do judge) within the Head doth lye,
Which yssuyng forth with poysoned beames doth rou fron eye to eye:
And taking place abrode in heads, a whyle doth fyrmely rest:
Till Phrensie framde in Fancie fond▪ discends from hed, to brest.
And poison strong, fron eies outdrawn do•h perce the wretched harte,
And all infectes the bloud aboute, and boyles in every parte:
Thus: when the beames, infected hath, the wofull Lovers blud:
Then Sences all, do strayght decaye, opprest with Furyes flud.
Then Lybertie withdrawes her self, and Bondage beares the swaye,
Affection blynd then leades the hart, and Wyt, is wownde awaye.
O Daphn•s then, the paines appeare, and tormentes all of hell.
Then sekes, the selye wounded soule, the flames for to expell.
But all to late, alas he stryves, for Fancie beares the stroke
And he, must toyle (no helpe there is) in slavysshe seruyle yoke.
His blud corrupted all within, doth boyle in every vayne,
Than sekes he howe to sewe for salve that maye redresse his payne.
And when the face, he doth beholde by which he shulde have ayde,
And sees no helpe, then lookes he long, a•d trembleth all afrayde.
And museth at the framed shape, that hath his lyfe in handes:
Nowe fast he flies, aboute the flames, nowe styll amased standes:
Yet Hope relieves, his hurtful Heate and Will doth Payne make lyght,
And all the griefes, that then he feeles doth Presence styll requyght.
But when the Lyght absented is, and Beames in hart remayne,
Then flames the Fyre fresh agayne, and newe begyns his Payne.
Then longe he lookes, his losse to se, then sobbes, and syghes abounde,
Then mourneth he, to mys the marke that erst to soone he founde.
Then shadefull places oute he lookes, and all alone he lyves,
Erylynge Joye, and myrth from him, hymselfe to waylynge gyves,
And styll his minde theron doth muse and styll, therof he prates,
O Daphnes here I swere to the, no griefe to Lovers state.
If he but ones beholde the place, where he was wont to mete,
The pleasaunt forme that hym enflamd, and joyfull Countnaunce swete.
The place (a wonderous thing I tell) his gryefe augmenteth newe,
Yet styll he sekes the place to se, that moste he shulde esc•ewe.
If but the name rehearsed be, (a thynge more straunge to heare)
Then Colour commes and goes in hast then quaketh he for feare,
The verye name, hath such a force, that it can dase the mynde,
And make the man amasde to stande, what force hath Love to bynde?
Affection none to this is lyke, it doth surmownt them all,
Of grei•fes, the greatest greif no doubt is to be Venus thrall,
And therfore, Daphnes nowe beware, for thou art yonge, and fre,
Take heade of vewynge faces longe, for losse of Lybertye,
I shall not nede (I thynke) to byd the, to detest the Cryme,
Of wycked love, that Jove did use, In Ganimedes tyme,
For rather wolde I (thoo it be much) that thou shuldest seake the fyre,
Of lawfull Love, that I have tolde, than burne with such desyre,
And thus an end, I weryed am, my wynde is olde, and faynt,
Such matters I, do leave to such, as finer farre can paint,
Fetche in the Gote: that goes astraye, and dryve hym to the folde,
My yeares be great I wyl be gone, for spryngtyme nyghts be colde.
Great thankes to the, for this thy tale Amintas here I gyve:
But never can I make amendes to the whilste I do lyve.
Yet for thy paynes (no recompenc•) a small rewarde have here.
A whistle framed longe ago, wherwith my father deare
His joyfull beasts, was wont to kepe. No Pype for tune so swete
Might shepharde ever yet po•ses. (a thynge for the full mete.)
