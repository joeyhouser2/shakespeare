Shall rigour raigne where ruth hath run, shall fansie now forsake?
Shall fortune lose that favour wonne, shall not your anger slake?
Shall hatefull harte be had in you, that frendly did pretende,
Shall slipper thought and faithe untrue, that harte of yours defende?

Shall nature shewe your beautie faire, that gentle semes to be?
Shall frowardnesse your fancies heire, be of more force then she?
Shall now disdaine the dragg of death, directe and leade the waie?
Shall all the imps upon the yearth, reioyce at my decaie?

Shall this the seruice of my youth, have such reward at laste?
Shall I receive rigour of ruthe, and be from favour caste?
Shall I therefore berent my heares, with wights that wishe to dye?
Or shall I bathe my self with teares, to feede your fickle eye.

No, no, I shall in paine lye still, with turtle Dove moste true,
And vowe my self to witt and will, their counsels to ensue:
Good Ladies all that Lovers be, and that to be pretende,
Give place to witt, let reason seme, your enemie to defende.

Lest that you thinke as I have thought, your self to strove in vaine,
And so to be in thraldome brought, with me to suffer paine.
