O Heavenly God, O Father deare, cast doune thy tender eye,
Upon a wretche that prostrate here, before thy throne doeth lye:
O powre thy precious oyle of grace, into my wounded harte,
O let the dropps of mercie swage, the rigour of my smarte.

My faintyng foule suppressed sore, with carefull clogge of synne,
In humble sorte submitts it self, thy mercie for to winne:
Graunt mercie then O Saviour sweete, to me moste wofull thrail,
Whose mornfull crie to thee, O Lorde, doeth still for mercie call.

Thy blessed will I have despised, upon a stubborne mynde,
And to the swaie of worldly thyngs, my self I have enclinde:
Forgettyng heaven and heavenly powers, where God and saincts do dwell
My life had like to tread the pathe, that leads the waie to hell.

But now my Lorde my lode starre bright, I will no more do so,
To thynke upon my former life, my harte doeth melte for woe:
Alas I sigh, alas I sobbe, alas I do repent:
That ever my licencious will, so wickedly was bent.

Sith thus therefore with yernfull plaint, I do thy mercie crave,
O Lorde for thy greate mercies sake, let me thy mercie have:
Restore to life the wretched soule, that els is like to die,
So shall my voyce unto thy name, syng praise eternallie.

Now blessed be the Father first, and blessed be the Sonne,
And blessed be the holie Ghoste, by whom all thyngs are doen:
Blesse me O blessed Trinitie, with thy eternall grace,
That after death my soule maie have, in heaven a dwellyng place.
