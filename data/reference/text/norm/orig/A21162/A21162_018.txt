THE wrong is great, the payne above my power,
That yeeldes such care in doubtfull dens to drowne:
Such hap is hard where Fortune doth so lower,
As freendly looke is turnd to froward frowne.
Is this the trust that faithfull freendes can finde?
With those that yet have promise broke?
By deedes in dout, as though no wordes can binde,
A vowed freend to hold him to his yoke.

O faithlesse freend, what can assure your mind,
That doubtes so soone before you have cause why?
To what hard hap doth Fortune here me bind,
When words nor deedes can no where satisfye.
What can I write? that hath not oft bine said,
What have I said? that hath not bind affyrmed:
What not approved? that ought to be assayed,
Or what is vowed? that shall not be perfourmed.

Cast of mistrust, in hast no credite give,
To this or that, that breedeth freendes unrest:
No doubt at all, but trust me if I live,
My deedes shall prove, that all is for the best.
And this beleeve, the Sea shall cease to flowe,
The Sunne to shine within the setled skye:
All thinges on earth shall leave to spring and growe,
Yea every Foule shall want his winges to flye.

Eare I in thought shall seeme once to retyre,
If you my freend remaine as I desyre:
Nowe lose no time, but use that while you may,
Forget not this, a dogge shall have a day.
