JN every wight some sundry sort of pleasure I do find,
Which after travaile he doth seeke to ease his toyling minn.
Diana with her trayning chase, of hunting had delight,
Against the fearful Deare, she could direct her shotte aright.
The lofty yeeres in every age, doth stil embrace the same,
The sport is good, if vertue do assist the cheereful game.

Minerua in her chattering armes her courage doth aduaunce,
In trial of the bloudy wars, she giveth luckie chaunce.
For savegard men imbrace the same, which do so needful seeme
That noble harts their cheefe delightes in use thereof esteeme,
In warlike games to ride or trye the force of armes they use,
And base the man we do account that doth the same refuse.

The silver sound of musickes cordes, doth please Apollos wit,
A science which the heavens aduaunce, where it deserves to sit,
A pleasure apt for every wight, celeefe to careful mind,
For woe redresse, for care a salve, for sadnes helpe we find.
The soveraigne praise of Musicke stil, doth cause the Poets faine.
That whirling Spheres, and eake the heavens do hermonie retaine.

I hard, that these three powers, at variance lateli fel,
Whiles each did prayse his own delight, the other to excel.
Then Fame, as an indifferent judge, to end the case they call,
The praise pronounced by her to them, indifferently doth fall.
Diana health and strength maintaine, Minerua force doth tame,
And Musicke gives a sweete delight, to further others game.

These three delightes to hawtie mindes the worthiest are estemed,
If vertue be annexed to them, they rightly be so deemed.
With joy they do revive the witte with sorow oft opprest,
And never suffer solempne greefe to long in mind to rest.
Be wise in mirth, and seeke delight, the same do not abuse,
In honest mirth a happy joy we ought not to refuse.
