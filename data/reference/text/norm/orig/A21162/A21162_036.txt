Policrates whose passing hap, causd him to lose his fate,
A golden ring cast in the seas to change his constant state,
And in a fish yet at his bourd, the same he after found,
Thus Fortune loe, to whom she takes, for bountie doth abound.

The myzers unto might she mounts, a common case we see,
And mighty to great misery, she sets in low degree:
Whom she to day doth reare on hie, upon her whirling wheele,
To morrow next she dingeth downe, and casteth at her heele.
No measure hath she in her gifts, she doth reward each sort.
The wise that counsell have, no more, then fooles that maketh sport.
She useth never partiall handes, for to offend or please,
Geve me good Fortune all men sayes, and throw me in the seas.

It is no fault or worthines, that makes men fall or rise,
I rather be borne Fortunate, then to be very wise.
The blindest man right soone, that by good Fortune guided is,
To whom that pleasant Fortune pipes, can never daunce amis.
