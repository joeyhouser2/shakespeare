The mountaines hie whose loftie topps, doeth mete the hautie sky
The craggie rocke that to the sea, free passage doeth deny:
The aged Oke that doeth resist, the force of blustryng blaste,
The pleasaunt hearbe that every where, a fragrant smell doeth caste.
The Lions force whose courage stout, declares a princelike might,
The Eagle that for worthinesse, is borne of kyngs in fight:
The Serpent eke whose poisoned jaws, doeth belche out venime vile,
The lothsome Tode that shumeth light, and liveth in exile.
These these I saie and thousands more, by trackt of tyme decaie,
And like to tyme do quite consume, and bade from tyme to claie:
But my true harte and seruice vowed, shall laste tyme out of minde,
And still remaines as thine by dome, as Cupid hath assingde.
My faithe loe here I vowe to thee, my trothe thou knowest right well,
My goods my frends, my life is thine, what neede I more to tell?
I am not mine, but thine I vowe, thy hests I will obeye,
And serve thee as a seruaunt ought, in pleasyng if I maie:
And sithe I have no fliyng wyngs, to see thee as I wishe,
Ne sinnes to cut the silver streames, as doeth the glidyng fishe.
Wherefore leave now forgetfulnesse, and sende againe to me,
And straine thy azured vaines to write, then I maie gretyng see:
And thus farewell more deare to me, then chiefest frende I have,
Whose Love in harte I minde to shrine, till death his fee do crave.
