IN choise of frends what hap had I, to chuse one of Cirens kind
whose harp, whose pipe, whose melody could fede my ears and make me blind
Whose pleasant voice made me forget, that in sure trust is gret deceit.

In trust I see is treason founde, and man to man deceitfull is,
And whereas treasure doeth abounde, of flatterers there do not misse:
Whose painted speache, and outward show, do seme as frends and be not so.

Would I have thought in thee to be, the nature of the Crocadill:
Which if a man a slepe maie see, with bloudie thirst desires to kill:
And then with teares a while gan wepe, the death of him thus slain a slepe

O favell false, thou traitor borne, what mischief more might thou devise
Then thy deare frend to have in scorne, and hym to wound in sundry wise
Which still a frende pretends to be, and are not so by proofe I see,
Fie, fie, upon such trecherie. W. H.
If such false Shippes do haunte the shore,
Strike doune the saile and trust no more.
