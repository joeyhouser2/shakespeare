JN youthful yeeres when first my young desyres began,
To pricke me forth to serve in Court a sclender tal young man:
My fathers blessing then I askt upon my knnee,
Who blessing me with trembling hand, these words gan say to me:
My sonne, God guide thy way, and shild thee from mischaunce,
And make thy just desartes in Court, thy poore estate to aduaunce:
Yet when thou art become one of the Courtly trayne,
Thinke on this proverbe old (qd he) that faire words make foles fain.

This counsell gravely given, most straunge appeares to me,
Till tract of time with open eyes, had made me plainely see:
What subtill fleightes are wrought by painted tales devise,
When hollow harts with freendly shewes the simple do entise.
To thinke all gold that shines to feede their fond desire,
Whose shevering cold is warmde with smoke, in stead of flaming fire:
Sith talke of tickle trust, doth breede a hope most vayne,
This proverbe true by profe I find, that faire words make fooles fain.

Faire speach alway doth wel, where deedes insue faire words,
Faire speach agayn alway doth evill, that bushes gives for birdes.
Who hopes to have faire words, to trye his lucky lot.
If I may counsell, let him strike it whyle the iron is hot.
But them that feede on cloddes, in stead of pleasant grapes,
And after warning often given, for better lucke still gapes
Ful loth I am, yet must I tel them in words playne,
This proverbe old proves true in them, that faire words makes fooles fayne.

Wo worth the time that words, so slowly turne to deedes,
Wo worth the time the faire swete flovers, are growen to rotten weedes
But thrise wo worth the time, that truth away is fled,
Wherein I see how simple harts, with words are vaynely fed.
Trust not faire words therfore, Where no deedes do ensue,
Trust words, as skilful falkeners do trust Haukes that never flewe.
Trust deedes, let words be words, which never wrought me gaine,
Let my experience make you wise, and let words make foles faine.
