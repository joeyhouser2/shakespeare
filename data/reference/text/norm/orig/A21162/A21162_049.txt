IN going to my naked bed as one that would have slept,
I hard a wife sing to her child, that long before had wept:
She sighed sore and sang ful sweete, to bring the babe to rest,
That would not cease but cryed stil, in sucking at her brest.
She was full wearie of her watch, and greeved with her child,
She rocked it and rated it, til that on her it smilde:
Then did she say now have I found, this proverbe true to prove,
The falling out of faithfull frends, renuing is of love.
Then tooke I paper, penne and ynke, this proverbe for to write,
In regester for to remaine, of such a worthy wight:
As she proceeded thus in song unto her little bratt,
Much matter uttered she of waight, in place whereas sh•e satt.
And proved plaine, there was no beast, nor creature bearing life,
Could well be knowne to live in love, without discord and strife:
Then kissed she her little babe, and sware by God above,
The falling out of faithfull frendes, renuing is of love,

She said that neither king ne prince, ne lord could live aright,
Until their puissaunce the did prove their manhod and their might.
When manhode shall be matched so, that feare can take no place,
Then weary workes make warriours, each other to embrace,
And leaved their forse that fayled them, which did consume the rout,
That might before have lived their time, and nature out:
Then did she sing as one that thought, no man could her reprove,
The falling out of faith ful freendes, renuing is of love.

She said she saw no fishe ne foule, nor beast within her haunt,
That mett a stranger in their kind, but could give it a taunt:
Since fleshe might not endure, but rest must wrath succede,
And forse the fight to fall to play, in pasture where they feede,
So noble nature can well ende, the worke she hath begone,
And bridle well that will not cease, her tragedy in some:
Thus in song she oft reherst, as dyd her wel behove,
The falling out of faithful frendes, is the renuing of love.

I meruaile much pardy quoth she, for to behold the route,
To see man, woman, boy and beast, to tosse the world about:
Some knele, some crouch, some beck, some chek, and some can smothly smile
And some embrace others in arme, and there thinke many a wile.
Some stand aloufe at cap and knee, some humble and some stoute,
Yet are they never freendes in deede, until they once fall out:
Thus ended she her song, and said before she did remove,
The falling out of faithful freends, is the renuing of love.
