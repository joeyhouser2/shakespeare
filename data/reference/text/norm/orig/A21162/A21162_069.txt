The fire shall freze, the frost shall frie the frozen mountains hie
What straunge things hath dame natures force, to turne her course awrie
My Love hath me left, and taken a newe man:
This is not straunge, it happes ofte tymes, the truthe to scan,
The more is my paine,
her Love then refraine.
Who thought she would flitt,
each one that hath witt,
Is this not straunge,
light Love will chaunge.
By skilfull meanes I here reclaime, to stoope unto my lure,
Such haggard Haukes will soare awaie, of them who can be sure
With silver bells and hoode, my joye was her to decke,
She was full gorgde, she would the soner give the checke,
The more is my paine,
her Love then refraine:
Who thought she would flitt,
each one that hath witt,
Is not this straunge,
light Love will chaunge.
Her chirpyng lips should chirpe to me, swete words of her desire
Such chirpyng birdes who ever sawe, to preach still on one brire,
She saied she loved me beste, and would not till she die,
She saied in wordes, she thought it not, as tyme doeth trie.
The more is my paine,
her Love then refraine:
Who thought she would flitt,
each one that hath witt,
Is not this straunge,
light Love will chaunge.
Can no man winne a woman so, to make her Love endure,
To make the Foxe his wiles to leave, what man will put in ure?
Why then there is no choise, but all women will chaunge,
As men do use, so some women do Love to raunge.
The more is my paine,
her Love then refraine,
Who thought she would flitt,
each one that hath witt,
Is not this straunge,
light Love will chaunge.
Sith slipper gaine falles to my lot, farewell that glidyng praie,
Sith that the dice doeth runne awrie, betymes leave of thy plaie.
I will no more lamente, the thyng I maie not have,
Then by exchaunge the losse to come, all shalt thou save.
Love will I refraine,
thereby thou shalt gaine,
With losse I will leave,
she will thee deceive:
That is not straunge,
then let her raunge.
