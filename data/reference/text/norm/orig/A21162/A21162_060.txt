The Conie in his cave, the Feret doeth annoye,
And fleyng thence his life to save, hym self he doeth destroye.
His Berrie rounde about beset, with Hunters snares,
So that when he to scape starts out, is caught therein unwares,
Like choise poore man have I, to bide and reste in Love,
Or els from thence to flie, as badde a death to prove.

I see, in Love to reste, unkindnesse doeth pursue,
To rent the harte out of his breast, which is a Lover true:
And if from Love I starte, as one that Love forsaks,
Then pensive thoughts my harte doeth pearce, and so my life it taks.
Then thus to flie or bide, hard is the choise to chuse,
Since death hath campd, and trenched each side, and saith life now refuse.

Content I am therefore, my life therein to spende,
And death I take a salve for sore, my wearie daies to ende.
And thus I you require, that faithfull Love professe,
When carcas cased is in cheste, and bodie laied on hearse.
Your brinishe teares to save, such as my corse shall move,
And therewith write upon my grave, beholde the force of Love.
