J Am a Virgin faire and free, and freely do reioyce,
I sweetely warble sugred notes, from silver voyce:
For which delightful joyes, yet thanke I curtesie love,
By whose almighty power, such sweete delightes I prove.

I walke the pleasant fieldes, adornd with lively greene,
And view the fragrant flowers, most lovely to be seene:
The purple Columbine, the Couslippe and the Lillie,
The Violet sweete, the Daizie and Daffadillie.

The Wodbines on the hedge, the red Rose and the white,
And each fine flowres els, that rendreth sweete delight:
Among the which I choose, all those of seemeliest grace,
In thought, resembling them to my deare lovers face.

His lovely face I meane, whose golden flouring giftes,
His ever living Fame, to loftye skie upliftes:
Whom loving me I love, only for vertues sake,
When vertuously to love, all only care I take.

Of all which fresh faire flowers, that flower that doth appeare,
In my conceit most like to him I hold so deare:
I gather it, I kisse it, and eake devise with it,
Such kind of lovely speach, as is for lovers fit.

And then of all my flowres, I make a garlond fine,
With which my golden wyer heares, togither I do twine:
And sette it on my head, so taking that delight,
That I would take, had I my lover stil in sight.

For as in goodly flowres, mine eyes great pleasure find,
So are my lovers giftes most pleasant to my mind:
Upon which vertuous giftes, I make more sweete repast,
Then they that for love sportes, the sweetest joyes do tast.
