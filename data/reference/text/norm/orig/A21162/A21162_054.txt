Each one deserves great prayse to have, but yet not like I thinke,
Both he that can sustaine the yoke of paines, and doth not shrinke.
And he whom Cupids covert craft can nothing move at all,
Into the hard and tangled knottes of Venus snares to fall.
Besturre you then who so delightes in vertues race to ronne,
The flying boye with bow ibent, by strength to over come.
As one dyd once when he was young, and in his tender dayes,
Whose stoute and noble deede of his, hath got immortall prayse.
The wicked Romaines did pursue the sely Christians than,
What time Valerian Emperour was a wicked cruel man.
Who spared not with bloudy draughtes, to quench his own desire,
Dispatching all that stucke to Christ with hote consuming fire.
At length a man of tender yeeres was brought before his sight,
Such one as Nature seemed to make a witnes of her migh.
For every part so well was set, that nothing was depraved,
So that the cruell king himselfe, would gladly him have saved.
So loth he was to see a worke, so rare of Natures power,
So finely built so sodainly destroyed within an howre.
Then meanes he sought to overcome, or win him at the lest,
To slip from Christ whom he before had earnestly profest.
A bedde preparde, so finely dect, such divers pleasant smels,
That well it might appeare a place, where pleasure only dwels,
By him he layd a naked wench, a Venus darling sure,
With sugred speach and lovely toyes, that might his mind allure.
Such wanton lewres as these he thought, might easly him entise,
Which thinges he knew with lustie youth, had alwayes ben in prise.
Such wayes I thinke the Gods themselves, could have inuented none,
For flattering Venus overcomes the sences every chone,
And he himselfe was even at poynt, to Venus to consent,
Had not his stout and manly mind resisted his entent.
When he perceived his flesh to yeeld to pleasures wanton toyes,
And was by flight almost provoked, to tast of Venus joyes.
More cruel to himselfe then those, that glad would him undoo,
With bloudy toth, his tender tongue, bote quite and cleane in twoo.
Thus was the paine so passyng greate of this his bloudie bite,
That all the fire and carnall luste, was quenched every whitt.
Do ill and all thy pleasures then full sone will passe awaie,
But yet the shame of those thy dedes, will nevermore decaie.
Do well and though thy paines be great, yet sone each one will cease,
But yet, the praise of those thy deedes will evermore increase.
