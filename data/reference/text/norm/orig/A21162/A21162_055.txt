Let rulers make moste perfect lawes, to rule bothe greate and smal
If they them selves obeye them not, it boteth not at all.
As lawes be nought but rulers dome, continyng egall might,
So rulers should be speakyng Lawes, to rule by line of right.
Zaleuch the Prince of Locrine once, appoincted by decree,
Each Lecherer should be punished, with losse of either eye.
His sonne by chaunce offended first, which when his father sawe,
Lorde God how earnest then was he, to execute the Lawe.
Then ranne the people all by flocks, to hym with weepyng eyes,
Not one emong the route there was, but pardon, pardon cries.
By whose outcries and earnest sute, his sonne in hope did stande,
That he thereby should then obtaine, some pardon at his hande.
But all in vaine, for he is founde to be the man he was,
And maketh haste so much the more, to have the Lawe to passe.
The people yet renued their sute, in hope of some relief,
Whose faces all besprent with teares, did testifie their grief.
And cried all for pities sake, yelde now to our request,
If all you will not cleane remit, yet ease the paine at least.
Then somewhat was the father moved, with all the peoples voyce,
And every man did give a shoote, to shewe they did reioyce.
Well then  he, it shall be thus, the Lawe shall be fulfilde,
And yet my sonne shall favour have, accordyng as you wilde.
One eye of his shall be pulde out, thus hath his leudnesse got,
And likewise so shall one of mine, though I deserve it not.
This worde no soner was pronounced, but straite the deede was doen,
Twoo eyes, no mo were lefte, betwene the father and the sonne.
Saie nowe who can, and on my faithe Apollo he shall be,
Was he more gentle father lo? or juster Judge trowe ye.
This man would not his Lawes belike, the webbs the Spiders weve,
Wherein they lurke when they intende, the simple to deceive.
Wherewith small flies full sone be caught, and tangled ere they wiste,
When greate ones flie and scape awaie, and breake them as they liste.
