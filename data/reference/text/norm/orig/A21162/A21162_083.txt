IN searche of thyngs that secret are, my mated muse began,
What it might be, molested moste the head and mynde of man.
The bendyng brow of princes face, to wrathe that doeth attende,
Or want of parents, wife or childe, or losse of faithfull frende.
The roryng of the Canon shot, that makes the peece to shake,
Or terrour such as mightie Jove, from heaven above can make.
All these in fine maie not compare experience so doeth prove,
Unto the torments sharpe and strange, of such as be in love.

Love lookes a loft and laughs to scorne all such as grefe anoye.
The more extreme their passions be, the greater is his joye.
Thus Love as victor of the felde, triumps above the rest,
And joyes to see his subiects lye, with livyng death in brest.
But dire disdaine letts drive a shaft, and gauls this braggyng foole,
He plucks his plumes, unbendes his bowe and sets hym newe to scole,
Whereby this boye, that bragged late, as conquerer over all,
Now yelds hym self unto disdaine his vessall and his thrall.
