The prettie Turtle-dove, that with no little mone,
When she hath lost her loving mate, sits mourning all alone,
The Swan that alwaies sings an houre before her death,
Whose deadly gripes do give the grones, that draw away her breath:
The Pellican that pecks the blood out of her brest,
And by her death doth only feed her yong ones in the nest,

I die because I see my death, and yet I cannot die:
The Pellican doth feed their yong ones with their blood,
I bleed to death, to feed desires that never do me good.
My heart imparked round within the ground of griefe,
Is to beset with hounds of hate, it lookes for no reliefe.
And sweet desire my dogge, is clogged so with care,
He cries and dies to heare delights, and come not where they are.
My tree of true delight, is sabd with sorrow so,
As but the heavens do sooner helpe, will be his overthrow.
In summe, my dole, my death, and my disgrace is such,
As never man that ever liu'd, knew ever halfe so much.
