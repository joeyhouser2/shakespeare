Laugh not too much, perhaps you are deceyved,
All are not fooles that have but simple faces:
Mists are abroad, things may be misconceyved,
Frumps and disdaines are favours in disgraces.
Now if you do not know what meane these speeches,
Fooles have long cotes, and Monkies have no breeches.

T•…he againe, why what a grace is this,
Laugh a man out before he can get in:
Fortune so crosse, and favour so amis,
Doomsday at hand before the world begin.
Marie sir then but if the weather holde,
Bewtie may laugh, and Love may be a colde.

Yet leave betimes your laughing tootoo mutch,
Or find the Foxe, and then begin the chase:
Shut not a Rat within a sugar hutch,
And thinke you have a Squirrell in the place.
But when you laugh let this go for a jest,
Seeke not a Woodcocke in a Swallowes nest.
