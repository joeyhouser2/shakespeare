Looke not too long upon those looks, the blinds the overlooker sore,
and if you speak, speak not to much, lest speaking once the speak no more▪
think not but what it is to think, to reach beyond the reach of thought▪
And if you do, do what you can, when you have don you can do nought.
But if you see against your will, looke but away and be not slaine,
And if a worde go unawares, with care it may be calde againe.
And for a thought it is not hurt, except it grow unto a thing,
But to undo that hath bene done, is only conquest of a king.
But since in thee O silly wretch, both sight, and speach, and thought and deed
By reason of a wrong conceit, do but thine own confusion breed.
Shut up thy eies, seale up thy tongue, lock up thy thought, lay downe thy head
And let thy mistres see by this, how love hath strock her seruant dead.
And that but in her heavenly eye, her worde, her thought, and only will
Doth rest the dead, to kill the quite, or else to cure thee of this ill,
