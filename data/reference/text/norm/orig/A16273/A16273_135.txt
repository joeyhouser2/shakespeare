Thirsis to die desired,
marking her eyes that to his hart was neerest:
And she that with his flame no lesse was fiered,
said to him: Oh hart's love deerest:
Alas, forbeare to die now,
By thee I live, by thee I wish to die too.

Thirsis that heate refrained,
wherewith to die poore lover then he hasted,
Thinking it death while he his lookes maintained,
full fixed on her eyes, full of pleasure,
and lovely Nectar sweet from them he tasted.
His daintie Nimph, that now at hand espyed
the haruest of loves treasure,
Said thus, with eyes all trembling, faint and wasted:
I die now,
The Sheepheard then replyed,
and I sweet life do die too.

Thus these two Lovers foutunately dyed,
Of death so sweet, so happy, and so desired:
That to die so againe their life retired.
