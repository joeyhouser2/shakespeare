Tell me thou gentle Sheepheards Swaine,
Who'se yonder in the Vale is set?
Oh it is she, whose sweetes do staine,
The Lilly, Rose, the Violet.
Why doth the Sunne against his kind,
Fixe his bright Chariot in the skies?
Because the Sunne is strooken blind,
With looking on her heavenly eyes.
Why do thy flocks forbeare their food,
Which sometime were thy chiefe delight?
Because they neede no other good,
That live in presence of her sight.
Why looke these flowers so pale and ill,
That once attir'd this goodly Heath?
She hath rob'd Nature of her skill,
And sweetens all things with her breath.
Why slide these brookes so slow away,
Whose bubling murmur pleas'd thine eare?
Oh meruaile not although they stay,
When they her heavenly voyce do heare.
From whence come all these Sheepheards Swaines,
And lovely Nimphs attir'd in greene?
From gathering Garlands on the Plaines,
To crowne our faire the Sheepheards Queene.
The Sunne that lights this world below.
Flocks. flowers, and brookes will witnesse beare:
These Nimphs and Sheepheards all do know,
That it is she is only faire.
