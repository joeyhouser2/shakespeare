A Satyre once did runne away for dread,
with sound of horne, which he him-selfe did blow:
Fearing, and feared thus, from him-selfe he fled,
deeming strange evili in that he did not know.

Such causelesse feares, when coward minds do take,
it makes them flie that, which they faine would have:
As this poore beast, who did his rest forsake,
thinking not why, but how him-selfe to save.

Even thus mought I, for doubts which I conceave
of mine own words, mine own good hap betray:
And thus might I, for feare of may be, leave
the sweet pursute of my desired pray.
Better like I thy Satire, dearest Dyer:
Who burnt his lips, to kisse faire shining fier.
