The cause why that thou do'st denie
To looke on me, sweete foe impart?
Because that doth not please the eye.
Which doth offend and greeve the hart.
What woman is, or ever was,
That when she looketh, could be mou'd▪
She that resolves her life to passe,
Neyther to love, nor to be lou'd.
There is no hart so fierce and hard.
That can so much torment a soule:
Nor Sheepheard of so small regard,
That reason will so much controule.
How falls it out Love doth not kill
Thy crueltie with some remorce?
Because that Love is but a will,
And free-will doth admit no force.
Behold what reason now thou hast,
To remedie my loving smart:
The very same bindes me as fast,
To keepe such daunger from my hart.
Why do'st thou thus torment my minde,
And to what end thy beautie keepe?
Because thou call'st me still unkinde,
And pittilesse when thou do'st weepe.
Is it because thy crueltie
In killing me doth never end?
Nay, for because I meane thereby,
My hart from sorrow to defend.
Be bold so foule I am no way
As thou do'st think, faire Sheepheardesse:
With this content thee, that I say,
That I beleeve the same no lesse.
What, after giving me such store▪
Of passions, do'st thou mock me too?
If aunsweres thou wilt any more.
Go seeke them without more adoo.
