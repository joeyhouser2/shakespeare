Away with these selfe-loving-Lads,
Whom Cupids arrowe never glads.
Away poore soules that sigh and weepe,
In love of them that lie and sleepe,
For Cupid is a Meadow God:
And forceth none to kisse the rod.

God Cupids shaft like destenie,
Doth eyther good or ill decree.
Desert is borne out of his bowe,
Reward upon his feete doth go.
What fooles are they that have not knowne,
That Love likes no lawes but his own?

My songs they be of Cinthias prayse,
I weare her Rings on Holly-dayes,
On every Tree I write her name,
And every day I reade the same.
Where Honor, Cupids rivall is:
There miracles are seene of his.

If Cinthia crave her ring of me,
I blot her name out of the tree.
If doubt do darken things held deere:
Then welfare nothing once a yeere.
For many run, but one must win:
Fooles only hedge the Cuckoe in.

The worth that worthines should move,
Is love, which is the due of love.
And love as well the Sheepheard can,
As can the mightie Noble man.
Sweet Nimph tis true, you worthy be,
Yet without love, nought worth to me.
