Sheepheard, why do'st thou hold thy peace?
Sing, and thy joy to us report:
My joy good Sheepheard) should be lesse,
If it were told in any sort.
Though such great favours thou do'st winne,
Yet daigne thereof to tell some part:
The hardest thing is to begin,
In enterprizes of such Art.
Come make an end, no cause omit,
Of all the joyes that thou art in:
How should I make an end of it,
That am not able to begin?
It is not just, we should consent,
That thou should'st not thy joyes recite:
The soule that felt the punishment,
Doth only feele this great delight.
That joy is small, and nothing fine,
That is not told abroade to many:
If it be such a joy as mine,
It never can never be told to any.
How can this hart of thine containe
A joy, that is of such great force?
I have it, where I did retaine
My passions of so great remorce.
So great and rare a joy is this,
No man is able to with-hold:
But greater that a pleasure is,
The lesse it may with words be told.
Yet have I heard thee heeretofore,
Thy joyes in open Songs report:
I said, I had of joy some store,
But not how much, nor in what sort.
Yet when a joy is in excesse,
It selfe it will oft-times unfold:
Nay, such a joy would be the lesse,
If but a word thereof were told.
