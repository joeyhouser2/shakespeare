Upon my corps poure forth thy wyne, O frend that comes this way,
And on my tombe with pleasant hand, thy precious spices laye.
No gulfe of griefe my grave shalbe, but springe of lasting blis,
I am not dead but changd my life, lo such my fortune is,
My former joyes are not decayd, but as they were before,
If ought or nought I beare in minde, yet blest for evermore.
Now wealth doth wield the world, and wealth doth worship gayn
Yea wealth doth wyn the frends at wyl, the pore ech where complayn
The grassy flag, the silke more soft
doth yeld the sleapes with great delight:
But stately beds in towers alofte,
the richmans rest with feares, affright.
Som hourding up great heapes of gold, not knowing how to use then,
Lyke sacred stuffe doth store up wealth: so folly doth abuse them,
But would to God he rather had, his time consumde in toyes.
Then cast such care on cruell dedes, or sought such wicked joyes,
When noble states he pluct a downe, and men of worthy fame,
His subiects love forthwith he lost, and gaynd eternall blame.
But when in bloud of simple soules, he bathde his bloudy hand,
Without renenge he felt the smart, and could no longer stand.
Such guerdon God doth geve, to men of cruel mynde
that seeke the lives of harmelesse folke.
Happy is he whome God hath provided suffycientlye wherwith to lyve.
Excedinge care of coyne, doth mortall men begile,
and love of wordly pelfe, all other joyes exile.
O safe estate of life,
The pleasant dayes which poore men passe, a blisse above the rest,
to Gods almost unknowen.
Amazed at this mischiefe newe, nowe riche and yet in wo,
His wished wealth lo now he lothes, that erst he loved so,
No store can honger stanch, drye thurst his throte tormentes,
Thus worthilye amids his gould, his former wishe repentes.
You mourning Muses reache, wherof I should endight,
And bathe▪ my face in bitter teares, wherwith my woes I wright.
