AT her faire hands how have I grace intreated,
With prayers oft repeated,
Yet still my love is thwarted:
Hart let her go, for she'le not be conuarted.
Say, shall she go?
Oh no, no, no, no, no.
She is most faire, though she be marble harted.

How often have my sighs declar'de mine anguish?
Wherein I dayly languish,
Yet doth she still procure it:
Hart let her go, for I can not endure it.
Say, shall she go?
Oh no, no, no, no, no.
She gave the wound, and she alone must cure it.

The trickling tears that down my cheeks have flowed,
My love have often showed;
Yet still unkind I prove her:
Hart, let her go, for nought I do can move her.
Say, shall she go?
Oh no, no, no no, no.
Though me she hate, I can not chuse but love her.
But shall I still a true affection owe her,
Which prayers, sighs, teares do shew her;
And shall she still disdaine me?
Hart, let her go, if they no grace can gaine me.
Say, shall she go?
Oh no, no, no, no, no.
She made me hers, and hers she will retaine me

But if the Love that hath, and still doth burne me,
No love at length returne me,
Out of my thoughts Ile set her:
Hart, let her go, oh hart, I pray thee let her.
Say, shall she go?
Oh no, no, no, no, no:
Fixt in the hart, how can the hart forget her.

But if I weepe and sigh, and often waile me,
Till teares, sighes, prayers fayle me,
Shall yet my Love persever?
Hart, let her go, if she will right thee never.
Say, shall she go?
Oh no, no, no, no▪, no
Teares, sighs, praiers faile, but true love lasteth eve•
