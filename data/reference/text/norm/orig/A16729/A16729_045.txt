Poets lay downe your pennes, let fancie leave to faine,
Bid all the Muses go to bed, or get a better vaine.
There musicks are to base, to sound that sweet consaite,
That on the wonder of the world, with wonder may awaite.
But if as yet v•knowne, there be some daintie Muse,
That can do more then all the rest, and will her cunning use▪
Let her come whet her wits, to see what she can do,
To that the best that ever wrote, came never neere untoo,
For Venus was a toy▪ and only feigned fable,
And Cresed but a Chawcers •east▪ and Helen but a bable.
My tale shalbe of trueth, that never treason taught,
My Mistresse is the onelie sweet, that ever Nature wrought.
Whose eyes are like those starres that keepe the highest skies,
Whose beautie like the burning Sunne▪ that blinds the clearest eies,
Whose haires are like those beames, that hang about the Sunne,
When in the morning forth he steps, before his course be runne
And let me touch those lips, by love, by leave, or lucke,
When sweet affect, by sweet aspect may yet some favour sucke▪
They are those little foldes, of Natures finest wit,
That she sat smoothing while she wrought▪ and wilbe smacking yet.
And for that purest red, with that most perfect white,
That makes those cheeks the sweetest chains, of lovers high delite.
What may be said but this, Behold the only feature,
That all the world that sees the face, may wonder at the creature
I will not stand to muse as many writers do.

Foe if thou wert on earth that could in part compare:
With everie part of everie part, wherin her praises are:
Either for Natures gifts or Vertues sweetest grace:
I would confesse a blinded heart, were in unhappie case.
But what doth Nature▪ Sence, and Reason doth approve,
She is the only saint on earth, whom God and man doth lou• ▪
Let this in summe suffice for my poore Muse and me,
She is the Goddesse of the earth, and there is none but she.
