I Judge and finde, how God doth minde,
to furnish, to furnish
his heavenly throne above.
With virgins pure, this am I sure,
without misse, without misse:
with other Saints he doth love:
It is allowed as you may reade,
And eke avowed by Paul indeede,
Virginitie is accepted,
a thing high in Gods sight:
Though marriage is selected,
a thing to be most right:
yet must I praise Virginitie,
For I would faine a Uirgin be.

¶ You Uirgins pure, your selves assure,
and credite, and credite:
great joy you shall possesse,
Which I (God knows) cannot disclose,
nor spreade it, nor spreade it,
ne yet by pen expresse.
Nor halfe the joies that you shall finde,
I can not judge for you assignde:
When hence your ghost shall yeelded be,
into the throne of blisse:
In chaste and pure Uirginitie,
for thought or deed y wisse:
Where you shall raign, with God on hie
For evermore eternally.

¶ And when doubtlesse, you shall possesse,
with Jesus, with Jesus,
these joies celestiall.
Then Ladie Fame, will blaze your name,
amongst us, amongst us,
which then on earth raigne shall.
She will resound in everie coast,
By trumpet sound, and will you boast?
So that although you do depart
This mortall life so vaine:
Your chastitie in everie heart,
by memorie shall remaine.
But hard it is, I saie no more,
To finde an hundreth in a score.
