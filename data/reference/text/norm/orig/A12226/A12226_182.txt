Looke in my griefes, and blame me not to mourne,
From thought to thought that lead a life so bad:
Fortunes Orphan, hers and the worlds scorne,
Whose clowded brow doth make my daies so bad.

Long are their nights, whose cares do never sleepe;
Lothsome their dayes, whom never sunne yet joyed;
A pleasing griefe impressed hath so deepe,
That thus I live both day and night annoyed.

Yet since the sweetest roote doth yeeld thus much,
Her praise from my complaint I must not part:
I love the effect, because the cause is such;
I praise her face, and blame her flintie hart:
Whilst that we make the world admire at us,
Her for disdaine, and me for loving thus.
