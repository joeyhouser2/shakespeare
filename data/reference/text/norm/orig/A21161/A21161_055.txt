IF that each flower, the godds have framed, are shapt by sacred skill
Were as I would (no wrong to wishe) and mine to weare at will.
Or els each tree, with lustie top, would lende me leave to love,
With spriggs displaied to spread my sute, a wailing hart to prove
Upon my helme sone should you see, my hedde aduaunced hie,
Some slipp for solace there to sett, and weare the same would I.
Yet would I not for greate delight, the Daises strange desire,
The Lillie would not like my lust nor Rose would I require.
The Marigould might growe for me, Rosemary well might reste,
The Fenell to that is more fit, for some unfrendly gest.
Nor Cowslopps would I crave at all, sometymes they seme to coy
Some joly youth the Gelliflower, estemeth for his joye.
The Lavender sometymes aloft, alures the lookers eyes,
The Paunsie shall not have the praise, where I may geve the prise
And thus no flower my fansie feeds, as liketh so my luste,
As that I maie subiect my self, to toyes of tickle truste.
For flowers though they be faire and fresh, of sent excelling swete
Yet growe they on the ground belowe, we tred them with our fete
And shall I then go stoupe to such? or els go seke to those,
Shall flowers enforse me once to faune, for feare of frends or foes.
Yet rather yelde I to the right, as reason hath assignde,
Mine authour saied there was no salve, in flowers for me to finde.
And yet perhapps some tree there is, to shroud me fron the shower,
That with her armes maie salve the soule, that yeldeth to her power.
Where I maie finde some pleasant shade, to salve me fron the sonne
Each thyng w• see that reason hath, unto the trees do runne.
Bothe men and beasts such soules as fly, the treasures are the trees
And for my part when braunches fall, I wishe no other fees.
But when that stormes beset me round, such succor God me sende,
That I maie finde a frendly tree, that will me well defende.
No tree there is which yelds no good, to some that do it seke
And as they are of divers kynds, their uses are unlike.
The Eve tree serves the Bowiers turne, the Ash the Coupers art,
The puisant Oke doeth make the post, the Pine some other part.
The Elme doeth helpe to hide the birds, in wearie winters night,
The Briers I gesse are nothyng worth, they serve but for despight
The willowe wisht I farre fron hens, good will deserve no wrong,
The Sallowe well maie serve their states, that syng so sad a song.
The Boxe and Beche each for hymself, above the reste doeth boste,
The Eglantine for pleasure oft, is pricked upon the poste.
The Hauthorne so is had in prise, the Baies do beare the bell,
And that these Baies did bryng no blisse, I like it not so well.
As erst I do that semely tree, by which those Baies I founde,
And where withall unwittyngly, I tooke so greate a wounde.
As if the tree by which I lent, doeth lende me no relief,
There is no helpe but doune I fall, so greate is growne my grief.
And therefore at the last I crave, this favour for to finde,
When every tree that here is told, beginns to growe unkinde.
The B. for beautie whom I boste, and shall above the rest,
That B. maie take me to her trust, for B. doeth please me best.
It liks me well to walke the waie, where B. doeth kepe her bower
And when it raines to B. I ronne to save me from the shower.
This braunche of B. which here I meane, to kepe I chiefly crave,
At becke unto this B. I bowe, to sarue that beautie brave.
What shall I saie the tyme doeth passe, the tale to tedious is,
Though loth to leave, yet leave I must, and saie no more but this,
I wishe this B. I might embrace, when as the same I see,
A league for life then I require, betwene this B. and me.
And though unworthy, yet good will, doeth worke the waie herein
And B. hath brought the same about, which beautie did begin.
