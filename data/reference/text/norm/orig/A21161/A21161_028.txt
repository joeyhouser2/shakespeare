IN youth when I at large did leade, my life in lustie libertie,
When hevy thoughtes no one did spreade, to let my pleasant fantesie
No fortune seemd, so hard could fall,
This freedome then, that might make thrall.
And twentie yeres I skarse had spent, when to make ful my happy fate,
Both treasures great were on me cast, with landes and titles of estate:
So as more blest then I stoode than,
Eke as me thought was never man.
For of Dame Fortune who is he, coulde more desyre by just request,
The health, with wealth, and libertie, all which at once I thus possest:
But maskyng in this joly joy,
A soden syght, prooud all a toy.
For passyng on these merie dayes, with new device of pleasures great,
And now and then to viewe the rayes, of beauties workes with cunnyng fret:
In heavenly hewes, all which as one,
I oft behelde, but bounde to none.
And one day rowlyng thus my eyes, upon these blessed wyghts at ease,
Among the rest one dyd I see, who strayght my wandryng lookes dyd sease:
And stayed them firme, but such a syght,
Of beautie yet sawe never wyght.
What shall I seke to praise it more, where tongs can not praise the same,
But to be short to lovers lore, I strayght my senses all dyd frame:
And were it wyt, or were it chaunce,
I woonne the Garlande in this daunce,
And thus where I before had thought, no hap my fortune might encrese▪
A double blis this chance forth brought, so did my ladies love me plese:
Her fayth so firme, and constant such,
As neve• hart, can prayse too much.
But now with torments strange I tast, the fickle stay of fortunes whele,
And where she raysde from height to cast, with greater force, of greefe to feele:
For from this hap of soden frowne,
Of Princes face she threwe me downe.
And thus exchange now hath it made, my libertie a thing most deare,
In hateful prison for to fade, where sundred from my loving feare:
My wealth, and health, standes at like stay,
Obscurely to consume away.
And last when humaine force was none, could part our love wherin we lived,
My ladyes life alas is gone, most cruel death hath it bereved:
Whose vertues, her, to God, hath wonne,
And least me here, a man undone.
