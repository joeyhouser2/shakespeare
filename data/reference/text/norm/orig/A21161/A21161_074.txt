What death maie be, compared to love?
What grief therein, now doest thou prove?
My paines alas who can expresse,
I see no cause of heavinesse.
My Ladies looks, my wo hath wrought:
Then blame thine eyes that first have sought,
I burne alas, and blowe the fire,
A foole consumes by his desire,
What shall I do than?
come out and thou can.
Alas I die,
what remedie?
My sugred sweete, is mixed with gall,
Thy Ladie can not do with all:
The more I seeke, the lesse I finde,
Then strive not with the streame and winde.
Her must I love, although I smarte,
With thy own sworde, thou slaiest thy harte:
Such pleasaunt baites, who can refraine,
Such beats will sure brede the greate paine.
What shall I do than?
Come out and thou can.
Alas I die,
what remedie.
Her golden beames, mine eyes do daze,
Upon the Sonne thou maiest not gaze:
She might reward my cruell smarte,
She thinks thou bearest a fained harte.
She laughs to heare my wofull cries,
Forsake her then, in tyme be wise:
No no alas, that maie not be,
No wise man then will pitie thee,
What shall I do than?
come out and thou can.
Alas I die,
what remedie.
A livyng death, loe thus I prove,
Such are the fruts of froward love:
O that I might her love once againe,
Thy gaine would not, halfe quite the paine.
Her will I love though she be coye,
A foole hym self will still anoye:
Who will not die for such a one,
Be wise at length, let her alone:
I can not do so,
then be thy own foe,
Alas I die,
what remedie.
