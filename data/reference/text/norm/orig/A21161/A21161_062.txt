The mountaines hie whose loftie topps, doeth mete the hautie sky
The craggie rocke that to the sea, free passage doeth deny,
The aged Oke that doeth resist, the force of blustryng blast,
The pleasaunt herbe that every where, a fragrant smell doeth cast.
The Lyons forse whose courage stout, declares a princlike might,
The Eagle that for worthinesse, is borne of kyngs in fight:
The Serpent eke whose poisoned waies, doeth belche out venim vile,
The lothsome Tode that shunneth light, and liveth in exile.
These these I saie and thousands more, by trackt of tyme decaie,
And like to tyme do quite consume, and vade from forme to claie:
But my true harte and seruice vowed, shall last tyme out of minde,
And still remaine as thine by dome, as Cupid hath assignde.
My faithe loe here I vowe to thee, my trothe thou knowest right well,
My goods my frends, my life is thine, what nede I more to tell?
I am not mine but thine I vowe, thy hests I will obeye,
And serve thee as a seruaunt ought, in pleasyng if I maie:
And sith I have no fliyng wings, to see thee as I wishe,
Ne finnes to cut the silver streames, as doeth the glidyng fishe.
Wherefore leave now forgetfulnesse, and sende againe to me,
And straine thy azured vaines to write, that I maie greetyng see:
And thus farewell more deare to me, then chiefest frende I have,
Whose love in harte I minde to shrine, till death his fee do crave.
