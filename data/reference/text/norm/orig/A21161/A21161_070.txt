V Uhat watche, what we, what want, what wracke?
Is due to those that toyle the Seas:
Life ledd with losse, of paines no lacke,
In stormes to winne, much restlesse case.
A bedlesse horde, in seas unrest,
Maie happ to hym, that chaunseth best.

How sundrie sounds with lead and line,
Unto the depe, the shipman throwes:
No foote to spare, he cries oft tymes,
No nere, when how the master blowes.
If Neptune frown, all be undoen,
Strait waie the shipp, the wrack hath won

These daungers greate do oft befall,
On those that there upon the sande:
Judge of their lives, the best who shall,
How vile it is, fewe understande.
Alacke? who then maie judge, their game:
Not they, which have not felt the same.

But they that fall in stormes and winde,
And daies and yeres have spent therein:
Such well may judge, since profe they find
In rage, no rest, till calme begin.
No more then those, that love do faine,
Give judgement of true lovers paine.
