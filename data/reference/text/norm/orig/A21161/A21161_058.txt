When sage Ulisses sailed by,
The perillous seas, where Cirens syng:
Hym self unto the mast did tye,
Lest their alluryng tunes might bryng,
His mynde on maze and make hym staie,
And he with his become their praie.

Ulisses O thou valiant wight,
It semed dame Circes loved thee well:
What tyme she told, to thee aright.
The seas wherein the Sirens dwell.
By meane where, against thy faile,
Their subtill songes, could not prevaile.

Were thou amongs us here againe,
And heard our Sirens melodie:
Not Circes skill nor yet thy braine,
Could kepe thee from their trecherie.
Such Sirens have we now adaies,
That tempt us by a thousande waies.

They syng they daunce, they sport, they plaie
They humbly fall upon their knees:
They sigh, they sobb, they prate, they praie,
With such dissemblyng shifts as these,
They calculate, they chaunt, they charme,
To conquere us that meane no harme.

Good ladies all letts joyne in one,
And banishe cleane this Siren kinde:
What nede we yelde, to heare their mone,
Since their deceipt we daiely finde.
Let not your harts to them apply,
Defie them all for so will I.

And if where Circes now doeth dwell,
You wisht you witt aduise, to learne:
Loe I am she that best can tell,
Their Sirens songes and them discerne.
For why experience yeldeth skill,
To me that scapt that Sirens ill.
