IN choise of frends what happy had I, to chuse one of Cirenes kinde,
Whose harpe, whose pipe, whose melodie, could feede my eares and make me blinde:
Whose pleasant noise made me forget, that in sure trust was great deceit.

In trust I see is treason founde, and man to man deceitfull is,
And whereas Treasure doeth abounde, of flatterers there do not misse:
Whose painted speache, and outward showe, do seme as frends and be not so.

Would I have thought in thee to be, the nature of the Crokadill,
Which if a man a slepe maie see, with bloudy thirst desires to kill:
And then with teares a while gan wepe, the death of hym thus slaine a slepe.

O flatterer false, thou traitor borne, what mischief more might thou devise,
Then thy deare frende, to have in scorne, and hym to wounde in sondrie wise:
Which still a frende pretends to be, and art not so by profe I se.
Fie fie, upon such trechery.
