Policrates whose passing happe, causd him to lose his fate,
A golden ryng cast in the seas, to change his constant state,
And in a fishe yet at his bourd, the fame he after found,
Thus Fortune loe, to whom she takes, for bountie doth abound.

The myzers unto might she mountes, a common case we see,
And mightie in great miserie, she sets in lowe degree:
Whom she to day doth reare on hie, upon her whitling wheele,
To morowe next shedingeth downe, and casteth at her heele.

No measure hath she in her gifts, she doth reward eache sort.
The wise that counsell have, no more then fooles that maketh sport.
She useth never partiall handes for to offend or please,
Geve me good Fortune all men sayes, and throw me in the seas.

It is no fault or worthines, that makes men fall or rise,
I rather be borne Fortunate, then to be very wise.
The blindest man right soone, that by good Fortune guided is,
To whome that pleasant Fortune pipes, can never daunce amis.
