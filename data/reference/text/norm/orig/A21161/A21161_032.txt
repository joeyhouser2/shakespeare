Who so doth marke the carelesse life, of these unhappie dayes,
And sees what small and slender hold, the state of vertue stayes:
He findes that this accursed trade, proceedeth of this ill,
That men be given, too much to yeelde to their untamed will.

In lacke of taming witlesse will, the poore we often see
Enuies the ritch, because that he, his equall cannot be:
The rich aduauned to might by wealth, from wrong doth not refraine,
But will oppresseth weaker sort, to heape excessive gaine.

If Fortune were so blinde, to geve to one man what he will,
A world would not suffise the same, if he might have his fill:
We wish, we searche, we strive for all, and have no more therin
Then hath the slave, when death doth come, though Cresus welth we win.

In getting much, we get but care, such brittle wealth to keepe,
The rich within his walles of stone doth never soundly sleepe:
When poore in weake and slender house, do feare no losse of wealth,
And have no further care but this, to keepe them selves in health.

Affection may not hide the sword of sway, in judgement seat,
Least partiall law do execute the lawe in causes great:
But if the minde in constant state, affection quite do leave,
The higher state shall have their rights, the poore no wrong receave.

It is accompted greater praise to Ceasars loftie state,
Against his vanquist foes, in warres to bridle wrekefull hate:
Then when to Rome he had subdued, the people long unknowne,
Wherby as farre as land was found, the same abrode was •lowne.

If honor can selfe will refuse, and justice be upright,
And private state desires but that which good appeares in sight:
Then vertue shall with soveraigne show, to every eye reveale
A heavenly life, a wealefull state, a happie common weale.

Let vertue then the Triumph win, and governe all your deedes,
