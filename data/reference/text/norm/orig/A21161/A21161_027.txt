Forlorne in filthy froward fate, wherein a thousand cares I finde,
By whom I do lament my state, annoide with fond afflicted mind:
A wretche in woe, and dare not crie,
I live, and yet I wishe to dye.
The day in dole, that seemeth long, I pas with sighes and heavy cheere,
And with these eyes I vewe the wrong, that I sustaine by loving here:
Where my mishappes as rife do dwell,
As plagues within the pit of hell.
A wailing wight I walke alone, in desart dennes there to complaine,
Among the savage sort to mone, I flee my frends where they remaine:
And pleasure take to shun the sight,
Where erst I felt my cheefe delight.
A captive clapt in chaynes of care, lapt in the lawes of lethall love,
My fleshe and bones consumed bare, with crauling greefes ful strange to prove:
Though hap doth bidde me hope at least,
Whiles grasse doth growe, yet starves the beast.
A seeged fort with forraine force, for want of ayde, must yeelde at last,
So must my weeried pined corse, submit it selfe to bitter tast:
Of crauling care, that carkes my brest,
Till hop or death, shall breede my rest.
