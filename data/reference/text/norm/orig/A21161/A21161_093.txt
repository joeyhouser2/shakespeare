Enforst by love and feare, to please and not offende,
Within the words you would me write, a message must I sende:
A wofull errande sure a wretched man must write,
A wretched tale, a wofull head, besemeth to endite.

For what can he but waile, that hath but all he would.
And yet that all, is nought at all, but lacke of all he should:
But lacke of all his minde: what can be greater greif,
That have and lacke that likes him best, must neds be most mischief.

Now foole what maks thee waile, yet some might saie full well
That hast no harme but of thy self, as thou thy self canst tell:
To whom I aunswere thus, since all my harmes do growe,
Upon my self, so of my self, some happ maie come I trowe.

And since I see bothe happ, and harme betids to me,
For present woe, my after blisse, will make me not forget thee:
Who hath a field of golde, and maie not come therein,
Must live in hope till he have forse, his treasure well to winne.

Whose joyes by hope of dreade, to conquere or to lose,
So greate a wealth doeth rise, and for example doeth disclose:
To winne the golden flese, stoode Jason not in drede,
Till that Medeas hope of helpe, did give hym hope to spede.

Yet sure his minde was much, and yet his feare the more,
That hath no happ but by your helpe, maie happ for to restore:
The ragyng Bulls he dread, yet by his Ladies charme,
He knewe it might be brought to passe, they could do little harme.

Unto whose grace yelde he, as I do offer me,
Into your hands to have his happ, not like hym for to be:
But as kyng Priamus did binde hym to the will.
Of Cressed false which hym forsooke, with Diomede to spill.

So I to you commende my faithe, and eke my joye,
I hope you will not be so false, as Cressed was to Troye:
For if I be untrue, her Lazares death I wishe,
And eke to thee if I be false, her clapper and her dishe.
