IN youthfull yeeres when fyrst my young desyres began,
To pricke me foorth to serve in Court a sclender tall young man.
My Fathers blessing then I askt upon my knee,
Who blessing me with trembling hand, these woordes gan say to me.
My sonne, God guide thy way, and shielde thee from mischaunce,
And make thy just desartes in Court, thy poore estate to aduaunce.
Yet when thou art become one of the Courtly trayne,
Thinke on this proverbe olde (qd he) that faire woordes make fooles faine.

This counsell gravely geven, most strange appeares to me.
Till tract of time, with open eyes, had made me plainely see.
What subtill sleightes are wrought, by painted tales devise,
When hollowe hartes with freendly shoes the simple do entise.
To thinke all golde that shines to feede their fonde desire,
Whose shivering cold is warmd with smoke, in stead of flaming fire.
Sith talke of tickle trust, doth breede a hope most vaine,
This proverbe true by proofe I finde, that faire woordes make fooles faine.

Faire speache alway doeth well, where deedes insue faire woordes,
Faire speache againe, alway doth evil, that busshes geves for birdes.
Who hopes to have fayre woordes, to trye his luckie lot,
If I may counsel let him strike it, whyle the iron is hotte.
But them that feede on cloddes, in steade of pleasaunt grapes,
And after warning often geven, for better lucke still gapes.
Full loth I am, yet must I tell them in woordes plaine,
This proverbe old proves true in them, that faire words makes fooles faine.

Wo woorth the time that woordes, so slowly turne to deedes,
Wo worth the time, that faire sweete floures, are growen to rotten weedes.
But thrise wo woorth the time, that trueth, away is fled,
Wherein I see how simple hartes, with woordes are vainely fed.
Trust no faire woordes therefore, where no deedes do ensue,
Trust words, as skilful Falkeners do trust Haukes that never flew.
Trust deedes, let wodrdes be woordes, which never wrough me gaine,
Let my experience make you wyse, and let woordes make fooles faine.
