IN every wight some sondrie sort of pleasure I do finde,
Which after he doth seeke to ease his toyling minde.
Diana with her training chase, of hunting had delight,
Against the fearefull Deare, she could direct her shotte aright.
The loftie yeares in every age, doth still imbrace the •ame,
The sport is good, if vertue do assist the chearefull game.

Minerua in her chattering armes her courage doth aduaunce,
In triall of the bloudie warres, she giveth luckie chaunce.
For savegard men imbrace the same, which do so needefull seeme,
That noble heartes their cheefe delights in use therof esteeme.
In warlike games to ride or trie the force of armes they use,
And base the man we do accompt, that doth the same refuse.

The silver sound of musickes cordes, doth please Apollos wit,
A science which the heavens aduaunce, where it deserves to sit.
A pleasure apt for every wight, releefe to carefull minde,
For woe redresse, for care a salve, for sadnesse helpe we finde.
The soveraigne praise of Musicke still, doth cause the Poetes faine,
That whliring Spheres, and eke the heavens, do hecmonie retaine.

I heard that these three powers, at variaunce lately fell,
Whiles eache did praise his own delight, the other to excell.
Then Fame, as one indifferent judge, to ende the case they call,
The praise pronounced by her to them, indifferently doth fall.
Diana health and strength maintaine, Minerua force doth tame,
And Musicke geves sweete delight, to further other game.

These three delightes to hawtie mindes, the worthiest are estemed,
If vertue be anexed to them, they rightly be so demed.
With joy they do releeve the witte with sorrow oft opprest,
And never suffer solempne greefe too long in minde to rest.
Be wise in mirth, and seeke delight, the same do not abuse,
In honest mirth, a happie joy we ought not to refuse.
