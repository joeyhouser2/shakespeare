IF ever man had love to dearly bought,
Lo I am he that plaies within her maze:
And finds no waie, to get the same I sought,
But as the Dere are driven unto the gaze.
And to augment the grief of my desire,
My self to burne, I blowe the fire:
But shall I come ny you,
Of forse I must flie you.

What death alas, maie be compared to this,
I plaie within the maze of my swete foe:
And when I would of her but crave a kis,
Disdaine enforceth her awaie to go.
My self I check: yet do I twiste the twine,
The pleasure hers, the paine is mine,
But shall I come ny you,
Of forse I must flie you.

You courtly wights, that wants your pleasant choise,
Lende me a floud of teares, to waile my chaunce:
Happie are they in love, that can reioyse,
To their greate paines, where fortune doeth aduaunce
But sith my sute alas, can not prevaile,
Full fraight with care, in grief still will I waile:
Sith you will needs flie me,
I maie not come ny you.
