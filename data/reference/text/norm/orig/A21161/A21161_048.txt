Where sethyng sighes and sower sobbs,
Hath slaine the slipps that nature sett:
And skaldyng showers with stonie throbbs,
The kindly sappe from them hath fett.
What wonder then though you do see,
Upon my head white heeres to be.

Where thought hath thrild and throne his speares,
To hurt the harte that harmth hym not:
And gronyng grief hath grounde for the teares,
Mine eyne, to staine my face to spot.
What wonder then though you do see,
Upon my head white heeres to be.

Where pinchyng paine hym self hath plaste,
There peace with pleasures were possest:
And walles of wealth are fallen to waste,
And povertie in them is prest.
What wonder then, though you do see,
Upon my head white heeres to be.

Where wretched woe doeth weave her webbe,
There care the clewe, can catche and caste:
And floudds of joye are fallen to ebbe
So loe, that life maie not long laste.
What wonder then, though you do see,
Upon my head white heeres to be.

These heeres of age are messengers,
Which bidd me, fast repent and praie:
They be of death the harbingers,
That doeth prepare, and dresse the waie.
Wherefore I joye, that you maie see,
Upon my head such heeres to be.

They be the line that lead the length,
How farre my race was for to ronne:
They saie my yongth is fledde with strength,
And how old age, is well begonne.
The which I feele, and you maie see,
Upon my head such lines to be.

They be the stryngs of sober sounde,
Whose Musicke is hermonicall:
Their tunes declare, a tyme from grounde
I came, and how thereto I shall.
Wherefore I joye that you maie see,
Upon my head such stryngs to be.

God graunt to those that white heeres have,
No worse them take, then I have ment:
That after they be laied in grave,
Their soules maie joye their lives well spent,
God graunt likewise that you maie see,
Upon my head such heeres to be.
