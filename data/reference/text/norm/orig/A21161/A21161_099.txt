I Rage in restlesse ruthe, and ruins rule my daies,
I rue (to late) my rechlesse youthe, by rules of reasons waies:
I ran so long a race, in searche of surest waie,
That leasure learnde me tread the trace, that led to leud decaie.
I gave so large a raine, to unrestrained bitt,
That now with proofe of after paine, I waile my want of witt:
I trifeled forthe the tyme, with trust to self conceiptes,
Whilst plenties use prickt forth my prime, to search for sugred baites.
Wherein once learnde to finde, I founde so sweete a taste,
That dewe foresight of after speede, self will estemed waste.
Which will, through wilfulnesse, hath wrought my witlesse fall,
And heedelesse youthes unskilfulnesse, hath lapt my life in thrall.
Whereby by proofe I knowe, that pleasure breedeth paine,
And he that evill seede doeth sowe evill frute must reape againe.
Let such therefore, whose youth, and pursses are in Prime,
Foresee and shun, the helplesse ruthe, which fews misspence of time.
For want is nexte to waste, and shame doeth synne ensue,
Evil speding proofe hath hedeles hast, my self hath proved it true.
When neighbours next house burnes, tis tyme thereof take hede,
For fortunes whele hath choise of turnes, which change of chanses breds.
My saile hath been aloft, though now I beare but lowe,
Who clims to high selde falleth soft, dedst ebbe hath highest flowe.
