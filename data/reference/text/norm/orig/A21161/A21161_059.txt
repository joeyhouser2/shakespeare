The Cony in his cave, the Feret doeth anoye,
And fleyng thence his life to save, him self he doeth destroye.
His Berrie rounde about besett, with hunters snares,
So that when he to scape starts out, is caught therein unwares,
Like choise poore man have I to bide and rest in love,
Or els from thence to start, and still as bad a death to prove.

I see, in love to rest, unkindnesse doeth pursue,
To rent the harte out of his breast, which is a lover true.
And if from love I starte, as one that love forsaks,
Then pensive thoughts my harte doeth perse, and so my life it taks.
Thus then to fly or bide, harde is the choise to chuse,
Since death hath campde, and treanhed each side, and saith life now refuse

Content I am therefore, my life therein to spende,
And death I take a salve for sore, my wearie daies to ende.
And thus I you request, that faithfull love professe,
When carcas cased is in chest, and bodie laied on hears.
Your brinishe teares to save, such as my corse shall move,
And therewith write upon my grave, behold the force of love.
