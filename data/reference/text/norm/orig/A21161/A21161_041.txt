I Am a Virgine faire and free, and freely do reioyce,
I sweetely warble sugred notes, from silver voyce:
For which delightfull joyes, yet thanke I curtesie love,
By whose allmightie power, such sweete delites I prove,

I walke the pleasant fieldes, adornd with lively greene,
And view the fragrant flowres, most lovely to be seene:
The purple Columbine, the Cousloppe and the Lillie,
The Violet sweete, the Daizie and Daffadillie.

The Woodbines on the hedge, the red Rose and the white,
And cache fine flowres else, that rendreth sweete delite:
Among the which I choose, all those of seemeliest grace,
In thought, resembling them to my deare lovers face.

His lovely face I meane, whose golden flouring giftes,
His ever living Fame, to loftie skie upliftes:
Whom loving me I love, only for vertues sake,
When vertuously to love, all only care I take.

Of all which freshe faire flowers, that flowre that doth appeare
In my conceit most like to him I holde so deare.
I gather it, I kisse it, and eake devise with it,
Such kinde of lively speeche, as is for lovers fit.

And then of all my flowres, I make a garland fine,
With which my golden wyer heares, together I do twine:
And sette it on my head, so taking that delight,
That I would take, had I my lover still in sight.

For as in goodly flowres, mine eyes great pleasure finde,
So are my lovers gyftss, most pleasant to my minde:
Upon which vertuous gyftes, I make more sweete repast,
Then they that for love sportes, the sweetest joyes do tast.
