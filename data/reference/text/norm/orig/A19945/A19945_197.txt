But if (these reasons being vainely spent)
You sight it out to the last argument,
Tell me but how one body can enclose,
As loving friends two deadly hating foes?
But when as contraries are mixt together,
The colour made, doth differ much from either.
Whilst mutually at strife they do impeach
The grosse and lustre proper unto each.
So, where one bodie joyntly doth inuest
An Angels face, and cruell Tigers brest,
There dieth both alleageance and command,
For selfe-devided kingdomes cannot stand.
But as a child that knowes not what is what,
Now craveth this, and now affecteth that,
And having weighs not that which he requires.
But is unpleasde, even in his pleasde desires.
Chaste Beautie so, both will, and will not have
The selfe-same thing it childishly doth crave:
And wanton-like, now love, now hate affecteth.
