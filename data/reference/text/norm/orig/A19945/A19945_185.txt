As she is faire, so faithfull I,
My seruice she, her grace I merit,
Her beauty doth my love inherit,
But Grace she doth denie.
O knowes she not how much I love?
Or doth knowledge in her move
No small remorce?
For the guilt thereof must lie
Upon one of these of force,
Her ignorance, or cruelty.

As she is faire, so cruell she,
I sowe true love, but reape disdaining,
Her pleasure springeth from my paining,
Which Pitties source should be.
Too well she knowes how much I love,
Yet doth knowledge in her move,
No small remorce.
Then the guilt thereof must lie
Her undeserved cruelty.

As she is faire, so were she kinde:
Or being cruell, could I waver,
Soone should I, either win her faver,

But neither our alas may be,
Scorne in her, and lovein me,
So fixed are.
Yet in whom most blame doth lie
Judge she may, if she compare
My love unto her crueltie.
